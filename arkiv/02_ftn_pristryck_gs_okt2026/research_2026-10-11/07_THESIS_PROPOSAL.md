## The decision in one paragraph

Write about the forced flow. When Fondtorgsnämnden removes a fund, Pensionsmyndigheten sells 20 to 44 per cent of that fund on one pre-announced day and buys the winners with the proceeds; 94 per cent of the money moves because nobody answered a letter, and the appeal round shows that the letter alone moves nothing. That is a dated, uninformed, very large demand shock, repeated across rounds, with a 2019 precedent, in a market where the tutor's own fields (index investing, demand curves, microstructure) supply the tools. The anchor is Greenwood and Sammon (2025), "The Disappearing Index Effect", Journal of Finance 80(2): 657-698. They show that the price reaction to the best-known pre-announced uninformed flow, S&P 500 index changes, has fallen from about 7 per cent to about zero because the market learned to supply liquidity to a flow it can see coming. The replication leg redoes their event study on OMXS30 reconstitutions 2005 to 2026. The extension asks whether the effect reappears when the state introduces a new pre-announced flow that no arbitrage community has learned yet: do prices move on the award, on the letter, on the execution day; do they revert; and who bears the cost, the savers being moved (if the fund swings its NAV) or the investors who stay (if it does not). The conclusion puts the transition cost next to the fee savings of 178 MSEK a year.

Working titles. Swedish: "Vad kostar det att flytta premiepensionen? Prispress och fördelning när staten byter fonder." English: "Pre-announced, uninformed, and large: the price of moving the premium pension."

## Why this and not the other twelve questions

File 01 scores fourteen questions. Three things decide it.

1. The unique asset of the institution is the forced flow (step 4 in file 01), not the tender (step 2) or the fee (step 5). Selection and fee questions either have no replication leg (Cookson et al., Goyal-Wahal-Yavuz: file 04) or turn out to be mechanical once the data are checked (only 4 of 49 winners created a PPM class; the fee cut is a contractual rebate: file 06, fact 3). The flow is what nobody else has.
2. The winner pattern (file 02) is unambiguous: since 2016 every prize winner anchors on one recent top-journal paper, replicates its main table, and uses a Swedish institutional feature as the extension, mostly in asset pricing or microstructure (16 of 25). Two winners are this exact design: 2015 (fund-flow risk, Coval-Stafford and Lou) and 2020 (predictable LETF rebalancing flows). The household questions (defaults, gender, menus) produce sharper facts but fit 1 of 25 winners and none of the tutor's fields.
3. Power. Of the questions in the flow family, the stock-level price test is the one where an 8-week BSc thesis can say something with numbers: the sell side is dated and mechanical, the pressure measure does not use prices (immune to Wardlaw 2020), and the Swedish small-cap round, the highest-dose event, executes in October 2026, during the thesis.

## The question, in three parts

Main. When the state moves premium-pension capital on pre-announced dates, do the affected stocks move, and do they move back?

Part A (anticipation). Three public dates per round: award (FTN press release), letter (about two weeks before exit), execution (one day per fund). Is there abnormal return or volume on the sell-side names between award and execution, which would mean the market pre-positions, as it does for index changes?

Part B (execution and reversal). On the execution day and the following week, is there price pressure proportional to the dose, and does it revert within 20 to 40 trading days?

Part C (incidence). On the execution day, does the losing fund's NAV fall against its benchmark (the stayers pay) or not (the fund swung its NAV, so the moved savers paid)?

Conclusion. Transition cost (A + B borne by moved savers, C borne by stayers) against 178 MSEK a year in fee savings: how many years of savings does the move cost?

## The anchor and what is replicated

Greenwood and Sammon (2025) JF. Main results: cumulative abnormal return from announcement to effective day for S&P 500 additions fell from about 7 per cent (1990s) to about 1 per cent (2010s), deletions likewise; the effect's disappearance is traced to the flow becoming anticipated and absorbed (pre-positioning, index migrations, higher liquidity) rather than to a smaller flow. Their core exhibits are an event-time CAR figure by decade and a table of announcement-to-effective and post-effective CARs with the reversal.

Replication on Swedish data (Table 2 and Figure 1 of the thesis). OMXS30 is reviewed twice a year (effective first trading day of January and July), announced by Nasdaq Stockholm about two weeks earlier. 2005 to 2026 gives about 40 reconstitutions and, by rough count, 40 to 60 additions and deletions. For each: CAR from announcement day to effective day, CAR over the following 20 and 40 days, abnormal volume; split by sub-period (2005-2014 versus 2015-2026) and related to the capital tracking OMXS30 (XACT OMXS30, Avanza Zero, Nordnet, and the PPM index funds themselves, from the panel). This leg is short, clean, and in the tutor's field; it also calibrates what a "learned" pre-announced flow does in this market, which is the benchmark the extension needs.

Why this anchor rather than Lou (2012) or Da et al. (2018). It is the most recent top-journal paper on pre-announced uninformed demand; its replication is an event study, which is tractable in two weeks; its logic is exactly the thesis's logic (a flow the market can see coming versus one it cannot yet); and it is index investing and demand curves, the tutor's listed fields. Lou (2012) and Wardlaw (2020) are cited for the pressure measure, Coval and Stafford (2007) for the fire-sale framing, Da, Larrain, Sialm and Tessada (2018) RFS as the closest institutional cousin (coordinated pension reallocations in Chile).

## Identification, stated plainly

- The flow is uninformed about the stocks: it is triggered by a procurement score on the fund, and 94 per cent of it moves because savers did not reply. The appeal round proves the letter alone does nothing.
- The flow is dated: award is a press release, the letter has a fixed lead, the exit is one day per fund (Pensionsmyndigheten's own process document).
- The dose varies across stocks within a round for reasons unrelated to the stocks' prospects: it is the losers' pre-award portfolio weights times the agency's stake. Round fixed effects absorb anything common to the day.
- Pre-trading by the losing manager between award and exit understates the execution-day effect and shows up as an anticipation effect; both are reported, and the quarterly FI holdings before and after bound how much was pre-sold.
- Winner purchases are not dated, so the sell side carries identification; the net measure is secondary.

## Power, written down (the course asks for it; no prize winner has done it)

- Replication: about 40 to 60 OMXS30 events, CAR standard deviation over a 10-day window about 5 to 6 per cent: minimum detectable mean CAR at 80 per cent power about 2 to 3 per cent. The 1990s S&P effect was 7 per cent, the 2010s effect 1 per cent; the Swedish replication can distinguish "large" from "gone", not finer.
- Extension, stock level: about 200 names in Swedish active, about 150 in small cap, about 150 in Nordic active; sd of CAR[-1,+5] about 4 per cent for large caps and 6 per cent for small caps; sd of pressure 0.25 to 0.35 pp of market cap (active) and 0.56 to 0.73 pp (small cap). Minimum detectable slope: about 2.7 per cent of CAR per pp of pressure in the active round alone, about 2.0 in small cap alone, about 1.4 to 1.6 pooled. Published magnitudes (Lou 2012; Coval and Stafford 2007; Da et al. 2018) sit around 1 to 2 per cent per pp over a quarter, so the pooled test is adequately powered only for an effect at the upper end of the published range. The thesis therefore commits in advance to reporting the MDE next to every estimate and to treating a precise null as a finding (as the 2024 prize winner did). Expressing pressure in days of volume raises the dose dispersion in small caps and is the pre-registered secondary measure.
- Incidence, index losers: daily tracking error 3 to 5 bp against 10 to 20 bp of expected dilution; with about 10 funds and about 10 tranche days the standard error is under 1 bp. Active losers: 30 to 50 bp daily noise against 5 to 15 bp signal, 18 funds, one or two days each: borderline, reported with the index result as the sharp one.

## What this process produced, relative to the two earlier folders
