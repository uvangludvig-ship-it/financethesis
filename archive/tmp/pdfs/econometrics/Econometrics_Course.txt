    This course offers an introductory overview of econometric methods and concepts specifically
relevant to the field of finance. My goal is to give you a broad understanding of these techniques
and their implementation in Python, but please note that this is not an exhaustive course. For more
comprehensive coverage and practical applications, you can consider further specialized courses or
books.


1      Course Format
The course consists of four in-person lectures in which I cover the fundamental concepts and methods.
In class, we also discuss how to implement these techniques in Python: we code together and work
through problems, so bring a laptop.
    There are no graded assignments and no exam. The course is there to give you a solid foundation
in econometric methods and their relevance in finance, and to support the thesis you write afterwards.
What you get out of it depends on how much you engage with the material during the lectures and
the research phase. If you have any questions or require further information, please feel free to reach
out to me.


2      Prerequisites
Officially, there are no prerequisites for this course. However, it is highly recommended that you
have a basic understanding of probability and statistics, which will be beneficial for your learning
experience. I do my best to explain the concepts in a clear and accessible manner, but having some
prior knowledge in these areas will help you grasp the material more effectively.
    Additionally, while this course focuses on econometric methods and their implementation in
Python, it is not a programming course. Therefore, having some familiarity with Python will be
advantageous. If you are not familiar with Python, I strongly recommend that you look at the
materials I post on Canvas. These materials will help you get started and give you the skills you
need to implement the econometric methods we cover in the course.

Software setup
Please arrive at the first session with a working Python environment. We do not spend class time
on installation.

    • Python 3.10 or newer. The simplest route is the Anaconda distribution, which bundles
      almost everything below.
    • Packages: numpy, pandas, statsmodels, linearmodels, matplotlib. With Anaconda, only
      linearmodels typically needs to be installed separately (pip install linearmodels).
    • An editor: Jupyter Notebook, JupyterLab, or VS Code. Any of these is fine; use whichever
      you are most comfortable with.
    • No-install alternative: Google Colab runs in the browser with the packages above already
      available. This is a reasonable fallback if you run into installation problems, though for thesis
      work a local setup is more convenient.

   To check that you are ready, run import numpy, pandas, statsmodels, linearmodels with-
out errors before the first lecture. If it fails, contact me in advance rather than on the day.

4     Research Phase
After completing the course, you will have the opportunity to work on your own project, and I will
be available to provide support during this period. You can book a time slot through this link. You
can also use this time to ask any questions you may have about the course material.
   Please note that while I can offer guidance and support in the areas of econometrics and coding,
I may not know all fields of finance. However, I will do my best to help you with the econometric
and technical aspects of your project.
   I am glad to work with you on your project, and I look forward to seeing what you find.
   P.S.: Chapter 15 of Brooks (2019) provides a good insight into the research process.


5     General Information
    • In your actual research or work, you will face many problems that you have not seen before.
      Therefore, you should learn how to find the answer to your questions. Part of what I am
      trying to do here is help you build that skill. Keep in mind that there is no unique process for
      answering your questions.
    • Before asking a question:

        – Ask Google
        – Use any AI tools that you like!
        – Search in stackoverflow
    • On AI tools: you can never justify a choice in your research with “I asked an AI and it said
      that. . . ”. The argument has to stand on its own. AI models are powerful and genuinely useful,
      but they cannot replace your own thinking: read their answers carefully, check them against
      a source you trust, and make sure you can explain every step of your analysis yourself.
    • Suggested textbooks:
        – Introductory Econometrics for Finance (Brooks 2019)
