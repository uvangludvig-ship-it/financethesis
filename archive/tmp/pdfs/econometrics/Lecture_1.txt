Interpretation of regressions


    Empirical papers until roughly the 1990s (probabilistic causality):
      ▶ The regression was motivated by a complex model, and the equation
        was meant to describe an economic process.
      ▶ All regressors on the right-hand side were more or less equally

        important.
    Empirical papers nowadays (counterfactual causality):
      ▶ Focusing on one causal relationship.
      ▶ Identification strategy meant to eliminate selection bias.
      ▶ Regressors are not equally important, and only one variable is seen as

        having causal effects.
      ▶ All others are controls included in service of the focused causal agenda.

Summary


    Causality requires a prior ordering of variables into causes and effects.
    With observational data, the observed difference in means is ATT
    plus selection bias.
    Our job is to find a setting in which the selection bias is zero.
    OLS estimates the best linear predictor of Y given X ; it recovers the
    CEF when the CEF is linear.
    Interactions: always include the main effects, and be careful when
    interpreting the coefficients.
