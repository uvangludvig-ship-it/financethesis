## 15 candidates (scores: replicable / Swedish edge / mechanism / topical / data, 0-2 each, max 10)

### C1. Dividend-month premium in a compressed annual dividend season (Apr-May) and reaching-for-dividends under negative rates
Anchors: Hartzmark & Solomon 2013 JFE, 2019 JF, 2022 RFS; Daniel, Garlappi & Xiao 2021 JF; Rantapuska 2008 JFE (Finnish ex-day). Replication: monthly panel 1995-2025 (FinBas), long-short "predicted dividend month" vs non-dividend firms, Fama-MacBeth + factor alphas (jkpfactors SWE). Extension: (i) Sweden pays once a year so ~80% of ex-days fall in 6 weeks: tests whether premium is crowded out (price pressure among simultaneous payers) or amplified; (ii) reaching-for-income: premium and ex-day price-drop ratio in negative-rate regime 2015-19 vs 2022-23 hikes; (iii) ISK (no tax on dividends at holder level since 2012) shifts ex-day ratio for retail-heavy stocks (Modular Finance Holdings ownership shares). H1 (price pressure/reaching for income): premium concentrates in high-retail, high-yield stocks and rises when rates fall. H2 (tax clientele/risk): ex-day ratio moves with ISK adoption; no rate sensitivity. Informative null: no premium in a season where all payers ex-date together rejects "dividend-seeking flow" as a mechanism. Risks: overlapping event windows, small-N months; must handle 2020 dividend cancellations (natural shock — also a feature). Scores 2/2/2/1/2 = 9.

### C2. Whose surprise moves Swedish assets? Riksbank vs ECB vs Fed high-frequency shocks 2004-2024
Anchors: Nakamura & Steinsson 2018 QJE; Bauer & Swanson 2023 AER; Altavilla et al. 2019 JME; Brusa, Savor & Wilson 2020 RoF; Hillenbrand 2025 RFS. Replication: regress daily/intraday OMXS30, bank, real-estate, 2y/10y SEK yields (SWEA) on the three surprise series. Extension: decompose Riksbank surprises into target/path/information (sign of stock vs yield co-movement) and test whether the "information effect" disappears when controlling for pre-meeting macro news (Bauer-Swanson) — Sweden has a tiny domestic information advantage relative to ECB/Fed, so an information effect that survives is hard to rationalise; negative-rate 2015-19 sub-sample tests the "reversal rate" for bank stocks. H1: domestic rate surprises dominate real-estate/bank stocks; ECB/Fed dominate index. H2: small open economy: Riksbank surprises are nearly fully priced via ECB spillover (null on domestic). Informative null either way. Risks: Riksbank database URL unverified; 8 meetings/yr → ~160 events; overlap with past winner (bank stocks/FOMC) must be pre-empted by focusing on the information-effect decomposition. Scores 2/2/2/2/1 = 9.

### C3. Fund fragility: the March 2020 suspension of Swedish corporate-bond funds
Anchors: Falato, Goldstein & Hortaçsu 2021 JME; Ma, Xiao & Zeng 2022 RFS; Jiang, Li, Sun & Wang 2022 JFE; Choi et al. 2020 JFE. Replication: flow-performance concavity and outflow sensitivity to illiquid holdings, Swedish fixed-income funds 2015-2025 (SHoF Morningstar daily NAV/TNA/flows; FI quarterly holdings since 2018). Extension: ~35 Swedish funds suspended dealing 19-24 March 2020 (no equivalent in EU peers): event-study of flows around suspension/reopening, and whether suspended funds sold liquid assets first (reverse flight to liquidity) using FI Q1 vs Q2 2020 holdings; 2022-23 real-estate bond crunch as second episode. H1 (strategic complementarity): outflows accelerate before gates in funds with more illiquid real-estate paper; reopening brings persistent outflows. H2 (liquidity shock only): flows track NAV losses; no first-mover advantage. Informative null: no run dynamics in the most gated market in Europe would challenge the run narrative. Risks: bond price data thin (Nasdaq Stockholm bond trade reports only partial); attribution of holdings quarterly. Scores 2/2/2/2/1 = 9.

### C4. Riksbank corporate-bond purchases 2020-22: per-ISIN price and issuance effects
Anchors: Todorov 2020 JFE; Grosse-Rueschkamp, Steffen & Streitz 2019 JFE; Boyarchenko, Kovner & Shachar 2022 JFE; Gilchrist et al. 2024 JME. Replication: DiD eligible (IG, SEK, non-financial) vs ineligible bonds/issuers around announcement (1 Jul 2020) and purchase start (Sep 2020), and issuance response. Extension: Riksbank publishes holdings per ISIN → actual purchases vs eligibility (announcement vs "what you buy"); real-estate-heavy eligible universe lets you test spillover to equity of eligible issuers (FinBas). H1: announcement effect dominates, purchases add nothing. H2: local supply (per-ISIN) matters in a thin market. Informative null: no per-ISIN effect in one of the most concentrated purchase programmes. Risks: bond prices — need Nasdaq/Refinitiv; equity-only version still feasible. Scores 1/2/2/1/1 = 7.

### C5. Short-sale disclosure at 0.5%: bunching, hidden shorts and return predictability
Anchors: Jank, Roling & Smajlbegovic 2021 JFE; Jones, Reed & Waller 2016 RFS; Muravyev, Pearson & Pollet 2025 JF. Replication: density of positions just below 0.5% (FI daily since 2012), returns after named disclosures. Extension: FI aggregate ≥0.1% series lets you measure undisclosed vs disclosed short interest by stock; test whether anomalies' short legs earn less in stocks where shorts hide (Muravyev et al.); Sweden's 2020 no-ban vs Spain/Italy/France/Belgium/Austria/Greece as DiD in cross-listed/peer stocks (Beber & Pagano 2013 JF; Boehmer, Jones & Zhang 2013 RFS). H1: disclosure deters informed shorts → weaker predictability at the threshold. H2: disclosure conveys information → copycat returns. Null: no bunching means the cost is trivial. Risks: past winner on short-sale bans (keep the ban part as robustness, not main). Scores 2/2/1/1/2 = 8.

### C6. Insiders below the top and MAR closed periods in the FI register (2016-2026)
Anchors: Hvide & Nielsen 2026 JFE; Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012 JF; Kacperczyk & Pagnotta 2024 JF; Ali & Hirshleifer 2017 JFE. Replication: abnormal returns after routine vs opportunistic insider trades, by rank (CEO/CFO vs other PDMR/closely associated persons). Extension: MAR 30-day closed periods create a sharp timing discontinuity; dual-class firms and ownership spheres let you test whether controlling-family associates trade informatively; take-private wave 2023-25 pre-bid trades. H1: profitability concentrates below the top (less scrutiny). H2: legal risk (FI enforcement 2018+) suppresses informed trades at all ranks. Null: no informativeness anywhere in a small, high-transparency market. Risks: register scraping; identity matching. Scores 2/2/2/2/2 = 10 (but agent 17 already flagged; overlaps corporate track).

### C7. Migration to the closing auction on Nasdaq Stockholm and price discovery
Anchors: Jegadeesh & Wu 2022 JFE; Comerton-Forde & Putniņš 2015 JFE; (Bogousslavsky & Muravyev 2023 JFM, non-core). Replication: closing-auction share of volume 2010-2025, auction-to-next-open reversals, imbalance predictability (SHoF Nasdaq HFT order book). Extension: Swedish specifics — dividend-season ex-days, OMXS30 review days (avoid index-inclusion framing: use as instrument for auction volume only), XACT ETF rebalancing flows at 17:25-17:30. H1: passive growth makes closes noisier (reversals rise). H2: auction concentration improves discovery (lower next-day reversal). Null: no change in reversal despite doubling of auction share. Risks: only two core anchors; "liquidity providers" exclusion nearby. Scores 2/1/1/1/2 = 7.

### C8. Night and day in Stockholm: overnight vs intraday returns with a 15:30 US overlap
Anchors: Lou, Polk & Skouras 2019 JFE; Bogousslavsky 2021 JFE; Hendershott, Livdan & Rösch 2020 JFE. Replication: decompose factor and anomaly returns into overnight/intraday (FinBas open/close; check open price availability — otherwise SHoF order book 2010+). Extension: Stockholm's day has a pre-US (09:00-15:30) and US-overlap (15:30-17:30) segment: does the "tug of war" (retail overnight vs institutional intraday) load on the overlap? Retail ownership from Modular Finance Holdings as cross-sectional sorting. H1: overnight premium is retail-driven (stronger in high-retail stocks, 2020-21). H2: it is an intermediary-inventory/risk premium (loads on US overlap). Null: no overnight/intraday split in a market without an official pre-market session. Risks: open price data; bid-ask bounce at open. Scores 2/1/1/1/1 = 6.

### C9. The price of a vote: A/B share pairs through the 2023-25 take-private wave
Anchors: Kalay, Karakaş & Pant 2014 JF; Levit, Malenko & Maug 2024 JF; Li, Maug & Schwartz-Ziv 2022 RFS. Replication: voting premium from ~40 listed A/B pairs (FinBas), 1990-2025, vs liquidity and control contestability. Extension: premium dynamics around AGM record dates (vote trading), takeover bids (mandatory bid rule/equal treatment), ownership-sphere concentration (Holdings), and Listing Act 2026 multiple-vote share reforms. H1: premium reflects expected control transfer (spikes at bids/AGMs). H2: premium is a liquidity discount on B... or A illiquidity discount making it negative. Null: no premium movement at record dates rejects vote trading. Risks: thin A-share trading; few events. Scores 2/2/1/1/2 = 8.

### C10. Report-day congestion: 40 quarterly reports on one morning

Replication: PEAD and announcement-day volume as a function of same-day report count. Extension: calendar-rotation IV (Noh et al.): Swedish firms fix report dates a year ahead, so weekday/Easter/dividend-season rotations shift congestion exogenously; retail ownership as attention proxy. H1: congestion → underreaction and larger drift. H2: institutional pre-scheduling neutralises it. Null: no drift difference on the most congested mornings in Europe. Risks: needs earnings-surprise data (analyst forecasts — I/B/E/S access?). Scores 2/2/1/1/1 = 7.

## Top 5 (ranked)
1. C1 Dividend-month premium in the compressed Swedish season (9). Best combination of a canonical, replicable anomaly, a genuinely sharpening Swedish feature (annual payouts crowd ex-days into six weeks; ISK; negative rates; 2020 cancellations), free data end-to-end, and two mechanisms with opposite predictions on timing and rate sensitivity. Prior reports' top-2; it survives the adversarial check.
2. C3 Corporate-bond fund suspensions March 2020 (9). Sweden is the only European market where dozens of funds gated simultaneously; SHoF daily flow data make first-mover/run tests feasible with clean pre-/post-suspension windows; 2022-23 real-estate crunch offers an out-of-sample episode. Main risk is bond-level pricing; the design stands on flows and holdings alone.
3. C2 Riksbank vs ECB vs Fed surprises (9, conditional). Highly topical (2022-23 hikes, information-effect debate), all price data free, and the decomposition question is distinct from the past bank-stock/FOMC winner. Do not commit until the Riksbank high-frequency surprise file is in hand (could not locate the URL today); fallback is building surprises from SWEA daily changes on meeting days.
4. C11 ISK and turn-of-the-year (9). Tight tax-based identification (2012 reform, 2025/26 allowances) with a well-known behavioral anomaly; Kontoghiorghes 2024 JFE is a near-template. Overlaps earlier corporate/tax candidate, so choose only if that track is not used.
5. C5 Short-sale disclosure threshold (8). Fully free daily data since 2012, sharp threshold design, and the 2020 no-ban as a bonus. Least novel of the five (Jank et al. covers EU), so the edge must come from FI's 0.1% aggregate series, which reveals hidden short interest that EU-wide studies lack.
