## B. Ranked ideas (score = replicable in 3 months / Swedish edge / mechanism with informative null / topical 2026 / data feasibility, each 0–2)

**1. Outright QT: flow and stock effects of Riksbank government-bond sales 2023–26 — D'Amico & King 2013 JFE (+ Lou, Yan & Zhang 2013 RFS). Score 2/2/2/2/2 = 10.** The Riksbank was the first major central bank to sell government bonds outright rather than merely let them roll off: 28 reverse auctions from 4 April 2023 to 2026, each with ISIN, offered volume, bid volume and accepted yield on the Riksbank site, plus a monthly ISIN-level holdings history. Replicate D'Amico–King's flow effect (yield change of the sold ISIN and its maturity neighbours on auction day vs. matched non-sold bonds) and stock effect (cumulative holdings share per ISIN vs. yield level), then test the Lou–Yan–Zhang auction-cycle pattern on Riksgälden issuance auctions in the same market. Mechanism: preferred-habitat/local supply; informative null = if Swedish government-bond yields do not react to sales of 1–3 bn SEK per auction, the segmented-market channel that justified QE is symmetric-absent in QT. Data: Riksbank auction pages + CSV, Riksgälden auction results, SWEA daily benchmarks, ISIN-level prices from LSEG at SSE. Note: memo 21 (C13) flagged this; it is NEW relative to the caller's considered list.

**2. Monetary-policy surprises and the investment channel across all Swedish firms — Ottonello & Winberry 2020 Econometrica (identification via Nakamura & Steinsson 2018 QJE). Score 2/2/2/2/1 = 9.** WP 445 provides 20 years of high-frequency Riksbank surprises (fallback: daily changes in 2y SEGVB/3m STIBOR on the ~200 announcement days from SWEA). Merge with Serrano (universe of Swedish limited companies, including private) to estimate whether investment responses to a 25 bp surprise are stronger for low-leverage/high-distance-to-default firms (O–W) or for young/small/bank-dependent firms (Cloyne et al.). Swedish edge: almost all corporate debt is floating-rate bank debt, so cash-flow pass-through is immediate; the 2022–23 cycle (0→4%) is the largest tightening in the sample and the first with private-firm coverage. Informative null: no heterogeneity by financial position in a floating-rate economy would contradict the O–W "risk-premium" channel. Feasibility hinge: get the WP 445 event file from the authors this week; otherwise use daily surprises. NEW relative to the list; memo 21 (C2) considered only asset-price responses, not the firm-level investment channel.

**3. Fund gating and a central-bank backstop: Swedish corporate-bond funds in March 2020 vs. 2022 — Falato, Goldstein & Hortaçsu 2021 JME (+ Ma, Xiao & Zeng 2022 RFS; Todorov 2020 JFE). Score 2/2/2/1/1 = 8.** About 35 Swedish corporate-bond funds suspended redemptions on 20 March 2020, and the Riksbank began buying corporate bonds in September 2020 (ISIN-level purchases and holdings public). FI's quarterly holdings (2018Q4–2026Q2, ~700 funds) give fund-by-ISIN exposure to Riksbank-eligible (IG, non-financial) vs. ineligible bonds. Design: fund flows and NAV recovery as a function of pre-crisis share of eligible bonds; bond-level DiD around eligibility à la Todorov; then repeat for the 2022 real-estate-bond outflow episode when there was no backstop. Informative null: if flows and pricing recover no faster for eligible-heavy funds, the "backstop channel" is absent and gating alone explains stabilisation. Overlaps memo 20/21 candidates; NEW relative to the list.

**4. Hidden shorts: bunching below the 0.5% disclosure threshold and the SBB episode — Jank, Roling & Smajlbegovic 2021 JFE. Score 2/1/2/1/2 = 8.** FI's register gives every named position ≥0.5% since 2012 (daily), and the aggregated ≥0.1% total per issuer. Replicate the density discontinuity at 0.5%, returns after crossing, and ESMA's 0.1% notification cut in March 2020 as a shock to hidden shorting. Swedish topical hook: the 2022–23 real-estate short campaign (SBB, Viceroy). Informative null: no bunching means disclosure costs are not binding for Nordic hedge funds. Overlaps memo 21 (C5); NEW relative to the list.

**5. Private equity and posted prices in Swedish dentistry — Gupta, Howell, Yannelis & Gupta 2023 RFS. Score 1/2/2/2/1 = 8.** Tandpriskollen (TLV) shows clinic-level posted prices per treatment code against the state reference price; SOU 2024:70 documents PE roll-ups (Colosseum, Aqua Dental, Smile). Scrape prices, match clinics to Serrano ownership histories, DiD around PE acquisition with independent clinics in the same municipality as controls. Mechanism: market power vs. efficiency; the informative null is prices unchanged but volume/mix changed. This is an extension of the considered "PE roll-ups and local market power (Serrano)" — the price outcome is new (Serrano has no prices), the ownership shock is not. Feasibility risk: scraping ~4,000 clinics from a JS app without an open API.

**6. Firm-level labour demand from job ads: PE ownership, rate hikes and skill upgrading — Davis et al. 2014 AER / Hershbein & Kahn 2018 AER. Score 1/2/1/2/2 = 8.** JobTech's historical Platsbanken ads (2006–2026, ~900 MB/year, employer org-nr, full text) merged to Serrano give vacancy counts and skill content for every Swedish firm. Two designs: (i) PE buyout event studies on postings and required skills (Davis et al. employment reallocation); (ii) Hershbein–Kahn: did the 2022–23 tightening accelerate AI/automation-skill demand in financially constrained firms? Informative null: no change in skill mix. Partly overlaps "SME acquisitions by parent type"; the vacancy/skill outcome is NEW.

**7. Loosening macroprudential limits: LTV 85→90% and abolition of the DTI-linked amortisation rule, 1 April 2026 — DeFusco, Johnson & Mondragon 2019 REStud (+ Benetton 2021 JF). Score 1/2/1/2/1 = 7.** Prop. 2025/26:119 (verified) is the first Swedish loosening after a decade of tightening. Exposure varies across municipalities by the share of buyers constrained at 85% LTV or 4.5× DTI (proxy: SCB price-to-income and FI's bolåneundersökning regional tables). Outcomes from Mäklarstatistik/SCB BO0501 (monthly prices, volumes, lagfarter) and Booli listings: do transaction volumes and prices rise more in high-exposure municipalities from April 2026? Informative null: no response means the constraints did not bind at the margin (consistent with bank-imposed KALP tests). Limitation: only ~8 months of post-period by December 2026; loan-level bunching (DeFusco) requires FI micro data. GENUINELY NEW — not in the considered list nor in memos 01–21.

**8. The Treasury auction cycle in a small market — Lou, Yan & Zhang 2013 RFS. Score 2/1/1/1/2 = 7.** Riksgälden auction calendar (nominal, real, T-bills) plus SWEA daily yields since 1990s: yields rise before and revert after auctions; test whether the cycle scales with dealer balance-sheet constraints (post-2015 negative rates, Basel III) and disappears when the Riksbank was the marginal buyer 2015–2021. Cheap to do; best as a chapter in idea 1. NEW relative to the list.

**9. Insiders below the top in the FI register 2016–2026 — Cziraki & Gider 2021 RoF / Cohen, Malloy & Pomorski 2012 JF. Score 1/1/2/1/1 = 6.** MAR-era register with rank, instrument and price; routine vs. opportunistic classification and dollar profits; First North small caps 2020–21 IPO wave as the Swedish hook. Downgraded because the marknadssok export endpoint returned 404 today (site migration) — re-check before committing. Overlaps memo 21 (C6).

**10. Sticky or discerning money in the premium pension — Sialm, Starks & Zhang 2015 JF. Score 1/2/1/1/1 = 6.** Pensionsmyndigheten's monthly per-fund flow statistics (online 2025–26, older by request) and daily NAVs since 2000 let you test flow–performance sensitivity in a default-heavy DC system, with the 2019 fund-platform purge and the 2024–26 procurement as menu shocks. NOT new — the considered "premium pension procurement flows (FI holdings)" covers the same shock; this is only a different anchor paper.
