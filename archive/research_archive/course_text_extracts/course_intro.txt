REPLICATION AND EXTENSION OF A PUBLISHED PAPER

•   Standard for BSc thesis: replication and extension of a (recent) published
    paper in a top journal
•   Guarantees that thesis is at frontier of research, uses correct methodology,
    asking relevant question
•   The extension needs to be meaningful
    • Avoid default option of applying the question to Nordic countries unless meaningful
    • Potential extensions: different dataset, time, application, methodology, same method
      but different question, etc. (usually a meaningful extension will fulfill more than one of
      these criteria)

•   Departing from the suggested format (extension of a paper) is risky. It can
    work if the question is very original and interesting
    • But: time-constraint
    • Potential high reward, but very high risk

RESEARCH PROCESS

1. Explore the frontier of the literature
•   Find the closest, most relevant papers
•   Check with your tutor to see if you understand the literature

2. Define a quantitative methodology
•   What is the methodology used in the literature?
•   Can you obtain the relevant data to answer your question?
•   What is the simplest way to identify the mechanism studied?
•   Build your contribution on solid ground by replicating the main quantitative exercise of a
    key paper in the literature

3. Starting writing early will help you formulate your questions
•   Constant feedback between writing and research

                                                   17
WRITING

•   Read published papers in top journal (see list in the Syllabus) to understand how to
    write a paper in Finance/Economics
•   Do not write it as a student project with a table of contents, a complete literature
    review, off-topic digressions, and pedagogic explanation of standard
    methodology (e.g., OLS) and background (do not do that!)
•   An academic paper is straightforward/to the point: every section, paragraph,
    sentence has a clear objective and is necessary
•   An academic paper is addressed to someone knowledgeable about the topic,
    not to BSc students
•   Familiarize yourself with scientific writing by reading articles in top journals and
    attempt to emulate as much as possible this writing style and format in your BSc
    thesis
•   The formatting of the final thesis (e.g., tables, figures, references) should look like a
    paper in one of the top 3 finance journals (JF, RFS, JFE)

WRITING

As close as possible to a published article in a top journal in Finance
•   Introduction (2-4 pages)
    • Paragraphs: background/motivation, research question, methodology, results, interpretation
    • What is the question? Why is it interesting/relevant? What is the methodology? What are the results?
    • Intro = self contained summary of paper

•   Related Literature (<1 page)
    • What are the closest papers and how does the thesis differ?
    • What is the contribution to the literature?
    • It is not a review of the literature!

•   Data and Methodology
•   Main Empirical Analysis
•   Interpretation of Empirical Findings / Discussion
•   Conclusion
•   Total length: up to 40 pages, single-spaced equivalent (including tables)

RESEARCH DESIGN WITH DATA

Textbook procedure for an empirical project:
1. Ask an important / interesting research question
2. Establish a credible estimation strategy
3. Use relevant data for the question


In Finance, most of the data is observational instead of experimental
•   Sample selection bias
•   Minimum detectable effect: check the power of your test

THE PROBLEM OF CAUSALITY


Consider the following questions:
•   Does financial distress reduce enterprise value?
•   Do high cash flows induce firms to invest more?
•   Do CEOs recruited externally create less value?


Correlations little informative because of:
•   Confounding factors (omitted variables)
•   Reverse causality


Answers to these questions require identification of causal links
                                              21
SOLUTIONS

The Statistical/Experimental/Reduced form approach
•   No assumption made on firms’ behavior
•   Counterfactual identified for firms whose treatment status is as good as random
•   Appropriate for corporate finance issues


The Structural approach
•   Behavior of independent variables modelled using theory
•   Counterfactual identified when reality behaves like model
•   Appropriate for asset pricing

REGRESSION
Why the Regression?
•   Useful for both prediction and identification (of causal links)
•   Very robust (does a good job in wide variety of contexts)
•   Starting base for most advanced empirical methods
•   Very transparent (easy to replicate)

IMPORTANT REMARKS

•   The thesis requires a lot of independent work (more than 15 credits worth)
•   Do not procrastinate, begin today.
    • Most thesis groups that start too late run into troubles

•   The research process can be frustrating. The feeling of being lost is part of the
    process as by definition you are pushing the research frontier.
•   A robust research question is a question that is interesting independently of
    the results
    • Interest of the question should not depend on whether you confirm a hypothesis

•   A good research question builds on strong foundations
    • Impossible to design a relevant research question without building on other papers
