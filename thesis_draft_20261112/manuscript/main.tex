\begin{center}\large\bfseries The Index Effect in Sweden\end{center}
\begin{abstract}
We replicate and extend Greenwood and Sammon's (2025) study of the index effect using OMX Stockholm Benchmark changes from July 2007 through June 2026. The complete sample contains 256 additions and 215 deletions. Addition returns from before announcement through after implementation fall from 8.52\% in 2007--2011 to 1.50\% in 2022--2026, but do not decline monotonically. Across 2017--2026, the average remains 2.15\%, with a 95\% interval of [1.01\%, 3.39\%]. An exploratory timing comparison shows that the median announcement-to-implementation gap falls from fourteen to six trading days for additions. On identical event cohorts, returns over the common initial seven trading days are 1.19\% and 1.80\%; the full-window decline is concentrated in the remaining interval. This accounting does not establish that calendar changes cause the decline. Reported fund purchases and concentrated implementation trading provide evidence of benchmark-related activity, while changing holdings coverage limits historical multiplier comparisons. The results distinguish a positive later addition association from the less precisely resolved latest period. They establish neither economic disappearance nor a permanent causal price effect of index membership.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} index effect; replication; passive investing; reconstitution; fund holdings.\par
