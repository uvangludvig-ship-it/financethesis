1. Introduction
1.1. Background and Relevance
Leveraged and inverse exchange traded funds (LETFs) are structurally similar to regular
exchange-traded funds (ETFs) but promise a return multiple of daily index returns.
Leveraged ETEs promise a return multiple of an underlying index greater than one,
whereas inverse ETF's promise an index return multiple smaller than zero. As of January
2018, assets invested in leveraged and inverse ETEs and ETPs (exchange-traded
products) amount to $87.3 billion (Reuters, 2018). For the Swedish LETF market, which
is the geographic scope of this study, this figure is SEK 2.2 billion, as of May 12, 2020
(XACT, 2020).
The complexity of this asset class and its aggressive growth have spurred substantial
academic inquiry into their market stability impact. The main characteristics subject to
inquiry are twofold. Firstly, positive feedback trading takes place when LETEs conduct
their daily rebalancing trading. Thus, if underlying asset returns are positive, LETEs
must buy more of the of the underlying asset to maintain a constant stock-to-cash ratio.
Correspondingly, if underlying asset returns are negative, LETEs sell. As a result, one
may anticipate that the dynamic portfolio rebalancing of LETEs amplifies return and
volatility through non-fundamental positive feedback trading. Additionally, it is likely
that these rebalancing trades have a comparatively larger effect due to their price-
insensitive
nature and condensed time-interval of execution. Secondly, due to the
mathematically predictable nature of these trades, there is likely to be a degree of
equivalently non-fundamental anticipatory trading undertaken by arbitrageurs prior to
LETE rebalancing trades.
The aggregate effects of anticipatory trades and rebalancing trades may 1) prove
debilitating to markets in turbulence, 2) reduce market confidence and 3) lead to market
wide sell-offs or rallies as a consequence of the price impact derived from rebalancing
trades (Tuzun, 2014).
Rebalancing trades occur daily shortly before market close in order to mitigate
tracking error and to maintain a fixed stock-to-cash ratio. The rebalancing trades of
LETEs in Sweden are executed in the futures market, where index arbitrageurs
propagate the effects of LETEs' futures trading to the underlying basket securities. The
size of rebalancing flows depends on the LETE's total net assets (TNA), the target
return multiple, and the daily index movement. With approximately SEK 2.2. billion in
total net assets for OMXS30 tracking LETEs as of April 20, 2020, a 1 % index move
causes LETEs to originate SEK 89 million worth of rebalancing flows, as of April 20,
2020. Tuzun (2014) implements a model of price impact developed in Kyle and
Obizhaeva (2012) to show that the effects of rebalancing flows are notable, especially
during the Financial Crisis 2008-2009, when markets experienced substantial daily
volatility and a large subsequent need for rebalancing. Thus, in times of high volatility,
2
lower liquidity and overall market turbulence, it is plausible that rebalancing trades
could pose elevated risks to market stability.
Daily Swedish LETF rebalancing volumes are relatively small fractions of the daily
volumes of stocks in the OMXS30 but constitute a substantially larger fraction of the
volume during the last hour of trading (see Figures II-IV). For a 1 % index move,
rebalancing flows are associated with a price impact in the average stock in the OMXS30
index of 4.0 basis points and 11.8 basis points of extra end-of-day volatility. During the
Corona Crisis, a 1 % index move is associated with an increased price impact amounting
to 8.9 basis points in an average stock in the OMXS30. This result is driven primarily
by increased volatility in OMXS30 index constituents. Furthermore, we establish support
for the prevalence of anticipatory trading and show that, for a 1 % index move,
anticipatory trading is associated with a price impact of 5.5 basis points in an average
stock in the OMXS30. Equivalently, trading in anticipation of expected rebalancing flows
in response to a 1 % index move increases end-of-day volatility with 16.4 basis points
for an average stock in the OMXS30. Thus, the bulk of end-of-day price and volatility
impacts across stocks seems to be derived from the indirect effect of rebalancing
(anticipatory trading), rather than the direct effect of daily rebalancing trades.
These estimates may be understated if prices for underlying assets already reflect
the anticipated future rebalancing impact, or if traders take the opposite sides of
rebalancing trades (Tuzun, 2014). Thus, to estimate the total effect of rebalancing
trades, we employ the price impact equation from Kyle & Obizhaeva (2012) to determine
the total index level price impact of rebalancing flows implied from index moves of 1 %.
Implied price impacts from rebalancing in excess of 1 % per 1 % index is a critical
level for market stability since this creates a risk for a 'cascade reaction' where
rebalancing trade volumes increase exponentially and become self-sustaining. By
computing historical implied price impacts, we show that this level was exceeded for
several months during the Financial Crisis, and for several weeks during the
contemporary Corona Crisis. Therefore, we provide support for the idea that LETEs
may have contributed notably to market turbulence during the contemporary Corona
Crisis and the Financial Crisis.
Finally, we conclude that implied price impacts from rebalancing trades are
insignificant in 'normal' markets but may prove destabilising to markets experiencing
high volatility or crisis.
1.2. Research Question
Our research seeks to determine the end-of-day volatility and price pressure impact from
rebalancing trades and anticipatory trading in the Swedish market. Furthermore, we aim
to outline the market stability effect of LETEs during volatile times, by explicitly
analysing the OMXS30 index development between February 24, 2020 and April 20,
2020, defined as the Corona Crisis. Our overarching research question can therefore be
defined as follows:
What is the market stability impact of LETFs' rebalancing trades in the Swedish stock
market?
1.3. Contribution
We consider the contribution of this thesis to be fourfold. Firstly, many studies have
covered LETEs' market stability impact on the US market (Tuzun, T., 2014; Trainor,
W.J., 2010; Cheng, M. & Madhavan, A., 2009; Bai, Q., Bond, S. A. & Hath, B., 2012;
P. Shum et. al., 2016; Ivanov, I.T., Lenkey, S.L., 2018), however, to the best of our
knowledge, there has been no comparable study conducted which covers the Swedish
market. Secondly, through precise knowledge of LETF trade execution, along with the
analytically appealing Swedish LETF market setting, we hope to arrive at more robust
conclusions by excluding confounding factors present in previous papers covering the US
market. These conditions also allow us to test for the prevalence and impact of
anticipatory trading, which has remained an untested hypothesis in previous literature.
Thirdly, since the LETEs' methods of trade execution and general market characteristics
vary over time, it is valuable to provide continuous and contemporary research on the
market stability impact of LETFs. Fourthly, by explicitly analysing the contemporary
Corona Crisis, we provide up-to-date results on the impact of rebalancing flows during
times of high market volatility.
1.4. Scope
This study has been limited to the Swedish market and the OMXS30 index due to the
nature of our data, the unique institutional setting implied by XACT's monopoly
position and our precise knowledge of rebalancing trade execution in the Swedish market.
Our intraday index and stock-level analysis is conducted on a sample period ranging
from September 19, 2019 until April 20, 2020. The remainder of the analysis conducted
is based on a sample period ranging from April 18, 2005, which was the first trading day
of the first Swedish LETF, through April 20, 2020.

2. Theory, Practice and Literature Review
This section provides the general mathematical framework behind LET rebalancing
along with some comments regarding its important aspects. Subsequently, we provide
firm-specific institutional details of Swedish LETFs managed by XACT, along with some
specifications of the nature of rebalancing trade execution. After that, we describe the
mechanism that propagates demand shocks from the index futures market to the
underlying index constituents. Following this, we present a literature review of the
previous academic papers pertinent to this thesis, along with a conceptual description of
