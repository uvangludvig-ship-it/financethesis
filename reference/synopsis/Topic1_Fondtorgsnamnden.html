Thesis Topic 1: The Impact of the Swedish Fund Selection Agency (Fondtorgsnämnden) on the Mutual Fund Market

Short description

Fondtorgsnämnden was created in 2022 to make the premium pension fund platform more transparent and efficient. Before, any approved fund could be listed on the platform. Now funds are selected through public procurements, one category at a time. Only a few funds win each category. The other funds are removed, and savers' money is moved to a selected fund unless they choose one themselves. Between 2024 and 2026 this has been done for European, global, Nordic and Swedish equity funds, involving more than SEK 200 billion. This thesis studies how the reform has affected the Swedish mutual fund market, with a focus on fees, the number of funds offered, and investor behaviour.

Tentative research question

How has the procurement of the premium pension platform affected fund fees, the supply of funds and investor flows in Sweden, and has the effect spread beyond the platform to the retail fund market?

The question matters because it tests whether competition between funds delivers low fees on its own, or whether an agency that selects funds on fees and quality can do better. We will test three things: (i) whether fees fall for the selected funds, and whether the same funds also lower fees for retail investors outside the platform; (ii) whether removed funds shrink, merge or close, so that fewer funds are offered; and (iii) whether savers actively choose a new fund or are moved by default, and whether the selected funds perform worse after receiving large inflows. A zero result is also interesting: it would mean the reform helps pension savers but does not change the wider fund market.

Data and method

Data. Morningstar (via the Swedish House of Finance): monthly fees, assets, flows and returns for all funds sold in Sweden, 2018–2026. Pensionsmyndigheten (public): monthly assets and net trading per fund on the platform, removal dates, fees after rebate, and the share of savers making an active choice. Fondtorgsnämnden (public): award decisions with bidders, winners, capital and dates. The public data were downloaded on 7 September 2026; Morningstar access will be confirmed in the first week.

Method. We build a panel of funds by month. Since categories are procured at different times, we compare funds in procured categories before and after the award with funds in categories that have not yet been procured (difference-in-differences with fund and category-by-month fixed effects). Outcomes are fees on and off the platform, the number of funds per category, flows and how strongly they react to past performance, and the selected funds' risk-adjusted returns after the award. We check that treated and control funds moved in parallel before the award. The global equity procurement, which was appealed and paused in 2026, is used as a placebo.

Short literature review

Berk and Green (2004) show that if investors move money to skilled managers and fund returns fall as funds grow, money flows to good funds until their expected extra return is zero. In their model, fees and performance are what competition produces, and a large inflow to a fund should lower its future performance. Leippold and Rueegg (2020) test how rational and competitive the fund market really is, using a large international sample, and find that it is only partly so: differences in fees persist even when performance does not justify them. Sialm, Starks and Zhang (2015) study U.S. pension plans where the savers are passive but the plan sponsor adds and removes funds from the menu, and show that these menu changes make pension money move to better funds. Fondtorgsnämnden is a national menu setter of this kind, with public and dated decisions. Our thesis uses the predictions of Berk and Green and the fee tests of Leippold and Rueegg, and applies the menu-change idea of Sialm, Starks and Zhang to Sweden, where the staggered procurements give a clear before-and-after comparison.
