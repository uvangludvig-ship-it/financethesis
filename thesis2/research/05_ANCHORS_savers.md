## Agent's overall top 3 for an 8-week BSc thesis on fund-level data

1. **Sialm, Starks and Zhang 2015 JF.** Best fit for both tutor and course standard inside this brief. The replication leg is real and standard: monthly fund flows on lagged performance rank (Sirri-Tufano piecewise), category and month fixed effects, fees and size as controls, on the 2018-2026 PPM panel; Pensionsmyndigheten also publishes monthly fund-trade reports that can cross-check the panel. The FTN extension mirrors their Table VIII exactly: PPM flows split into agency-driven (default moves on known dates) and saver-driven, then Table IX asks whether agency-driven money went to funds that subsequently did better. Add the sex split and the appeal round as a third flow type (information without default). Caveat: the agency versus voluntary split depends on dating each default move from fund event letters, and the removal-month flow mixes both.
2. **Keim and Mitchell 2018 JPEF, with Choukhmane and De Silva 2026 JF for top-journal cover.** The only published study of a menu cut with default mapping, so the natural template for Q10 and Q14 together: opt-out share, fee, risk and allocation changes of mapped money. The 2019 purge is an independent replication sample (313 funds, AP7 or sibling default, 97 per cent defaulted) and FTN is the extension (equivalent-winner default, 94 per cent). Caveat: JPEF is not on the list, its tables are individual-level, and with 67 FTN funds the cross-sectional drivers of a 6 per cent opt-out are underpowered; pool with 2019 or the thesis is descriptive.
3. **Kronlund, Pool, Sialm and Stefanescu 2021 JFE.** The appeal round is the cleanest natural experiment in the whole setting and this is the published template for "information shock, no default": fund-level flows respond to fee and short-term performance after disclosure, less so in large menus. Replication leg without FTN: fee and performance sensitivity of PPM flows and its interaction with category menu size. Extension: flows out of the frozen losers after 24 Feb 2026 versus same-category winners and versus earlier rounds with a default. Caveat: no Swedish disclosure shock was verified, so the replication is of the specification rather than the event; 38 treated funds and a window that may close before December.

Runner-up: Pool, Sialm and Stefanescu 2016 JF if post-exit NAVs can be pulled from Morningstar; it gives an asset-pricing flavour (does removal predict alpha?) the tutor will like.

## Editor's note (main session)

The facts in file 06 settle most of this brief before any regression: 94 per cent of capital moves by default (97 per cent in 2019); the appeal round shows that information without a default releases 1 to 2 per cent in six months, the same as untreated funds; men opt out 1.5 times more than women in both episodes. These are sharp, cheap and new, and they fit none of the tutor's four fields and only 1 of 25 prize winners (file 02). They go into Table 1 of the main thesis as the exogeneity argument for the flow, and the rest is a second paper. SSZ 2015 stays the best anchor for that second paper.
