## B. Synopsis (slides 6 to 8)

- [ ] Two possible thesis topics, 1 to 2 pages per topic.
- [ ] Each topic: clearly defined topic and quantitative exercise.
- [ ] At most one of the two topics may be based on a tutor's suggestion from the "Tutors and topics" document.
- [ ] Feasibility shown: access to the required datasets and the time constraint addressed.
- [ ] Short literature review: 2 to 3 papers from top journals (list in the "Synopsis" document and the Syllabus) closely related to the question, with an explanation of how the thesis builds on them and extends their contribution.
- [ ] Not a copy or modification of a previous thesis topic; start from the academic literature, not prior theses.
- [ ] Submitted on Canvas as a PDF with the file title "BScThesis Surname1 Surname2.pdf".
- [ ] Written in pairs. A student submitting alone is assigned a partner.
- [ ] Tutors may be suggested in the synopsis.
- [ ] Both students must be Finance students; cross-department theses are not supported.
- [ ] Do not contact tutors before the tutor is assigned (allocation within a week of submission).
- [ ] Read the Canvas documents "Synopsis" and "Tutors and topics" before writing.

## C. Topic and anchor (slides 10 to 13, 17, 26)

- [ ] Standard format: replication and extension of a recent published paper in a top journal (slide 10).
- [ ] The extension must be meaningful. Avoid the default of applying the question to Nordic countries unless meaningful. A meaningful extension usually changes more than one of: dataset, time, application, methodology, or same method with a different question.
- [ ] Departing from the replication format is allowed but described as very high risk under the time constraint.
- [ ] Read recent papers (under 15 years) in top finance and economics journals, or working papers of researchers at top universities. Do not read other articles or other BSc theses to find a topic (slide 11).
- [ ] Once the topic is fixed, perform a full literature review, including relevant theses, and acknowledge relevant work in the thesis (slide 11).
- [ ] Check citation counts (over 100 unless the paper is recent) via Google Scholar (slide 12).
- [ ] Data access confirmed (WRDS account, library resources) before committing (slide 13).
- [ ] Method chosen on "simpler is better"; a complicated method must be justified or dropped (slide 13).
- [ ] Replicate the main quantitative exercise of a key paper to build the contribution on solid ground (slide 17).
- [ ] Topic sits in the tutor's area of expertise where possible (slide 11).
- [ ] The question is interesting independently of the result; its interest must not depend on confirming a hypothesis (slide 24).
- [ ] "Do not chase statistical significance." What matters is interesting and reasonable hypotheses (slide 5).
- [ ] The thesis answers "why" (which channels are at play), not only "what" (slide 5).

## D. Substantive requirements (slide 14)

- [ ] As close as possible, in all dimensions, to a published article in a top finance journal (list in the Syllabus).
- [ ] Question based on solid theoretical and/or empirical grounds.
- [ ] Transparent formulation of the research question.
- [ ] Generation of new knowledge and understanding of the current state of knowledge.
- [ ] Explicit use of relevant theory and/or models.
- [ ] Robust methods of data collection and analysis.
- [ ] Follows the structure and writing style of top-journal articles.
- [ ] Grading criteria (slide 4): Useful (to a researcher in the field), Correct (accurate analysis, proper methodology), Relevant (fits recent literature), Contribution (extends existing research meaningfully), Independence.

## E. Structure and length (slide 19)

- [ ] Introduction: 2 to 4 pages. Paragraphs covering background and motivation, research question, methodology, results, interpretation. The introduction is a self-contained summary of the paper.
- [ ] Related literature: under 1 page. The closest papers, how the thesis differs, the contribution. Not a review of the literature.
- [ ] Data and Methodology.
- [ ] Main Empirical Analysis.
- [ ] Interpretation of Empirical Findings / Discussion.
- [ ] Conclusion.
- [ ] Total length: up to 40 pages, single-spaced equivalent, including tables. (The slide does not say whether references and appendix are excluded; assume they count unless Canvas says otherwise.)
- [ ] No table of contents (slides 14 and 18).

## F. Writing style and formatting (slides 14, 18, 19)

- [ ] Read top-journal papers to learn the form; emulate their writing style and format.
- [ ] Not a student project: no table of contents, no complete literature review, no off-topic digressions, no pedagogic explanation of standard methodology (for example OLS) or background.
- [ ] Every section, paragraph and sentence has a clear objective and is necessary.
- [ ] Addressed to someone knowledgeable about the topic, not to BSc students.
- [ ] Formatting of tables, figures and references looks like a paper in JF, RFS or JFE.
- [ ] Table captions are detailed and contain all the information needed to understand the table (slide 14).
- [ ] Practice, not rule: the winners restate the estimated equation and define every symbol in each caption, state the sample period and the standard-error treatment, and convert coefficients to economic units in the text.

## G. Research design and inference (slides 20 to 23)

- [ ] An important or interesting research question.
- [ ] A credible estimation strategy.
- [ ] Relevant data for the question.
- [ ] Sample selection bias addressed (observational data).
- [ ] Minimum detectable effect: check the power of the test and state it (slide 20). No prior winner did this; the slides ask for it.
- [ ] Causality: confounding factors (omitted variables) and reverse causality discussed; correlations alone are "little informative" (slide 21).
- [ ] The approach is named: reduced form (counterfactual from as-good-as-random treatment, corporate finance) or structural (behaviour modelled from theory, asset pricing) (slide 22).
- [ ] Regression is the default tool: transparent, replicable (slide 23).
