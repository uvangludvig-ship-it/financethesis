## 0. Title and abstract (1 page)

Purpose. Name the object, the setting and the finding in one breath.

What the winners did. Four of five use a colon title: a hook, then a descriptive subtitle ("Chasing Your Own Tail: The Market Stability Impact of Leveraged and Inverse Exchange-Traded Funds"). Abstracts are 120 to 200 words and carry the headline numbers (2016: "a total mean absolute pricing error of 6.8% per annum"; 2020: five numbers in basis points) and, in the last sentence, the calibration (2016: "Our results do not prove the validity of the model, but rather bring into question the validity of the standard benchmarks").

Exhibits. Title: "Pre-announced, uninformed, and large: the price of moving the premium pension". Add a subtitle naming the two settings, for example "Index reconstitutions and Fondtorgsnämnden's fund procurements on Nasdaq Stockholm". Abstract of at most 180 words with: the anchor (Greenwood and Sammon 2025), the replication number (OMXSB announcement-to-effective abnormal return by sub-period), the FTN number (basis points per unit of forced flow over ADV, and the reversal), the incidence finding (who paid), and the cost-benefit sentence against SEK 178m a year. Keywords, tutor, examiner, as in 2020 and 2024.

## 1. Introduction (3 pages) plus related literature (under 1 page)

Purpose. A self-contained summary of the paper: background and motivation, question, method, results, interpretation (course_intro.txt slide 19). A reader who stops after page 3 must know the answer and its size.

What the winners did. 2020 states the research question in italics and gives every headline number by page 3. 2024 does the same and closes the intro with a run-in paragraph headed "Related literature" of exactly one page. 2015 names and numbers the hypotheses inside the intro and then writes "Taking both hypotheses to the data, we reject the first but find strong support for the second". 2016 gives the result and then the limit of comparability in the same paragraph. All five end with a one-paragraph roadmap.

What the first page must do, paragraph by paragraph.
1. The fact. A state agency redeems between a fifth and almost half of each losing fund on dates it announced months earlier, for reasons that have nothing to do with the stocks the funds hold. Give the total money moved and the largest single redemption as a share of the fund and as days of volume in the most exposed stock.
2. The anchor and the replication, in two sentences. Greenwood and Sammon (2025) show the S&P 500 index effect fell from several percent to near zero over four decades even as index assets grew. OMXSB reconstitutions 2013 to 2026, 394 events, tracker assets from SEK 15bn to 120bn, let you ask whether a small market with rising tracker demand shows the same disappearance. Report the number.
3. The extension and why it is not "the same thing in Sweden". The FTN procurement is pre-announced (like a reconstitution) and uninformed (like a reconstitution) but is larger relative to the funds' holdings than any reconstitution, and it has three dated steps (award, saver letter, execution), which separates anticipation from execution. It also has a fund-level dimension no reconstitution has: who bears the cost, stayers or movers.
4. Results with numbers. The pressure coefficient in basis points per percent of ADV, the share of the move that happens before execution, the reversal horizon, the NAV-dilution estimate for funds without swing pricing, and the cost in SEK against the SEK 178m a year of fee savings.
5. What the thesis does not claim (one sentence each): causality for anticipatory trading, welfare (price pressure is partly a transfer to liquidity providers), generalisation to larger procurements.
6. Contribution in three numbered points, as in 2015 and 2020, and the roadmap.

Related literature (run-in paragraph, under one page). Three strands, two or three papers each, and for each strand one sentence on how the thesis differs: (i) the index effect and its decline (Shleifer 1986; Harris and Gurel 1986; Chang, Hong and Liskovich 2015; Greenwood and Sammon 2025); (ii) flow-induced price pressure and slow-moving capital (Coval and Stafford 2007; Lou 2012; Greenwood 2005; Duffie 2010; Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2005); (iii) the Swedish premium pension and fund flows (Cronqvist and Thaler 2004; Dahlquist, Martinez and Söderlind 2017, plus any FTN-specific work). Cite the 2015 and 2020 SSE winners if their exposure measure or table design is borrowed; the course slides treat uncited borrowing of table formats as plagiarism. Verify every citation against the original before it goes in.

Trap. A literature review. The course slides say "It is not a review of the literature!" and the winners never exceed a page. The second trap is modesty: the FTN section is the contribution, the replication is the proof of competence; the intro must say so in that order of importance.

## 2. Institutional background (2.5 pages)

Purpose. Turn the institution into the identification. The reader must finish this section convinced that the flows are (a) decided by a regulator, not by the market, (b) dated to the day, and (c) large relative to the holdings they hit.

What the winners did. 2020 gave XACT's monopoly and the exact rebalancing window (2.2, one page) and a worked numerical example ("XACT BEAR 2 rebalanced 63.42 % of its total net assets"). 2024 gave the law in one paragraph, the dates of first reading, enactment and both deadlines, and a paragraph on why the control group is valid. 2017 SV (Swedish unbundling) and 2018 PH (Norwegian parking) did the same. None wrote a history of the institution.

Structure.
2.1 OMXSB reconstitutions. The review cycle, the announcement notice, the effective date, which funds track the index and their assets over time (the SEK 15bn to 120bn series). One sentence on what the tracker must do on the effective day.
2.2 Fondtorgsnämnden's procurement, step by step. (1) FTN announces a procurement for a fund category. (2) Bids and evaluation. (3) Award decision (tilldelningsbeslut), published on a date, with a standstill and the possibility of appeal (record any delays). (4) Fund agreements. (5) Pensionsmyndigheten writes to savers in non-awarded funds; the letter date; the choice deadline; what happens to savers who do not choose (name the destination fund or the default, and cite the source). (6) Execution: the state redeems the premium-pension holding in each losing fund and buys the destination funds; the execution date or dates; whether redemption is in cash or in kind. (7) Fee savings as reported by the authority (SEK 178m a year; cite). For each step give the date source.
2.3 Why the flows are uninformed, pre-announced and large. One paragraph each. Uninformed: the award criteria (fees, quality, sustainability) do not depend on the stocks held. Pre-announced: the award-to-execution lag in days, with the range across procurements. Large: the redeemed share of each losing fund (20 to 44 percent) and the implied sales in days of ADV for the most exposed stocks.

Exhibits. Figure 1: a timeline of one procurement round with award, letter, execution marked, and the daily premium-pension capital of one losing fund dropping on execution. Appendix Table A1: every procurement with category, award date, letter date, execution date(s), number of losing funds, money moved, and the destination.

Trap. Writing three pages on the history of the premium pension system since 2000. The second trap is vagueness about the destination of the money: without knowing where it went, the net flow into each stock cannot be computed and the pressure measure is wrong by construction.

## 3. Theory and hypotheses (3 pages)

Purpose. Give the reader a reason to expect a sign and a size before seeing a table, and tie each hypothesis to one table.

What the winners did. 2015 took one published utility function (Brennan 1993), changed one argument, stated the resulting pricing equation and delegated the derivation ("Brennan shows that solving the maximization problem ... provides the following asset pricing model"), then drew the predicted price path as a schematic figure with a stand-alone caption. 2020 wrote the rebalancing identity in four lines and stated the Kyle-Obizhaeva impact formula with a footnote to the derivation. 2021 derived fifteen equations over nine pages; that is the ceiling, not the model. 2016 did it in words. The rule: one equation per idea, intuition in plain sentences, derivation cited.

Structure.
3.1 A pre-announced uninformed flow with risk-averse liquidity providers (Greenwood 2005; Duffie 2010). State the reduced form, not the model:

   (1) P_t = P* - λ_t u, where u is the known future flow and λ_t is the price concession demanded by the liquidity providers who hold the position between t and the time the flow is absorbed.

   Intuition: when the flow is announced some capital positions itself, so part of the move happens at announcement; the rest happens at execution because capital is slow; afterwards the concession reverses as more capital arrives. Three predictions: announcement effect, execution effect, reversal. Draw it in Figure 3's schematic panel (as 2015 did in Figure 2).
3.2 Predatory trading (Brunnermeier and Pedersen 2005). One paragraph. Strategic traders who know the date and size of a forced sale sell ahead of it and buy back after, so the price overshoots at execution and the reversal is larger than the slow-capital story alone implies. The prediction that separates the two stories is the sign of the execution-day move relative to the pre-execution drift, and the size of the post-execution recovery.
3.3 Square-root benchmark for expected impact.

   (2) E|ΔP/P| = Y · σ_i · sqrt(Q_i / V_i), with σ_i daily volatility, Q_i the forced quantity, V_i average daily volume, Y a constant around 0.5 to 1 from the literature (cite Almgren et al. 2005; Tóth et al. 2011; Frazzini, Israel and Moskowitz 2018; and Kyle and Obizhaeva 2016 for the invariance version the 2020 winner used).

   Use eq (2) twice: ex ante, to say what size of effect the design should expect for a typical exposure, and ex post, as the benchmark in the cost-benefit table.
3.4 Hypotheses. Four, numbered, each with the sign, the test and the table:

   H1 (replication: demand scaling and disappearance). Around OMXSB reconstitutions, additions earn positive and deletions negative abnormal returns from announcement to effective date; in the cross-section the effect increases in expected tracker demand scaled by ADV; whether it has declined over 2013 to 2026 as in Greenwood and Sammon is the sub-question. Table 2.

   H2 (FTN price pressure and reversal). Stocks with larger expected net forced sales relative to ADV earn lower abnormal returns in the execution window, and the effect reverses within k weeks. Table 3 and Figure 3.

   H3 (anticipation). A measurable part of the move occurs between the award (or the saver letter) and execution. Under predatory trading the execution-day move adds to the drift and the post-execution reversal exceeds the execution-day move; under slow-moving capital alone the pre-execution drift is gradual and the recovery slow. Table 4.

   H4 (incidence). On the execution date, losing funds that do not apply swing pricing show NAV returns below their benchmark by approximately c · r / (1 - r), where c is the realised impact cost on the sold portfolio and r the redeemed share; funds with swing pricing do not, because the concession is charged to the redeeming investor. Table 5.

   The cost-benefit calculation (Table 6) is not a hypothesis; it is arithmetic on H2 and H4.

Exhibits. Equations (1) and (2) in the text; the schematic price path as panel A of Figure 3; the four hypotheses as indented numbered blocks, as 2015 did with its propositions.

Trap. Deriving Greenwood (2005) or Duffie (2010). The second trap is hypotheses that no single table can refute; if a hypothesis needs two tables, split it or drop it.

## 4. Data (3 pages)

Purpose. Say where every number comes from, what was dropped and why, and what the exposure variable looks like before any regression.

What the winners did. 2015 and 2020 went source by source with N and period inside each paragraph. 2016 went variable by variable and put a correlation table after each factor. 2024 described the hand-coding protocol in enough detail to be repeated ("When no pronouns were used, we visually determined the gender"). 2021 organised data by hypothesis. All stated the filters with counts and the known defects of the data (Compustat exclusion bias, confidential series, a spliced source).

Sources list (verify access in the first week; the course slides say feasibility first).
- OMXSB: Nasdaq index review notices (announcement and effective dates, additions, deletions, weights); constituent histories via Nasdaq Global Index Watch or Refinitiv.
- Tracker assets and holdings: fund fact sheets and annual reports for the OMXSB trackers; Morningstar; Finansinspektionen's quarterly fund holdings reports.
- Stock prices, volume, shares outstanding, free float: Swedish House of Finance (FinBas), Refinitiv Eikon or Bloomberg; intraday from SHoF if the execution window is to be measured within the day.
- Swedish factor returns for abnormal returns: Swedish House of Finance factor data or Kenneth French's European factors as a fallback.
- FTN: procurement notices, award decisions, appeal rulings, fund agreements (FTN website and diary); the destination rule for non-choosers.
- Pensionsmyndigheten: saver letter dates, fund-level premium-pension capital and number of savers by month, execution dates, the fee-savings figure.
- Losing and winning fund holdings: Finansinspektionen quarterly holdings, fund half-year and annual reports (holdings at the last report before the letter and the first after execution).
- Fund daily NAV and benchmark: Morningstar, fund companies, Pensionsmyndigheten's fund list; benchmark named in the prospectus.
- Swing pricing: the fund's prospectus and fund rules (justerat fondandelsvärde), coded as a dummy with the date it was adopted.

Structure.
4.1 Reconstitution sample. 394 events, additions versus deletions by year, dates, tracker assets (Figure 2 panel A).
4.2 Procurement sample. Procurements, losing funds, redeemed share r_j, money moved, destination funds (Table 1 panel A).
4.3 The exposure variable. Expected net forced flow into stock i at execution e:

   (3) F_ie = - Σ_{j in losers} r_j A_j w_ij + Σ_{k in destinations} m_ke w_ik,

   where A_j is fund assets, w_ij the portfolio weight in the last holdings report before the saver letter, and m_ke the money arriving in destination fund k. Scaled: x_ie = F_ie / ADV_i, in days of volume. Report the distribution (Table 1 panel B) and the top ten stock-events. Construct a gross version (sales only) for robustness.
4.4 Summary statistics. Table 1: panel A procurements; panel B exposure x_ie (mean, median, p90, max, share above 0.1 days of ADV); panel C stock characteristics by exposure tercile.

Exhibits. Table 1 (three panels, one caption that defines every variable). Figure 2: panel A tracker assets and the OMXSB announcement-to-effective abnormal return by year (the "disappearing" picture); panel B the distribution of x_ie.

Trap. Stale holdings. If a losing fund sold down between the letter and execution, the holdings report before the letter overstates the execution-day sale, and the holdings report after execution understates it; use both and treat the difference as evidence on H3, not as noise. The second trap is overlap: an OMXSB reconstitution within 30 days of an FTN execution contaminates both samples; flag and exclude in robustness.

## 5. Methodology (2.5 pages)

Purpose. Specify the regressions once, with every scaling choice justified in a sentence, and state the power of the test before any result is shown.

What the winners did. 2020 wrote the anchor's specification, said what was added and why ("but include scaled creation and redemption flows as an additional control variable"), scaled returns by volatility and flows by ADV to handle heteroskedasticity across stocks, winsorised at 1 and 99 percent, and clustered by date. 2024 clustered by firm and cited Bertrand, Duflo and Mullainathan (2004) for the reason. 2016 reported two kinds of t-statistics in the same table. Nobody wrote a paragraph explaining OLS.

Structure.
5.1 Replication design (Greenwood and Sammon). For each addition and deletion: abnormal return on the announcement day, from announcement to effective day, on the effective day, and over the post-effective window (effective+1 to +20 and +21 to +60). Abnormal return from a market model estimated before the announcement; use a market index that excludes the stock. Regress the announcement-to-effective abnormal return on expected tracker demand scaled by ADV and on sub-period dummies:

   (4) CAR_i[ann, eff] = α + β · (D_i / ADV_i) + Σ_p γ_p · Period_p + ε_i,

   with D_i the tracker demand implied by assets times index weight. Copy the anchor's table layout exactly and say so.
5.2 Pressure measure and windows for FTN. Four windows fixed in advance and named in the hypotheses: award [a-1, a+1]; anticipation [letter, exec-1]; execution [exec-1, exec+1] or the days the fund's capital drops; reversal [exec+2, exec+20] and [exec+21, exec+60].

   (5) CAR_ie[w] / σ_i = α_w + β_w · x_ie + controls + ε_ie,

   returns scaled by the stock's daily volatility as in 2020, so β_w reads as volatility units per day of ADV; convert to basis points for the median stock in the text, as 2020 did in every paragraph.
5.3 Incidence regression at fund level.

   (6) (NAV return_je - benchmark return_je) = a + b · r_j/(1 - r_j) · (1 - Swing_j) + c · r_j/(1 - r_j) · Swing_j + ε_je,

   where b is the realised dilution rate; compare b with the stock-level c from eq (5) applied to the fund's portfolio.
5.4 Inference. The shock is by date, so cluster by execution date (as 2020 did), not by stock. With few execution dates, report wild-cluster bootstrap p-values beside the clustered t-statistics, and a Fama-MacBeth-style average across procurement rounds. State it once in the text and once in each caption.
5.5 Power. Before the results, give the minimum detectable effect at 5 percent size and 80 percent power:

   (7) MDE = (1.96 + 0.84) · σ_CAR / sqrt(N_eff),

   with σ_CAR the standard deviation of the window CAR among unexposed stocks and N_eff the effective number of independent observations after clustering. Put the number next to the square-root prediction from eq (2) for the median and the p90 exposure. If the MDE exceeds the predicted effect for the median stock, say so and restrict the headline test to high-exposure stock-events. No prior winner did this; the course asks for it.
5.6 Cost-benefit arithmetic.

   (8) Cost_e = Σ_j r_j A_j · c_je, with c_je the realised (from eq 5) or predicted (from eq 2) impact on fund j's sold portfolio; annualise across procurement rounds and divide into SEK 178m.

Exhibits. Equations (4) to (8) in the text; no table in this section.

Trap. Choosing the window after seeing the data. Fix the windows in section 3, use them in section 5, never add a fifth in section 6. The second trap is clustering by stock when 200 stock-events share five execution dates; the t-statistics will be two to three times too large.

## 6. Results (9 pages)

Purpose. One table per hypothesis, in the order of the hypotheses, each introduced by what it tests, read in two paragraphs with economic magnitudes, and benchmarked against the anchor's numbers in the text.

What the winners did. 2020 reported the anchor's specification first (Table IV column 1) and then its own (column 2) in the same table, and compared with Tuzun in a sentence. 2021 quoted Kyle and Obizhaeva's fitted lines beside its own. 2024 quoted Ahern and Dittmar's 3.56 percent and 1.9 percent in the paragraph reading each table. 2015 read each table with "Three things are of note here" and numbered them. 2016 used declarative table titles that state the finding.

6.1 Replication: the OMXSB index effect (2 pages). Table 2, panel A: average abnormal returns by window and sub-period for additions and deletions, with N per cell; panel B: regression (4). Text: compare the Swedish magnitude and trend with the anchor's US numbers, sentence by sentence. Say whether the effect scales with demand over ADV and whether it has fallen as tracker assets rose eightfold. If the Swedish effect did not disappear, say what in the anchor's decomposition (liquidity growth, migrations, anticipation) differs in Sweden; do not speculate beyond what Table 2 shows.

6.2 FTN price pressure and reversal (3.5 pages). Table 3: regression (5) for the execution window and the two reversal windows, columns with and without controls, gross and net exposure. Figure 3: panel A the schematic price path from section 3; panel B the average cumulative abnormal return in event time from award to exec+60 for the top exposure tercile against the bottom tercile, with 90 percent bands, execution date marked. Text: coefficient to basis points for the median and the p90 stock-event; fraction reversed by exec+20 and exec+60; comparison with the square-root prediction (eq 2) and with the index-effect magnitude from Table 2 at equal exposure.

6.3 Anticipation (1.5 pages, inside 6.2 if space is short). Table 4: the CAR decomposition by window (award, anticipation, execution, reversal) for high-exposure stock-events, with the share of the total move in each window, and the same decomposition using the holdings change between the pre-letter and post-execution reports as a direct measure of early selling by the losing fund. Text: which of the two stories in section 3 the pattern fits, and the sentence from 2020 to adapt: the thesis does not claim to fully disentangle predatory from slow-capital trading, only to measure the pre-execution share and its size relative to the execution-day move.

6.4 Fund-level incidence (2 pages). Table 5: regression (6), with the realised dilution rate b for funds without swing pricing, c for funds with it, and a column where the right-hand side is the stock-level prediction (c from Table 3 applied to the fund's sold portfolio). 6.5 Cost-benefit (1.5 pages). The second trap is burying the replication's disagreement with the anchor; if the Swedish index effect did not disappear, that is a finding and belongs in the abstract.

## 7. Robustness (1.5 pages)

Purpose. Answer the three objections an examiner will raise, and no more.

What the winners did. 2020 re-ran the impact figures with 40-day and 60-day windows and split market ups from downs. 2024 used a placebo country, an alternative outcome and a year-by-year reduced form. 2021 ran per-year regressions and reported that the slope failed in ten of eleven years rather than hiding it. 2015 used footnotes and "in results not shown" for the minor checks. None ran a page of unmotivated alternatives.

Exhibits. Table 7, one row per check, columns for the key coefficient from Table 3 and from Table 5:
1. Placebo execution dates (same stocks, dates shifted by one quarter, and random dates).
2. Exclusion of stock-events within 30 days of an OMXSB reconstitution.
3. Exposure from the pre-letter versus post-execution holdings report.
4. ADV window 20, 60, 120 days; abnormal return model market-adjusted, market model, four-factor.
5. Drop the largest procurement round; drop the largest stock-event; winsorise at 1 and 99 percent.
Remaining checks (gross versus net flow, clustering alternatives, index excluding the event stock) in one paragraph with "results available in Appendix Table A3".

Trap. The laundry list. Each row of Table 7 must correspond to a named threat; if the threat cannot be named in one clause, drop the row.

## 8. Conclusion (1.5 pages)

Purpose. What was found, in numbers; what it means for the two literatures; what the design cannot show; what a researcher should do next.

What the winners did. 2016: "Our results do not prove the validity of the model, but rather bring into question the validity of the standard benchmarks." 2020: "LETFs appear to have a negligible effect on 'normal' markets but may prove harmful to markets in crisis." 2021: "Kyle and Obizhaeva (2017) do not investigate causality between the two variables, and neither do we." 2024 stated the null first and kept the policy sentence conditional on the institutional features that produced it. 2015 closed on why rational investors should not treat the premium as a risk factor.

Structure. Paragraph 1: the three numbers (index effect trend, FTN pressure and reversal, incidence) and the cost against SEK 178m. Paragraph 2: what it says about pre-announced uninformed flows in a small market, and how it sits against Greenwood and Sammon. Paragraph 3: the limits, written before the examiner writes them: anticipation cannot be attributed to a specific trader type; the price concession is partly a transfer, so the cost-benefit is from the savers' side only; the number of execution dates is small, so the clustered inference is the binding constraint; the result may not scale to the larger procurements still to come. Paragraph 4, only if the stock-level result is null: state the minimum detectable effect and what sizes of impact the data rule out, then say what the fund-level and anticipation evidence still shows. Paragraph 5: two concrete next steps (intraday execution data; the next procurement rounds as an out-of-sample test).

Trap. Policy over-reach. The thesis measures a price concession on dated flows; it does not evaluate the procurement regime. Keep the recommendation to the execution mechanics (staggering, in-kind transfers, swing pricing), where the evidence actually bears.

## Exhibit checklist (what the finished thesis contains)

Tables: 1 summary statistics; 2 index-effect replication; 3 FTN price pressure and reversal; 4 anticipation decomposition; 5 fund-level incidence; 6 cost-benefit; 7 robustness. Appendix: A1 procurement calendar; A2 variable definitions; A3 additional checks.

Figures: 1 procurement timeline with one fund's capital; 2 tracker assets and index effect by year, plus exposure distribution; 3 schematic price path and event-time CAR by exposure tercile; 4 fund NAV excess return against redeemed share by swing-pricing status.

Caption rule, from 2015, 2016, 2020 and 2021: every caption restates the estimated equation, defines each variable, names the sample and period, and states the standard-error treatment. A caption is finished when the table can be understood without the text. Table titles may state the finding, as 2016 did ("Broker-Dealer Leverage is Pro-Cyclical, Primary Dealer Capital Ratio is not").

Equation rule: eight numbered equations in the main text, no more. Derivations go to a citation or, if original, to the appendix.
