**Anchor recommendation for the FTN thesis — researched 4 October 2026**

My first choice is Gordon Cookson, Tim Jenkinson, Howard Jones and Jose Vicente Martinez (2021), **“Best Buys and Own Brands: Investment Platforms’ Recommendations of Mutual Funds,” Review of Financial Studies 34(1), 227–263**. [Published article](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhaa057); [accepted manuscript downloaded for this review](sources/cookson_et_al_accepted_2019.pdf).

Keep Michael Klug’s Fondtorgsnämnden area. Narrow it to this question:

> **Does selection by Fondtorgsnämnden attract capital from investors outside the premium pension system, beyond the capital reallocation within that system?**

A suitable working title is *Beyond the Pension Menu: Public Fund Selection and Capital Flows Outside Sweden’s Premium Pension System*.

This is the strongest intellectual match among the candidates investigated. It is **not yet an unconditional, fully data-cleared commitment**. Public Swedish inputs have been downloaded and matched, including historical observations; broad monthly outside-PPM flow coverage depends on a licensed source whose availability to your group has not been demonstrated. The original UK data are confidential. Calling either issue solved would make the recommendation less reliable. The supporting [data audit](DATA_AUDIT.md) states exactly what was verified.

**Why this anchor fits the actual question**

Cookson et al. study how platform recommendations relate to fund selection, investor flows and subsequent performance. The central empirical flow analysis is their equation (2)/Table 6; Figure 1 describes additions and deletions. Their UK platform data distinguish recommendations from fund availability. That makes this a closer starting point for an FTN selection-and-flows question than a paper principally about whether retirement contributions react to past performance. The manuscript uses confidential information supplied in connection with an FCA study; an open article is not an open dataset. [FCA’s original research page](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/occasional-papers/occasional-paper-no-30).

The valuable extension is the **boundary of an intermediary’s influence**. A commercial platform recommends funds to its own customers. FTN selects funds for a public pension platform, with consequences for eligibility and capital allocation. Do investors outside that platform also respond? Outside investors are not mechanically reassigned by the PPM reform, although other intermediaries can still manage their allocations. Their response is therefore informative about effects beyond the pension menu.

That economic difference justifies Sweden. Geography alone does not. The contribution would concern whether public selection has an effect beyond the assets directly governed by it, and when that response occurs. It would not be a generic claim that Swedish mutual funds are different.

There are two related distinctions to exploit:

| Distinction | Economic question | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| PPM versus outside-PPM flows | Does the response extend beyond the platform subject to FTN selection? | Outside-PPM money is not necessarily Swedish household money or individually chosen money. |
| Award announcement versus admission/transfer | Does outside demand respond when selection becomes public, before implementation? | Announcements also change expected fees, scale, marketing and future eligibility. They are not pure information shocks. |

For this reason, the thesis should lead with **selection spillovers**. “Government certification” is a mechanism to investigate, not an identified causal effect to assume in the title. Public selection bundles information with other economic consequences. The best version of the thesis makes that distinction visible from the beginning.

**How this meets the assignment, and where it does not yet**

The original [course introduction](../../course_context/source_materials/Introduction_to_research_in_finance_Fall_2026_RS.pdf), especially slides 5 and 10–11, calls for a recent published top-journal paper, replication and a meaningful extension, a feasible dataset, and an answer to both what happens and why. It explicitly discourages significance chasing and an automatic Nordic application. Cookson meets the journal, recency and substantive-fit criteria.

The honest replication description is **a Swedish adaptation of the recommendation–flow analysis, followed by an extension to outside-platform demand and implementation timing**. It does not reproduce the original UK coefficients. Different data and applications are contemplated by the course, but that does not establish that any adapted regression satisfies its replication requirement. If literal original-data replication is required, Cookson fails that requirement without authorized access to the UK data. Resolve that precise interpretation with the tutor using the concrete mapping below; it is a substantive design issue, not a request for permission to do this research.

| Component | Proposed implementation | Necessary qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Core flow analysis, equation (2)/Table 6 | Estimate how fund flows differ around public selection, controlling for pre-selection performance and characteristics, with fund and calendar effects. Start with PPM flows to retain the platform setting. | Eligibility and compulsory transfers differ from UK recommendations. The coefficient is not the same economic parameter. |
| Event-time presentation, Figure 1 | Plot selected and comparison funds around award dates; mark implementation separately. | Do not label every change a response to new information. Procurement is anticipated and selection is endogenous. |
| Extension | Repeat for measured or carefully estimated outside-PPM flows; examine the period before implementation. | This is the main contribution, and requires credible outside-flow measurement. |
| Selection analysis | Describe ex ante winner/comparison differences and overlap. | Small samples and incomplete losing-bid scores make a large selection model or regression discontinuity unsuitable by default. |
| Subsequent performance | At most a modest descriptive secondary check on the earliest cohort. | Do not make long-horizon alpha or welfare a second thesis with the currently available post-period. |

In the original Table 6, percentage flows are scaled by the fund’s assets at the previous year-end. Preserve and document that convention in the closest baseline. For the extension, also report SEK flows and a predetermined pre-award asset denominator. Do not silently change denominators and call the coefficients identical. Do not borrow an “own-brand” interaction for a setting without own-brand variation.

**A tractable empirical design**

Use a fund-month panel if the licensed data pass the coverage check. A practical starting window is January 2022 through the latest complete month available at the analysis freeze, rather than 2001–2026. Earlier years can help sensitivity analysis, but introduce mergers, mapping changes and institutional changes without adding FTN treatment events. Prefer the 2024–2025 award cohorts for the main common-horizon analysis. Add 2026 cohorts only in windows they can actually support.

Construct a dated fund identity map first: PPM number, ISIN, share class, parent portfolio, manager, currency, mergers, award status, admission date and actual transfer dates. The unit should normally be the parent fund portfolio, with all PPM holdings in that portfolio aggregated once. A winner’s new institutional share class is not a newly born investment strategy. Keep failed and discontinued funds in historical coverage where possible.

The preferred comparison is unsuccessful but substantively evaluated bidders in the same procurement, provided identities and eligibility can be documented. The first Europe report makes the problem concrete: 35 submissions did not create 35 equally plausible candidates. It records 23 failing the initial requirements, then a withdrawal and an interview-threshold exclusion, leaving ten fully evaluated funds and six selected funds. Treating every unsuccessful submission as an equivalent control would create avoidable selection bias. The public report does not establish access to every losing score. [Original FTN report, sections 5.3–5.6](sources/ftn_europe_active_2024.pdf).

If qualified-loser identities cannot be established, use a **predefined same-category public-market comparison sample**, matched or weighted on pre-award size, fees, returns, risk and flows where observed. State that this changes the estimand and weakens causal interpretation. Funds outside the platform also need reliable histories; do not pretend that the PPM price file supplies prices for every non-PPM comparator. Use within-category comparisons and display overlap. Avoid selecting controls because their post-treatment trajectories look convenient.

For each procurement cohort, estimate event-time differences between selected and comparison funds, with fund effects and category-by-calendar-time effects. Use cohort-specific estimates or a carefully constructed stacked design; do not rely on a single staggered two-way-fixed-effects coefficient that can mix already-treated comparisons and heterogeneous effects. Prevent duplicated controls in stacked cohorts from receiving unintended weights. Predefine the weighting and aggregation rule.

Treat a comparison with unsuccessful bidders as a **relative selected-versus-unsuccessful response**. Losing itself can convey information or cause withdrawals, so such a coefficient is not an absolute selected-fund effect against an unaffected world. Broader category controls provide a useful second comparison, with their own selection problem. Competition can create spillovers to either control group.

For monthly data, a compact main estimand is cumulative outside-PPM net flow during the first three complete months after the announcement, relative to its stated counterfactual and normalized by pre-announcement assets. Separately estimate an announcement-to-implementation window only where actual dates leave enough observations. Display the announcement month separately: a March 25 decision has very little exposure in March. Common six- or twelve-month estimates should use only cohorts with that follow-up, with this restriction chosen before results.

Mechanism evidence should be specific:

- Outside flows changing before actual PPM transfers are harder to explain by direct compulsory transfers. They can still reflect marketing or anticipated changes in fund scale and fees.
- A response confined to PPM around transfers suggests that capital reallocation is concentrated within the affected platform. It does not by itself establish investor irrationality or absent informational value.
- An outside response accompanying unchanged observed retail charges is less easily explained by that particular fee change. It is not proof that every investor’s effective fee stayed fixed.
- An active-versus-passive difference could speak to evaluation uncertainty, but few independent procurement events make this optional exploratory evidence, not a required interaction with promised statistical power.

The global-active procurement’s reported appeal provides an interesting separation between award and implementation. It is not an instrument: appeals are not random and can change confidence in the award. Keep it a supplementary institutional check if status and follow-up permit. [Pensionsmyndigheten implementation status](https://www.pensionsmyndigheten.se/forsta-din-pension/valj-och-byt-fonder/upphandlat-fondtorg).

**What could invalidate the attractive story**

Winning is endogenous. FTN selects on characteristics that may also attract future flows, including information unavailable to the researcher. Fixed effects, observable controls, matching and flat-looking pre-trends do not eliminate this. The default claim should be a conditional differential response around selection, with causal language earned by additional evidence.

There are few independent procurement shocks. Eleven category awards on seven dates are not eleven unrelated large experiments, and thousands of fund-month observations do not change that. In the main 2024–2025 window there are seven categories on four dates. Fund-clustered errors alone can understate common procurement uncertainty. Report estimates by procurement, sensitivity to dropping each procurement/date, and inference that acknowledges the few groups. Neither a wild bootstrap nor permutation labels automatically fix the problem; permutation requires a defensible exchangeability assumption.

Outside flows are a subtraction of quantities that may be noisy or differently scoped. Provider-estimated total flow minus observed PPM trading does not become an exact observed outside flow. Check both components against asset identities and known transfers. All-currency parent assets, one SEK share-class return and one PPM class are not automatically compatible.

Longer pre-periods improve diagnostics but cannot manufacture more treatment events. Set a minimum economically meaningful flow response before estimating results, calculate uncertainty and detectable effects from pre-event variation under an explicit dependence structure, and show whether the design can distinguish meaningful alternatives. A wide confidence interval is an inconclusive result. A tightly estimated small effect is informative. Neither should cause a switch from flows to survival after looking at results.

**What I would change in the current PLAN**

1. Replace SSZ as the primary intellectual anchor with Cookson; retain SSZ for the pension allocation channel.
2. Replace “certification effect” as a presumed estimand with outside-platform selection spillovers, then specify evidence bearing on certification.
3. Replace the broad fee–supply–flow–performance programme with one principal outside-flow question and a small number of linked diagnostics.
4. Separate award, platform admission, saver notification and actual transfer dates. “Selectable in June” is not proof that all transfers happened in June.
5. Remove the assumption that all decision-month flows are sponsor decisions and all other flows are participant decisions. Aggregate monthly observations do not identify those actors.
6. Specify common currency, date, portfolio scope and predetermined denominators before subtracting flow components. Do not subtract unrelated percentage flow rates.
7. Replace “no academic study exists” with a bounded contribution claim. The search did not establish exhaustive absence of related work.
8. Remove a result-dependent switch to fund survival and claims that a null outside response proves a particular equilibrium model.
9. Replace a fixed table-count recipe with exhibits that each answer a necessary question. The winner audit does not support a magic number of tables.

**What would make this ready to commit**

The most important next milestone is not another literature search. It is a small, reproducible licensed-data export covering selected and comparison funds before and after at least two early procurement decisions. It must establish parent/share-class identities, historical assets, returns and estimated flows, and permit a same-scope PPM reconciliation. Assemble the full historical coverage matrix before committing to cohort counts; use the public FI files as an independent cross-check.

The data review found a real public route, but the cleanest single-class subset contains only seven procured funds in the June 2026 snapshot. That cannot presently be sold as a statistically adequate substitute for the broader monthly panel. If broader coverage is unavailable, retain FTN as the area but narrow the inferential ambition before choosing the final empirical question. Do not simply relabel the same weak data as a different anchor.

I would commit to this anchor once the monthly export and the course’s replication interpretation are settled. It has the clearest connection between literature, institutional difference, measurable outcome and economically meaningful extension among the papers reviewed. That is a defensible reason to choose it; a guarantee of perfection, causality or an award would not be.
