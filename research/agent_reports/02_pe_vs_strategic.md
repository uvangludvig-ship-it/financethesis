# Agent 2: PE vs strategic buyers — key takeaways
Core verified papers: Gorbenko & Malenko 2014 JF 69(6) 2513-2555; Bargeron et al 2008 JFE 89(3) 375-390; Martos-Vila, Rhodes-Kropf & Harford 2019 JFQA 54(6) 2635-2661; Axelson, Jenkinson, Strömberg & Weisbach 2013 JF 68(6) 2223-2267; Haddad, Loualiche & Plosser 2017 JF 72(1) 371-414; Malenko & Malenko 2015 JFE 117(3) 607-627; Boucly, Sraer & Thesmar 2011 JFE 102(2) 432-453; Davis et al 2014 AER 104(12) 3956-3990; Cohn, Hotchkiss & Towery 2022 RoF 26(2) 257-285; Bernstein, Lerner & Mezzanotti 2019 RFS 32(4) 1309-1373; Erel, Jang & Weisbach 2015 JF 70(1) 289-328 (European private targets, Amadeus); Eliason et al 2020 QJE 135(1) 221-267 (roll-ups dialysis); Olsson & Tåg 2025 JF 80(4) 2107-2151 (Swedish registers); Antoni, Maug & Obernberger 2019 JFE 133(3) 634-657 (German buyouts).
Gaps: no core paper on PE vs strategic pricing after 2022 rate shock (BIS WP Avalos et al 2026 ends 2020); no core paper on add-on pricing; European SME lit compares PE vs no deal, never PE vs strategic on same population; no Nordic study combining CapIQ price + Serrano post-deal accounts. Prior art: Kärnä & Myers 2025 Econ Letters (4000 Swedish LBOs, Serrano+PitchBook DiD).
Top-3: (1) Rate shock 2022 DiD PE vs strategic multiples (Axelson x Gorbenko-Malenko) — risk: selection/missing multiples (only ~3% have EV/EBITDA). (2) Add-on pricing + Serrano outcomes (Eliason/Boucly). (3) PE-owned Swedish SMEs in 2022-23 rate shock (BLM 2019 x Cohn et al 2022) — safest, Serrano only; novelty vs Kärnä & Myers must come from shock design + PE/strategic/independent triple comparison.
