## D. Ranking — top 8 with mechanism, hypotheses and informative null

**1. Norway's aquaculture resource-rent tax (Sep 2022 → May 2023), Σ=9.** The cleanest Nordic firm-level shock of the period: a surprise 28 Sep 2022 proposal (40 %), diluted to 25 % (bill 28 Mar 2023, adopted 31 May 2023), applied retroactively from 1 Jan 2023. Treatment intensity is continuous — share of group volume from Norwegian sea-based licences (Mowi ~45 %, SalMar/Lerøy/Grieg higher, Bakkafrost 0 % Norway) — and the three dates give three event windows with opposite signs (shock, dilution, adoption). Mechanism: a pure rent tax should be capitalised (H1: abnormal return ≈ −PV of tax share × Norwegian-rent share, no change in investment); a distortionary tax (H2: bunnfradrag, normprice design, land-based exemption) predicts cuts in Norwegian capex/smolt investment and reallocation to Faroese/Icelandic/land-based capacity in Capital IQ segment data 2023–25. The null of "capitalisation without real effects" is informative because it disciplines the industry's lobbying claim. Main risk: ~8 treated firms, so inference must lean on cross-sectional intensity and on LSEG salmon forward prices as a control; retroactivity means the "effective date" is not an event. A Swedish comparison does not help; Faroese/Icelandic peers are the control.

**2. Staggered Nordic FDI-screening acts (DK 1 Jul 2021; SE 1 Dec 2023; FI/NO tightening), Σ=9.** Frattaroli 2020 JFE (France's 2014 decree) and Dinc & Erel 2013 JF supply the design: targets in "sensitive" sectors vs others, before/after each act, in Capital IQ deals (foreign-acquirer share, premia, time-to-close, withdrawals) and FinBas prices (do sensitive-sector valuations fall when takeover-premium expectations drop?). H1: screening deters foreign bidders → fewer cross-border deals and lower premia in sensitive sectors; H2 (entrenchment, Frattaroli): managers in protected firms reduce investment/raise pay. The informative null: with very few formal blocks, no measurable chilling effect would itself be a finding relevant to the ongoing EU screening revision. Risk: sector classification is judgment-heavy, deal counts per country-sector-year are small, and Denmark's 2021 date is pre-window (the Swedish 2023 act is what puts it inside 2022–26). A Swedish comparison is not just helpful — the Nordic stagger *is* the identification.

**3. Norwegian wealth-tax hikes, owner exodus and the 20 Mar 2024 exit tax, Σ=8.** Norway raised the wealth tax to 1.0/1.1 % and cut the share valuation discount, then closed the escape route with a tightened exit tax (latent gains > NOK 3 m, 12-year instalments). Roughly 80 large owners moved to Switzerland 2022–24, traceable via media lists and the open shareholder register. Two designs are feasible with Holdings and Capital IQ: (a) payout: wealth-tax-liable Norwegian individual owners need dividends to pay the tax (H1: dividend/earnings rises with domestic-individual ownership share after each hike; Jacob & Michaely 2017 RFS, Jakobsen et al 2020 QJE), vs. H2 that firms instead lever up or owners emigrate; (b) the 20 Mar 2024 exit-tax deadline as a bunching event in emigration/ownership transfers. Ring 2024 REStud (Norwegian assessment discontinuities) and Kleven et al 2013 AER / Moretti & Wilson 2017 AER anchor the migration channel. Risk: endogenous migration, manual owner–firm matching, small listed N (private-firm coverage in Capital IQ is patchy). A Swedish comparison strengthens it: Sweden abolished its wealth tax in 2007, so Swedish-listed firms with comparable ownership are an untreated control.

**4. Novo Nordisk as a one-stock market (20 Dec 2024 CagriSema; 2025 warnings), Σ=8.** Novo's market cap exceeded Danish GDP in 2023; on Novo-specific bad-news days home-biased Danish pension funds and OMXC trackers rebalance mechanically. Design: abnormal returns and volume of other Copenhagen stocks on Novo-only news days, scaled by each stock's passive/index ownership (Holdings, Capital IQ), with Swedish and Finnish stocks as placebo. H1 (non-fundamental comovement/flow pressure; Chang, Hong & Liskovich 2015 RFS; Barberis, Shleifer & Wurgler 2005 JFE) predicts negative co-returns that reverse; H2 (information: Danish macro exposure to Novo) predicts persistent effects concentrated in Novo suppliers. A no-spillover null speaks to the "Danish Dutch disease" debate. Risk: single stock; "Novo-only" days need a defensible rule (|return| > 5 %, no macro release). Fully doable in FinBas.

**5. GPFG exclusions and observations (~60 decisions 2022–26), Σ=7.** Hong & Kacperczyk 2009 JFE, Berk & van Binsbergen 2025 JFE and Heath et al 2023 RoF frame the question: does divestment by the world's largest fund move prices or behaviour? Because NBIM divests *before* announcing, the announcement return isolates the stigma/information channel (H1: negative AR, ownership shifts in Capital IQ), while the flow channel (H2) must show up as abnormal volume in the weeks before. A zero at announcement plus a pre-announcement volume signature is itself informative. Risk: existing working papers; treated firms mostly non-Nordic; the Nov 2025 governance break shrinks the sample. Swedish comparison neutral.

**6. Danish callable-bond prepayment wave 2022–23, Σ=7.** When rates rose, Danish households could buy back their 0.5–1 % callable mortgage bonds at 60–80 % of par — the mirror image of the refinancing inaction in Andersen, Campbell, Nielsen & Ramadorai 2020 AER 110. SSE lacks the household register, but Nasdaq Copenhagen publishes quarterly extraordinary prepayments per ISIN and LSEG carries bond prices, allowing a bond-level study: which series (coupon, vintage, discount, issuer) saw the fastest buy-backs, and did prepayment respond to price discounts (H1: attentive, option-exercise-like behaviour) or to salient media/advisor waves (H2: lumpy, herding)? The informative null is that Danish borrowers were as inert in 2022 as in Andersen et al's 2010s sample. Risk: ISIN-level data assembly is heavy and bond series map coarsely to borrower types. No Swedish analogue — Sweden's non-callable covered bonds are the institutional contrast, not a control.

**7. Norwegian dividend-tax step-ups (uplift factor to 1.60 in 2022 and 1.72 from 6 Oct 2022) — Σ=7.** Norway raised the effective dividend tax on individuals twice within a year, the second time with immediate effect to pre-empt payout rushes. With Holdings ownership data one can test the Jacob & Michaely 2017 RFS clientele prediction across Norwegian firms sorted on domestic-individual ownership, using Swedish firms as untreated controls: H1 dividends fall/buybacks rise with individual ownership after the hike; H2 (agency-muting) no response in widely held firms. Ex-dividend-day price drops in FinBas provide a second, price-based test (Michaely 1991 JF). Risk: rate changes are small (2–3 pp) and the wealth-tax "dividend need" pulls the other way, making a null hard to interpret; uplift factors still need verification.

**8. Nordic bank-tax differences (SE riskskatt 2022; DK financial-sector corporate surcharge 2023/24; NO finansskatt), Σ=7.** Schepens 2016 JFE and Heider & Ljungqvist 2015 JFE anchor pass-through of bank taxes to lending spreads and capital structure. Nordic listed banks (~45 incl. ~20 Norwegian savings-bank equity certificates) form a rare multi-country panel with staggered taxes; Capital IQ bank financials and FinBas prices allow both pass-through (net interest margin, loan growth) and announcement-return tests. H1: pass-through to borrowers (margins up, valuations unchanged); H2: incidence on shareholders (valuation drop, no margin change). Risk: Danish surcharge dates unverified here; the 2022–24 rate cycle is a dominant confound; N small. Cross-country comparison is the design.

## E. Discards and cautions
- Utlånsforskriften LTV 90 % (1 Jan 2025) vs Sweden's 2026 LTV law is a neat stagger but has no loan-level data at SSE (SSB/Norges Bank aggregates only).
- Finnish osakesäästötili cap raise (2024) and Norway's 2023–24 extra employer contribution: real reforms, but treated populations small and unobservable in SSE data.
- Iceland, RE bond crisis, green bonds, Euronext Oslo: see table rows 12–15; covered by reports 06/22 or not identifiable.
- Verification gaps to close before the synopsis: Danish screening act date/thresholds; Danish financial-sector surcharge; Norwegian dividend uplift factors; Nasdaq Copenhagen prepayment data access.
