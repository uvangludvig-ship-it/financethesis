# Conclusions: what the 25 winners do that the 33 non-winners don't

Prepared 2 October 2026. Every concluding section of the 25 awarded SSE finance theses and of the 33-thesis non-winner sample used in `winners_vs_nonwinners_analysis.md` was read in full, plus the end of the discussion where the conclusion was short. Companion to that document; this one looks only at the conclusion.

## Summary statistics

| | Winners (n=25) | Non-winners (n=33) |
|---|---|---|
| Headline result with numbers in the conclusion | 12/25 (48%) | 8/33 (24%) |
| Anchor paper compared by name in the conclusion | 16/25 (64%); 24/25 counting the adjoining discussion | 20/33 (61%); 10 name no anchor at all |
| Mechanism or institutional explanation offered | 20/25 (80%) | 19/33 (58%); five more offer only speculation |
| Concrete limitations | 15/25 (60%) | 23/33 (70%), five of them boilerplate |
| "Future research" paragraph | 22/25 (88%) | 31/33 (94%) |
| Policy or practice implication | 13/25 (52%), mostly regulator- or model-directed | 16/33 (48%), mostly investor advice |
| Question restated | 17/25 (68%) | 27/33 (82%) |
| Median length of the conclusion proper | about 460 words (170 to 1,200) | about 470 words (90 to 1,420) |
| Clear over-claiming | 4/25 (16%) | 19/33 (58%) |
| Over-claiming including mild | 9/25 (36%) | 28/33 (85%) |
| Under-claiming | 3/25 (12%), all mild | 3/33 (9%) |
| Tone matches the tables | 13/25 (52%) | 2/33 clean, 5 borderline |

What does not discriminate: length, restating the question, naming an anchor, having a limitations section, having a future-research paragraph. Non-winners do all of these at the same or higher rates. What discriminates: numbers in the conclusion (twice as common), a mechanism that is argued rather than listed, and above all calibration. The non-winner over-claim rate is 3.5 times the winner rate.

Failure modes counted across the non-winners:

- Insignificant results narrated as "tendencies", "seems", "observably": 7 theses. Among winners, one (2019 value premium, "goes in the right direction").
- Causal verbs after a correlational design: 9. Among winners: 2022 ownership, and softer in 2018 intergroup bias.
- "More research is needed" in place of an explanation: 6. Among winners: none that substitute; the 2011 short-sale thesis asks for more studies after naming endogeneity as the reason.
- Conclusion contradicts the tables or itself: 5. Among winners: 2019 value premium.
- A claim introduced that was never tested: 5 non-winners, and 3 winners (2013 rumours on insider trading, 2021 Covid on the Swedish strategy, 2018 intergroup on "social loss").

## What winning conclusions do

**(a) Put the coefficient and the anchor's coefficient in the same sentence.**
- 2011 Norway quota: "These cumulative abnormal returns are rather big, reaching -2.71 percent without any adjustments, -2.43 percent adjusting for exchange rate effects and -1.40 percent when excluding outliers. This result is in line with the research by Ahern and Dittmar (2009) who obtained a mean three day cumulative abnormal return of -2.6 percent".
- 2016 EPU and CDS: "First, we confirm the findings of Wisniewski (2015), i.e., that changes in levels of EPU indeed has a widening effect on the CDS spreads. Secondly, after controlling for other market effects, we show that the magnitude of this effect is about 0.4 % the same month and 0.7 % the following month."
- 2020 bank stocks: "The pre-crisis results conform with the original study by English et al. (2018) but are weaker, which is to be expected considering our use of daily data."

**(b) Put the identification problem inside the claim sentence, not in a separate limitations list.**
- 2011 short-sale bans: "However, graphical results and various subsample re-estimations indicate that the adoption of the bans is endogenous. Hence, it is hard to assign the observed effects to an attempt by short sellers to circumvent the bans."
- 2019 filing lag: "The relationship between filing lag and Tobin's q is empirically proven, but the mechanism through which filing lag affects firm performance is only theoretical. ... It is possible that filing lag is correlated with a variable, that is unaccounted for, that affects Tobin's q."
- 2017 unbundled costs: "These results are by no means suggested to be causal ... There is no claim to causality of such characteristics."

**(c) Say what the result does not mean.**
- 2016 intermediary asset pricing: "the AEM model's outperformance does not suggest that the AEM model is correct in its configuration, but only that the CAPM and Fama-French three factor models are questionable benchmarks."
- 2013 French FTT: "The results of our study should not be seen as a reliable augur of what the wider European FTT experience would be like; the differences in design are vast."

**(d) Rank candidate explanations and report having tested at least one.**
- 2011 Norway quota lists six explanations and kills one: "However, we tested for three year lagged effects in our regressions but we were still not able to find that the Norwegian gender quota law had any effect on the Norwegian companies' financial measures, which opposes the theory of lagged effects."
- 2024 French quota: "We argue that our results showed no adverse effects of the French gender quota as firms did not have to rush to fulfill the quota but could adjust smoothly to the new legislation, as shown by the data. In contrast to the setting for Norwegian firms, which had two years to fulfill the mandated quota, French firms had six years."

**(e) Report what was not found alongside what was.**
- 2020 bank stocks: "When bank characteristics were interacted with these monetary shocks some problems arose. Coefficients for the level and slope surprise flipped in the pre- and post-crisis periods compared to their respective baseline regressions".
- 2022 crypto: "We validate the invariant relationship for six out of 16 cryptocurrencies along the time series dimension ... Along the intraday dimension for individual cryptocurrencies, we strongly reject the invariant relationship."

**(f) Give a counterfactual magnitude or a residual with a number attached.**
- 2018 BEV parking: "Using counterfactual simulations we estimate that the change in parking policy led to a 2.85% reduction in total BEV sales in 2017 ... Had all 418 municipalities chosen to charge BEVs for parking we estimate that the sales would have decreased by 7.80%".
- 2017 muni puzzle: "A constant in the region of ~20 basis points also opens up for discussions regarding thus far potentially overlooked variables, including investor attention."

**(g) Derive a directional policy statement from the sign of the coefficient.**
- 2012 tick size: "Based on this study an increased tick size would thus not be recommended as a remedy for the increased volatility."
- 2015 fund flow risk: "it would be inappropriate to do so if the purpose of the model is to estimate the required rate of return on an investment opportunity."

**(h) Future research is a named next experiment, dataset or model, not a wish.**
- 2013 French FTT: "Most recent is the STT that was implemented on Italian markets on March 1st, 2013, soon to be bolstered by an HFTT and a derivatives tax on July 1st, 2013."
- 2018 BEV parking: "In late 2017 the Norwegian government announced that the BEV parking policy would be changed again ... it would be highly interesting to investigate the effect of the second policy change."

Winners that break the pattern: 2019 value premium reads insignificant alphas as directional ("Although both alphas are statistically insignificant ... we see that the inclusion of our disaster risk proxy goes in the right direction"); 2021 Covid asserts "the renowned Swedish Covid-19 strategy has been beneficial for some Swedish stocks"; 2022 ownership closes a 170-word conclusion with "it is the joint effect of ownership concentration and equity identity that impacts firm value" on cross-sectional OLS with 10% stars; 2018 intergroup bias concludes "university belongingness can serve as a predictor of access to venture funding" after its own discussion says larger samples are needed.

## What non-winning conclusions do

**(a) Narrate an insignificant coefficient as a tendency, then act on it.**
- 4088: "Even if the regressions do not present significant results, we find tendencies that support the glass cliff in CEO positions."
- 5888: "We find that there is a negative correlation and that during crises there seems to be less underpricing ... however our findings are not statistically significant." Two paragraphs later: "Issuers undergoing an IPO based on our results should not fear underpricing as much during crises".
- 6581, on a 54.2% win rate: "only slightly above the random benchmark of 50%. However, it indicates that the strategy ... captures a weak but systematic tendency".

**(b) Use causal verbs on group comparisons and OLS.**
- 4110: "this study finds that sin-stock performance differs between countries due to differences in welfare, consumption, and religious denomination."
- 5314: "IPOs within a sector leads to increased uncertainty and reduced prices for its peers".

**(c) Hand the explanation to future research.**
- 4131: "we fail to either reject or support our second hypothesis and thus conclude that more research is needed in this field of study."
- 6581: "Most importantly, this thesis does not attempt to explain why overreactions arise in crude oil prices."

**(d) Present an untested claim as a finding.**
- 6013: "We find that the regulation of spinning through the COBS 11A.1.6 rule and the mandatory adoption of the IFRS have increased trust between underwriters, issuers, and investors in the IPO process." No test of regulation appears in the paper.
- 5885: "Instead, we find support for market inefficiencies, driven by investor biases."

**(e) End with investment advice as the payoff.**
- 4251: "for profit maximizing investors the best thing is to refrain from using any of the trading strategies and instead only hold the Unsustainable portfolio."
- 6003: "we conclude that sports betting can indeed be a profitable asset for an investor of the correct disposition."

**(f) Muddle the hypothesis logic or undermine the paper's own result.**
- 4130, after stating that no causality can be identified: "we conclude that our second hypothesis saying that 'Dialogues generate a positive impact ...' cannot be rejected."
- 4088: "the results of our study might be the way they are by coincidence or that they are inaccurate."

**(g) Close with a flourish unmoored from the tables.**
- 4110: "One might wonder: what norms and values are going to influence the stock market of tomorrow?"
- 5202: "even early-stage sustainability frameworks generate market movements." The Wilcoxon test did not confirm it.

**(h) The opposite failure: disclaim the result entirely.**
- 4440's whole 87-word conclusion: "This study supports that green bonds do not have a statistically significant different issue price ... this result cannot by itself be interpreted as showing the non-existence of a green premium, nor can it be interpreted as showing how or if a green bond label affects the required yield of bonds."

Non-winners that read like winners in the conclusion: 6208 ("our results support the presence of information asymmetry in the IPO market but does not support that the pandemic underpricing spike can be attributed to heightened informational frictions") and 4694. Both lack numbers in the conclusion. 6812 and 6365 are well calibrated in their limitations sections but let the implications run ahead of the power of the tests.

## Null results: how winners conclude them

**2011 Norway quota.** The null is stated flatly, confirmed against named prior work, then six explanations are ranked and one is reported as tested: "From studying the regression results we can conclude that there are no statistically significant changes in the financial measures for Norwegian firms after the introduction of the gender quota requirement. ... This result is in line with previous research from Rose (2007) ... We believe there to be several possible explanations ... However, we tested for three year lagged effects ... which opposes the theory of lagged effects." A conflicting result is called what it is: "is confusing."

**2023 FOMC cycle.** The null is a change in the data, attributed to two measured institutional shifts, and the competing explanation is confronted with its own prediction: "We show that the previously documented pattern ... does not persist in the post-sample period 2017 to 2022. ... We document that the two factors, timing of Board of Governors meetings and proportion of Fed funds rate increases versus decreases, have changed in the post-sample period. When controlling for both ... we find that the effect on daily excess return is consistent over both sample periods." And: "we are yet unable to confidently determine whether the pattern is a pure anomaly ... or if the pattern contains a risk parameter."

**2024 French quota.** Every null is listed, then a mechanism anchored in two design differences and a measured non-result: "we find no support for the French board gender quota impacting firm value negatively. ... We found that board size remained roughly constant ... Board tenure was also not significantly impacted ... This likely points to the new female directors replacing incumbent male board members ... French firms had six years. Also, the French board gender quota had a softer punishment for compliance failure than in Norway". Its one weakness is the last sentence, which turns a failure to reject into a positive: "a gender quota does not need to negatively affect firm value".

**2011 short-sale bans.** A significant effect the authors decline to interpret: "we find that the short sale bans are associated with a statistically and economically significant increase in option trading volume ... However, graphical results and various subsample re-estimations indicate that the adoption of the bans is endogenous. Hence, it is hard to assign the observed effects to an attempt by short sellers to circumvent the bans." The verb throughout is "associated with", and the call for further research comes after the reason has been named.

**Contrast, non-winners with nulls.** 4088 reads insignificant coefficients as support, offers a Hofstede explanation it cannot test, and undercuts its own result. 5232 has no conclusion at all; its discussion is careful but the paper dissolves into limitations and never states what it found. 5888 describes the null correctly and then issues advice from it. 4130 converts both results into hypothesis boilerplate ("cannot be rejected" for a positive claim after stating no causality can be identified). 6812 is closest to the winners, but a low-powered null becomes evidence that regulation works: "green window dressing is not a universal phenomenon but instead depends on regulatory pressure". The winners' nulls rest their mechanism on something measured (lagged tests, board size and tenure, transition length); 6812's rests on the absence of a result it says it lacked power to detect.

## The single biggest difference

It is not structure. The difference is calibration: whether the sentence that states the finding carries the same weight as the table it comes from. Winners state the coefficient, state the anchor's coefficient, and state the identification problem in the same breath ("associated with", "hard to assign", "only theoretical", "does not suggest ... but only that"), and when the result is null they rank explanations and say which one they tested. Non-winners either call weak results tendencies and build advice on them, or assert a causal mechanism the design cannot support and defer the explanation to future research. A winning conclusion is the tables rewritten as prose; a non-winning conclusion is the introduction rewritten as if the tables had cooperated.
