Should You Trust Reddit with Your Money? — A Machine Learning Approach
to Estimating Stock Returns

Abstract:
We replicate the results of Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning by Gu, Kelly,
and Xiu (2020), and make an extension by using a daily interval instead of a monthly.
Furthermore, we make an extension of the topic by generating data from wallstreetbets,
in order to investigate whether there is explanatory value of the subreddit to predict
stock returns. The activity and sentiment attributable to different companies on
wallstreetbets are analyzed and used in the prediction. Boosted decision trees and
artificial neural networks are modelled and evaluated based on root mean square error
       2
and R and compared between same day returns and next day returns, as well as with
and without the reddit data in order to determine the explanatory value of it. We find
some explanatory value of the wallstreetbets posts for the same day returns, however no
predictive value for the next day returns, in line with the Efficient market-hypothesis.
Keywords:

1 Introduction L. Hedberg & O. Ribberheim

1 Introduction

Asset pricing is the act of estimating the risk premium of an asset, and can be estimated
by using various data of a stock. The risk premium of an asset is the surplus yield of
the asset above the risk-free rate, and acts as a compensation for the risk the investor
is taking when investing in a stock compared to the risk free rate. The risk premium is
important when valuing financial assets. The stock market can be extremely lucrative
for investors, if navigated correctly. Risk premiums are however hard to measure, since
unpredictable shocks from the market affect them and make it problematic to distinguish
the pure value of the asset from outside influence.

The subforum wallstreetbets on the social news website reddit caused the long-time poorly
performing stock of Gamestop to skyrocket in January 2021. (TradeNews.com, 2021) Be-
fore, hedge funds such as Melvin Capital started to short sell the Gamestop stocks, lending
stocks and selling them, in hope that their price would decline even more, so that they
could be rebought for a lesser price and the hedge fund would profit from the difference. In
reaction to the short selling, the people on wallstreetbets started propagating for purchas-
ing large volumes of the stock, causing the stock to increase its value. The value increase
of the stock forced the hedge funds to buy back the stock to minimize their loss while
the underwriters of options on gamestop had to buy stocks to delta hedge their positions,
known as a “gamma squeeze”. This in turn caused the value of the stock to increase even
more. The stock reached its peak on January 27th 2021, with an adjusted closing price of
$347,51, having increased by 1553% compared to its value on 28th December, 2020 of $21.
(Yahoo Finance, 2021) Simultaneously, Melvin Capital lost roughly $4.5 billions during
January 2021, largely because of this, going from assets of $12.5 billions to assets of $8
billions. (BusinessInsider.com, 2021) This event revealed the potential power of individ-
ual investors. Individual investors, also known as retail investors, are a large segment of
securities trading, with a significant impact on the stock market and the sentiment of it.
(Investopedia.com, 2021) Retail investors today make up 20% of equity volume trades in
the USA, for example. (Financial Times, 2021) We seek to investigate to which extent the
sentiment analysis of the posts and comments on wallstreetbets can be used to determine
and foresee the risk premium of stocks, and whether the data from the social news website
could be used in addition to other features to improve the measurement of equity risk
premiums.

We seek to partially replicate Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) by contributing to a machine
learning toolbox in Python which estimates the equity risk premiums of stocks. We
also add the extension of using daily data instead of, as used by Gu, Kelly, and Xiu
(2020), monthly data from the Compustat database, provided by S&P global market
intelligence. In our replication, we use a selection of the features deemed most important in
the paper, of which the most important groups are recent price trends, liquidity variables,

and volatility variables. Some of the features included in our toolbox are short-term
reversal, stock momentum, turnover, total and idiosyncratic return volatility, earnings-
to-price, and asset growth. Our replication investigates if using daily, instead of monthly,
data improves the measurement of the risk premiums as done by Gu, Kelly, and Xiu
(2020).

We seek to contribute to the literature twofold, firstly we aim to contribute by extract-
ing and analyzing posts and comments from wallstreetbets through neural networks and
boosted decision trees to inquire if this sentiment and API feature has a predictive value
for the stocks of the companies mentioned in the posts. Secondly, by replicating Gu,
Kelly, and Xiu (2020) with modifications, with the extension of using daily data instead
of monthly to investigate whether using more frequent data improves the measurements
of risk premiums. The authors find that using monthly data yielded better predictions
than when using annual data, and it is of interest to further see if daily data yields better
results than monthly data.

We use data from the time period 1980 to 2019 in our replication of the toolbox by
Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020). The data is the from Center for Research in Security Prices
(CRSP), which has been accessed through Wharton Research Data Services (WRDS).

For our analysis with wallstreetbets, we built and used a script to scrape the comments
and posts from the subreddit from its beginning in 2012 to the end of 2020. We then
analyzed them and divided them into groups of sentiments connected to companies, which
we then analyzed through boosted decision trees and neural networks.

Our results show that there is explanatory value in the wallstreetbets posts for the same
day stock returns, but no predictive value for foreseeing the results of the next day. The
replication of Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) succeeded on monthly data, having a considerable
higher return than the benchmark of equally weighted positions in each stock, whereas the
extension with daily data had low explanatory value, and performed close to the equally
weighted portfolio.

In section 5, the empirical results, in section 6, our discussion and finally our conclusion

2 Literature Review L. Hedberg & O. Ribberheim

2 Literature Review

The first significant model of asset pricing, The Capital Asset Pricing Model (CAPM),
was introduced by Sharpe (1964), Lintner (1965), and Mossin (1966) and is still widely
used. The CAPM obtains the expected rate of return from the risk-free rate, the beta,
and the market risk premium. Since the 1960s, substantial research has been performed
on asset pricing, and produced improved and more advanced models for asset pricing.

Empirical Asset Pricing via Machine Learning by Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) analyzes
machine learning methods for valuing assets. The study shows that machine learning
algorithms, especially neural networks and trees, can be useful for predicting and mea-
suring equity risk premiums, and prove more successful than popular regression methods.
Furthermore, the authors identify the most successful predictor variables for asset pricing:
variations of liquidity, momentum, and volatility. We replicate this paper by using the
most important predictor variables, but we use daily data instead of monthly data. It is
of interest to see whether this improves the prediction, since Gu, Kelly, and Xiu find that
monthly data improved the prediction compared to yearly data. However, it is possible
that using daily data might include more noise and interfere more with our predictions,
making it worse than using monthly data.

Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001) collect data from the online forum RagingBull.com, which
analyzes finance. The authors analyze frequency and sentiment activity for companies
on the forum together with abnormal stock trading volumes and stock prices, through
regressions. It is concluded that investor opinion changes are correlated with unusual
industry-adjusted returns and unusually high trading volumes on days with unusually
high post activity in the forum. Tumarkin and Whitelaw find no evidence of forum posts
affecting the stock returns or trading volume, however they suspect a reverse causality
between the two, which is in accordance with market efficiency. It is of interest to see
whether our study as well produces results which are in line with the Efficient-market
hypothesis, as defined by Fama et al (1969). According to this hypothesis, the price of
the stock always represents all the information that is available. Therefore, abnormal
returns would be unattainable since returns would not be affected by for example activity
on internet forums.

Yang, Zhu, Cheng (2020) perform a similar investigation, and find results that indicate
that prediction of trading volumes is possible through analyzing the activity on a stock
forum, which, on the contrary to Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001), is not in line with the
Efficient Market-hypothesis. Yang, Zhu, Cheng (2020) use data from China’s largest stock
message board (Eastmoney.com), and create a “Sentiment index" and a “Panic index” to
represent the emotions of investors on the forum. Yang, Zhu, and Cheng (2020), unlike
Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001), find through regression models that you can predict

trading volumes through analyzing the board; that a high “Sentiment Index" of a stock
causes abnormal trading volumes, and that the “Panic Index” could help foresee a stock

We investigate whether the activity, analyzed by sentiment and frequency, of the most
popular companies on WSB has any predictive value for the next day’s stock return, as
well for the same day. Our method of analyzing the internet posts includes sentiment and
occurrence analysis of an online stock forum, in similarity to Tumarkin and Whitelaw
(2001) and Yang, Zhu, Cheng (2020), but differs from them in both the forum which
is analyzed and the type of analysis used. We use neural networks and trees instead
of regressions and event studies. Since Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001) and Yang, Zhu,
Cheng (2020) get results which differ widely in however one can foresee stock behaviour
through analysis of internet postings, we seek to explore how our results compare to these
and the Efficient market-hypothesis.

John Maynard Keynes wrote about the concept of the Keynesian beauty contest in his
book The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money (1936). The concept,
which contrasts the Efficient market-theory, compares a beauty contest, where participants
should choose the most popular faces from a number of photographs, to the stock market.
The similarity is that a more advanced strategy would be to not choose the faces/stocks
that you believe are the most attractive, but the ones that you believe that the other
participants believe will be the most popular ones. A parallel can be drawn to our
exploration of how WSB affects the stock market where, if they turn out to have an effect
on the pricing of stocks, it could be seen as an indication of the notion of the Keynesian
beauty contest. If the concept holds true, participants on WSB would choose stocks that
they believe are popular among the public and, as an example, a large amount of positive
posts about a certain stock could then signal that the stock is popular, and hence convince
others to buy it.

3 Methodology

A library to replicate Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) is developed together with PhD students
and students pursuing a master’s degree at SSE. The library consists of two parts. Firstly,
a script which connects to the CRSP and Compustat databases and generates a dataset
similar to the one described in Gu, Kelly, and Xiu, but with daily data instead of monthly,
from 1980 to 2020. The features with the highest explanatory value for the model from
Gu, Kelly, and Xiu are defined and cleaned. Secondly, a neural network which is trained
on the data with the purpose of predicting stock movements. For each day, it chooses the
ten stocks which it predicts will perform the best and takes long positions in those.

To compile the reddit data, the API PRAW (The Python Reddit API Wrapper) is used
to extract all comments and posts published on wallstreetbets from the first of January
2012 to the last of December 2020. Since the server extracts all posts and comments
the moment they are posted, the data set reflects the subreddit from the historic day,
even if posts or comments are deleted retrospectively. However, the number of upvotes,
the hierarchy of submissions and comments, and occurrence on hot pages and r/all is not
captured with this methodology. Although it is true that the data does not fully represent
the state of the subreddit at the current time being, it is more consequent and captures
all published posts and activity.

After all comments and submissions are downloaded, a dictionary, seen in appendix 1
in section 8.1, which links names and tickers associated with certain companies to their
respective company, is used to parse through all comments and submissions and save
down all comments and submissions which contain the given ticker or name of the current
day. For each day, the frequency of comments and submissions where each word occurred
at least once separately in is calculated and each of those comments is saved down. A
sentiment for each stock is determined for each day by using DistilBERT’s sentiment
analysis transformer Hugging Face to determine the sentiment in each comment and
submission where the name or ticker of a company occurs. Hugging Face takes a string
of text as input and returns a value between -1 and 1, where -1 is extremely negative
and 1 extremely positive. A mean for each day is calculated for each stock based on the
comments and submissions where the linked names occur.

A normalized frequency measure and an average sentiment for each company and date is
calculated for further analysis and modelling. The frequency measure, defined in equation
1, is the number of occurrences of words corresponding to a given company, xi over the
number of occurrences of words corresponding to any company for that given day, x. We
sort all companies by the sum of frequency measures to find the most relatively mentioned
companies and look more into the top 100. Companies which are not mentioned especially
often become hard to analyze and predict the stock performance for since there are small
amounts of data and large time gaps between it being mentioned. Among the top 100
mentioned companies, we removed the companies which were at high risk of not being
intended in the sentence, where the company had a ticker which is equal to a common
word. For example, the company "World Health Energy Holdings, Inc." has the ticker
"WHEN", which is also a commonly used English and not only a ticker. However, we
choose not to remove the companies Facebook and Twitter since, even though people may
mention them and not intend to comment on the stock of these companies, they are still
referring to them as companies unlike words that are just common, like "when" and "we".

Thereafter, to determine the explanatory value of wallstreetbets for stock returns, both
a boosted decision tree and a neural network are used to predict the stock return of
the stocks. Firstly, the models are created solely based on the adjusted closing returns
of the S&P 500 index adjusted closing returns. Secondly, they are created based on
both the adjusted closing returns of the S&P 500 and the sentiment and frequency data
corresponding to the specific stock and date. Additionally, the S&P 500 data set is shifted
one date so that the models try to predict the stock returns the following day instead of
the same day, and identical boosted decision trees and neural networks are trained on this
new data set. The data sets are split into a training data set and a test data set, where
the training data set consists of 90% of the data, randomly chosen, and the test data set
consists of the remaining 10%. By creating the model based on the training data and
assessing it based on the test data, we can find overfitted and unsuitable models since the
model does not have seen the data in the test set previously. By comparing the accuracy
of the models, it is possible to determine and quantify the explanatory value of the reddit
data to determine stock returns.

When implementing the boosted decision tree, the XGBoost library for Python is used,
and more explicitly the XGB class. Squared error is used as the objective function and a
depth of 50 layers is used when implementing the boosted decision tree, which has proven
to be an efficient trade-off between accuracy and stability. For the evaluation method,
the root mean square log error (RMSLE) is used, which is the default option and similar
to RMSE in terms of characteristics. The model is trained on the training data before
evaluating it on the test data.

The neural network is implemented in Python using the TensorFlow library. The rectified
linear unit (relu) activation is used and we are using normal distributed tensors as kernel
initializers. Furthermore, adaptive moment estimation (adam) is used as an optimizer
and mean square error (MSE) and mean absolute error (MAE) as loss evaluation metric.
We use a batch size of 100 and 150 epochs for the training on the training data set, and
then evaluate it on the test data set.

4 Data Description

For the replication of Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020), we collect and use stock data from
the database of Center for Research in Security Prices (CRSP), which is accessed through
Wharton Research Data Services (WRDS). We gather daily stock market data from CRSP
and merge it with quarterly and annual data from WRDS, where each data point from
CRSP is merged with the most recently published quarterly and annual data, to the
highest extent possible in order to replicate the scenario for each day with the data
available at that point in time. Examples of the data with the imported and calculated
features can be found in appendix 8.2 , as well as the definitions of the features.

For our wallstreetbets extension, we build and use a reddit scraper script in Python to
scrape all comments and submissions from the subreddit wallstreetbets from its creation
date in January 31 2012 to December 31 2020. The number of posts amounted to 2982474.
After collecting the data in the form of comments and submissions with a Python script,
we use a dictionary which links names and tickers corresponding to a specific company,
for example, the words "Tesla" and "TSLA" are linked to Tesla, Inc. The regex which the
parser is based on looks for independent words in the comment/submission. For example,
for the company "Advanced Micronic Devices" with the ticker "AMD", the parser does
not find posts with words such as "ramdrive" or "amdy", even though these words contain
"amd", since the word is not independent. The number of companies amounted to 7449.
A Python script is used to analyze the frequency of each company, and we then use the
22 most commonly mentioned companies on WSB for our further analysis.

Analyzing the data from wallstreetbets delimits the analysis to what the users of the
forum decide to post online. The population of wallstreetbets may not be representative
of the aggregate stock buyers and sellers. They amount to 10.1 million users as of May
10th 2021 (wallstreetbets.com, 2021), and are described as “mostly young, mostly male
amateur investors” on the website Forbes. (Forbes.com, 2021) In other words, they may
not be representative for the population of all stock traders in the world. As a result,
their sentiment and analysis of stocks may not coincide with that of the average stock
trader, especially for “meme stocks” which are popular on the subreddit. “Meme stocks”
are described as stocks that gain popularity and volume from an online hype, from for
example reddit, instead of popularity which is based solely on the financial performance
of a company. (TheBalance.com, 2021)

6.1 Results
6.1.1 Empirical Asset Pricing Replication
As described in section 5.1, the predictive power of the replicated neural network on
monthly data is high and the trading strategy by consistently taking long positions in
the top ten stocks yielded a higher return than equal weighting between all stocks in
the test. However, when trying the same with daily data, the prediction value is close
to zero. This can mean two things; firstly, it might be that the paper and its findings
are not replicable with daily data instead of monthly data. This might for example be
due to larger spreads and variance in daily data, and small differences on a daily basis
in many features which decrease the prediction power. Secondly, the neural network we
implement is not appropriate or wrongly implemented. There are no recognized rules or
models on how a neural network should be designed and constructed, which makes us
question whether the paper is not replicable or if it is our neural network which is at
fault. Nevertheless, since the prediction value is close to zero on the daily data, there is
no reason for us to extend the data set used with the wallstreetbets features for the daily
data. As for the monthly data, the timespan of wallstreetbets and the amount of activity
of the forum only stretches a few years back, thus making it difficult to implement and
extend a data set which goes back to 1980. This is primarily due to that the amount
of data points would be small, especially considering wallstreetbets gaining a substantial
size of data only in recent years.

6.2 Methodology
Initially, we planned to add our analysis of wallstreetbets as a feature on the machine
learning toolbox which replicates Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020). However, the replication of
Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020) performs significantly worse while using daily data, than the
monthly data. Since the paper cannot be replicated with the daily data, there is no value
in adding an extension to an already faulty analysis. It would not generate any clear
results as to the predictive value of wallstreetbets when the replication analysis is flawed
from the start. Therefore, we decide to perform our analysis of wallstreetbets separately,
in order to be able to eliminate as many possible erroneous variables as possible, and to
distinctively decide the predictive power of wallstreetbets.

Our boosted decision trees prove more successful than the neural networks in our analysis
of predicting asset pricing through analysis of WSB posts. This is surprising, since Gu,
Kelly, and Xiu (2020) find that neural networks performed the best compared to other
methods of empirical asset pricing, including boosted decision trees. There might be sev-
eral reasons for this. Firstly, while the neural network is a more complex and sophisticated
technique than the boosted decision tree, neural networks are to a larger extent subject to
overfitting, which means that the model finds patterns or relation in the training data set

                                                                         27/39
6 Discussions L. Hedberg & O. Ribberheim

which do not exist in the test data set or the complete data set as a whole. This results
in worse results when the neural network finds these patterns in the explanatory variables
but these do not correspond to the same change in the response variable, which causes
the larger error. Secondly, whereas the neural network is more sensitive to overfitting, the
boosted decision tree is regarded as "robust" and can efficiently handle extreme outliers
and influential data points, without the loss in accuracy. Our reddit data is probably of
lower quality and subject to higher variance than the one used by Gu, Kelly, and Xiu
(2020), explaining why the neural network performs worse. And lastly, a boosted decision
tree is much easier to both calibrate and run, whereas a neural network is more like a
"black box", where there are no general ways of efficiently choosing the amount of layers,
nodes, and algorithms to yield the best result.

                                                                                                    2
When determining the accuracy of the models, we are choosing to use RMSE, R , and
              2
adjusted R . This is due to several reasons, but mainly two. Firstly, these measures
capture the accuracy of the model performance on the test data and not the complete
data set. Secondly, they measure the qualities of the models which are interesting for us,
accuracy/error, and explanatory value. Two examples of alternative ways of determining
the explanatory value are correlation and mean absolute error, MAE. Correlation is a
measure which tells us to what extent the linear least-square estimator of an explanatory
and response variable explains the relationship between them. Thus, correlation is a great
metric if there is, or is expected to be, a linear relationship between the variables. Other-
wise, the correlation might be significant but it tells little about the actual distribution of
the response variable and how well we are able to predict the actual outcome of a draw-
ing from the distribution. However, it may be of interest to see the correlation between
estimated values and actual values, but this should not be a primary measure to use. To
summarize, correlation is a measurement not suitable to quantify the explanatory value of
a model. MAE, in contrast to RMSE, quantifies the average of the errors but gives small
and large errors equal weighting, whereas RMSE gives higher weighting to larger errors
and smaller weightings to smaller errors. In this case, it is beneficial to penalize large
errors since these would correspond to the days with the largest swings and possibility
for profits and losses. Therefore, while MAE would fill the same function as RMSE and
                                                                                           2
measure the mean error, RMSE is a more appropriate measure to use. Adjusted R is the
                      2
same as ordinary R  except it penalizes more features. When introducing new features
to a model, it generally provides some level of explanatory value to the model at the cost
                                  2
of overfitting. The adjusted R takes this into account which only increases when a new
feature which introduces a substantial increase in explanatory value is introduced.

which is the center of the post. A company name might get mentioned in a post whose
meaning is not to describe the company, that is, the sentiment of the post is not intended

for that company. For example, both the words "Facebook" and "Twitter" have been

the matching of posts to the correct company and sentiment, for example by finding
the subject of the sentence and matching it to different companies, or by some even
more sophisticated sentence analysis tool. However, even though this would have been

Our results are consistent with Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001), since we find no explana-
tory value in using our stock forum data in order to predict stock returns later on, similar
to their findings. By extension, our results do not coincide with Yang, Zhu, Cheng (2020),
since they, unlike us, do find explanatory value in online stock board activity and trading
volumes. We find that the posts which we analyze have some explanatory value due to
                  2
the increased R  in the same-day analysis when the reddit data is added. This can be
compared to the finding of Tumarkin and Whitelaw in that they find a correlation between
abnormal activity on their forum and abnormal returns on the same day. Tumarkin and
Whitelaw (2001) do not, however, investigate the causality of this. The authors discuss
that a possible reason for this correlation may be that the forum reacts to the abnormal
stock returns by abnormally high activity, and this scenario could be similar in our case.
That is, the members of WSB may react to a change in stock price or another factor
which is correlated with the price and decide to make posts about it, which then draw
attention to that particular stock. This could explain the explanatory value of the posts
and the same day stock returns. Further investigations into this to establish a causality
would be an interesting extension.

The results which we obtain are in line with the Efficient market-hypothesis by Fama et
al (1969), since they show no predictive value between the wallstreetbets posts analyzed
by sentiment and frequency and the next day stock returns. The large decrease of the
            2
adjusted R when adding the reddit data in the next day analysis, speak for that there is
no usefulness in analyzing the WSB data in order to predict next day stock returns. By

Since we find no influence of the WSB posts on the stock prices, as indicated by the lowered
            2
adjusted R  when including the reddit data on the next day stock returns, it contrasts
with the notion of the Keynesian beauty contest (Keynes, 1936). If the Keynesian beauty
contest holds true, we would have expected to see an effect of the postings on WSB on the
stock returns the next day, since we would expect people to flock to the stocks which they

believe that others regard as popular and base their opinions on stocks’ values on what
they think others believe. For example, a high, positive activity on the forum around a
certain stock might be interpreted as a sign of a positive market sentiment of that stock,
and then inspire others to buy it as well. We would expect this scenario to lead to some
predictive value of the WSB posts for the next day stock returns, and since we do not get
any predictive value, it speaks against the idea of the Keynesian beauty contest in this
context.

Weaknesses in our comparisons of our results with the Efficient market-hypothesis and
the Keynesian beauty contest are for example the limited amount of data that we have
used and the fact that the population of WSB is just a small fraction of all the world’s
stock buyers. Furthermore, people that are members of wallstreetbets also get influenced
by other sources of stock information, such as other social media, newspapers, television,
etc. This could lead to the posts of WSB actually having an effect on the population of
WSB, but is too small to be picked up in this investigation. This effect would be in line
with the Keynesian beauty contest, and in conflict with the Efficient market-hypothesis.
Since the stock prices are influenced by an enormous amount of other factors, there might
be too much noise to distinguish the effect of wallstreetbets’ joint efforts by the means of
this thesis.

One alternative to using the API could be to download all comments and submissions

undeniably of higher data quality than what can be accessed through the API since it
contains activity such as rating, comments, and upvotes/downvotes. The votes, the type of
post (submission or comment), and the number of comments are examples of factors which
likely affect how much attention a post gets, and by extension its potential of influencing
sentiment. However, this approach would not have been scientifically appropriate to use
since the thesis is about predicting stock movement and see whether wallstreetbets can
help with predicting stock movements. Then it is not of interest to use historic data
where 40 percent of data is missing, since there might be a structural relation between
the submissions and comments that are deleted and those which are not. The method
of accessing the data through the API is not optimal, but in this case preferable since

6.3 Future work
This thesis gives some insight of the relationship between wallstreetbets and the stock mar-
ket, however the wallstreetbets data only stretches to the first of January 2021. Gamestop
has experienced massive volatility during the first quarter of 2021 and it would be of in-
terest to look into what happened there, and a more comprehensive and in-depth analysis
of Gamestop in particular. It would be of interest to explore if it would have been possible
to predict the increase in volatility and stock movement of January and February, and
then use the same models and techniques to try to predict upcoming volatility. Hence,
one interesting area of future work would be to look more specifically into certain "meme-
stocks" and how the memes and activity have affected, or been affected, by the stock
performance. However, this would require a more sophisticated way of determining the
sentiment of the comments and submissions, as the Gamestop quickly transformed into
a decentralized movement against the hedge funds Melvin capital and Citadel, making it
difficult to determine and quantify the sentiment.

Additionally, the Hugging Face sentiment analysis transformer has been trained and de-
veloped on ordinary sentences and conversations. However, wallstreetbets is known for
a vulgar language with frequent swearing and derogatory words. This creates a need for
retraining of the transformer so it becomes specially developed to be able to determine
the sentiment of the wallstreetbets comments and submissions. For example, the sen-
tence "Shit is getting spring loaded to blast off to the fucking moon" is considered by
wallstreetbets to be a positive sentence, indicating trust and belief in the stock, while
most people would consider the sentence to be negative. DistilBERT labels this comment
with a sentiment index of -0.995, that is, significantly negative. The solution to this would
be to feed the model with labeled sentences which are characteristic to the dialogue on
wallstreetbets. However, this labeling would have to be done manually which would be
time consuming and realistically not done in a comprehensive way, which would lead to
large fluctuations and volatility in the day-to-day average sentiment. Therefore, to train
the sentiment transformer to more accurately determine the sentiment of wallstreetbets
would be an area of high interest for further studies to determine and see whether the
explanatory value would be even higher.

Lastly, it would be of interest to make a further segmentation of the time of comments and
submissions in relation to the market. Now, we have segmented it per business day, but
with further segmentation, it would be interesting to see how the sentiment and frequency
relates to pre-market, aftermarket and during market hours, as well as how the aggregated
sentiment of longer holidays relate to the market performance. This would require more

                                                                         31/39
6 Discussions L. Hedberg & O. Ribberheim

detailed stock and exchange data, as well as more data since the lack of comments and/or
submissions referring to each stock would be much smaller and cause gaps in the data.

7 Conclusion

We explore the predictive value of adding sentiment and frequency data from popular
companies on the internet stock forum wallstreetbets in addition to the S&P 500 index,
to predict the stock returns of these popular companies through neural networks and
boosted decision trees. We also replicate a the empirical asset pricing by Gu, Kelly, and
Xiu (2020) with the extension of using daily instead of monthly data.

The empirical evidence found through the boosted decision trees suggests that the posts
of wallstreetbets have some explanatory value for the same day stock returns. This could
be as a result of the posters reacting to the stock return by writing about it on the
forum. The posts on wallstreetbets do not have any predictive value for predicting the
stock movements of the next day. This is in line with the Efficient market-hypothesis. It
furthermore reinforces the result of Tumarkin and Whitelaw (2001), in that it points to
the fact that it is not possible to gain an advantage and beat the market by analyzing data
from internet stock forums. At the same time, it contrasts with the Keynesian beauty
contest (Keynes, 1936) as there is no found influence of the online posts that seem to
affect other buyers of the stock market.

Unlike Gu, Kelly, and Xiu (2020), our boosted decision tree performs better than our
neural networks. While there are many possible answers to why that is, the most likely is
that the neural network is sensitive to overfitting and influential outliers, which decreases
the predictive power when run on the test data. Additionally, Empirical Asset Pricing
via Machine Learning is successfully replicated on monthly data but not on daily. This
suggests that the paper either is not replicable with daily data due to noise such as larger
variance in daily data which worsen the prediction, or that the neural network used is
either unfitting to this analysis or implemented wrongly.
