Bonds in Sweden Compared to China

Abstract:

   This  thesis  investigates  the  market  reactions  to  corporate  green  bond  announcements  in
   Sweden  and  China,  employing  an  event  study  methodology  within  the  framework  of  signaling
   theory  and  cost  of  capital  considerations.  The  findings  reveal  distinct  regional  reactions:  a
   positive  response  in  Sweden  indicates  that  the  market  perceives  green  bond  issuances  as
   credible  signals  of  environmental  commitment.  Conversely,  in  China,  the  negative  response
   could  suggest  market  skepticism  about  the  financial  implications  of  such  environmental
   initiatives.   These  differing  reactions  underscore  the  significance  of  regional  economic
   contexts   and   cultural   perceptions   in   sustainable   finance.   The   study   contributes   to
   understanding   the   strategic   implications   of   green  finance,  highlighting  how  investor
   perceptions  can  significantly  impact  the  effectiveness  of  signaling  through  green  bonds.

4.1   Reasons Behind Issuing
4.1.1   Signaling
    In the realm of environmental sustainability, companies often hold more detailed knowledge
about their capabilities, including their dedication to environmental responsibility, than their
investors. This informational imbalance can create hurdles for investors in identifying companies
with desirable environmental characteristics (Akerlof, 1970; Williamson, 1987).   To address
this challenge, companies employ signaling strategies, aiming to convey information credibly to
investors. According to signaling theory, a signal is deemed credible if it is challenging and costly
for other firms lacking desirable characteristics to imitate (Riley, 1979; Spence, 1973).
   The issuance of corporate green bonds exemplifies the application of signaling theory in
environmental sustainability. Investors face difficulties in assessing a company’s environmental
commitment due to limited information (Lyon & Maxwell, 2011; Lyon & Montgomery, 2015).
This underscores the need for investors to distinguish between companies genuinely dedicated
to environmental responsibility and those merely offering verbal affirmation without substantive
action or genuine commitment. Through the issuance of green bonds, companies can effectively
signal their commitment to environmental sustainability (Flammer, 2021). Several factors con-
tribute to the credibility of this signal. Firstly, companies allocate significant funds to finance
environmentally friendly projects through green bond issuance, with the average size of corpo-
rate green bonds among public firms at approximately $0.26 billion (Flammer, 2021). Secondly,
independent third parties often certify green bonds, ensuring the proceeds are directed towards
the specified green projects outlined in the bond prospectus. Compliance with green bond stan-
dards, such as the Climate Bonds Standard of the Climate Bonds Initiative, demands substantial
managerial effort and resources, thereby increasing the cost to the issuer. Furthermore, failure
to comply with certification, termed "green default," incurs its own costs, potentially resulting
                                 18
in the revocation of certification   .
    In essence, the issuance of green bonds serves as a credible signal of a company’s commitment
to environmental sustainability.  This signaling role is often recognized in anecdotal accounts,
such as ICA groups 3,5 billion SEK green bond issue, which aimed to underscore the centrality
                                                                19
of sustainability to the company’s business model and future  .  The signaling argument gen-
erates several testable implications.  Firstly, prior research suggests that shareholders respond
positively to companies’ environmental initiatives, with event studies demonstrating positive
abnormal returns in response to eco-friendly behavior (Flammer, 2021; Tang & Zhang, 2020;
Pedersen & Thun, 2019). Consequently, one would anticipate a positive stock market reaction
to the issuance of green bonds, especially those that are certified and issued by first-time is-
suers.  Secondly, following the issuance of green bonds, issuers are expected to enhance their
environmental performance, as evidenced by indicators such as reduced CO2 emissions. While
green bonds may not directly cause these improvements, they signal a credible commitment
to environmental responsibility, which should eventually translate into enhanced environmental
performance (Flammer, 2021, 2013). Thirdly, the issuance of green bonds is likely to attract long-
term and environmentally conscious investors, thereby increasing ownership by such investors
(Tang & Zhang, 2020). As companies signal their commitment to environmental sustainability

4.1.2   Cost of Capital
    Adding on to the signaling argument, another reason for the benefit of issuing green bonds is
that it might result in a lower cost of capital for the issuer. Caroline Flammer (2021) considers in
her paper that investors are willing to accept lower yields “for the greater good of fighting climate
change”, and that it could potentially constitute a cheaper source of financing when compared
to the other options.  This consideration constitutes interesting research, that is looking over
if Swedish and Chinese investors are willing to finance a company at a lower yield if they are
                            20
committed to sustainability  . As the culture and baseline for sustainability differ between the
regions, our thought is that the results may also differ. However, the scope of this paper is not to
look directly at the pricing of the corporate green bonds but rather just its effect on share price
action. Thus, whether or not they achieve a lower cost of financing would predict a positive or
potentially negative stock price response - since the investors would be benefiting from it.

5   Main Empirical Analysis

5.1   Analysis of Stock Market Reaction to Corporate Green Bond Announce-
    ment
   In this section, we stay in line with the methodology of Flammer (2021) and employ an
event study methodology to examine the stock price response following the announcement of
corporate green bond issuance. Utilizing Eikon’s fixed income database, which also provides the
announcement date of the issuance of respective bonds, we focus on this crucial date, outlined
in Flammer (2021), as it conveys new information to the market. Unlike the issuance date, the
announcement date captures the dissemination of information to investors.
   To perform the event study, and to be consistent with previous research, we designate the
announcement date as day 0 (Flammer, 2021; Kreuger, 2015). Furthermore, keeping consistent,
we consider the baseline event window of [-5,10] days, while incorporating the five preceding
and ten subsequent trading days. The thought process behind this is that this window accounts
for the possibility of pre-announcement information leakage and allows for a staggered market
response (Flammer, 2018). Following the same logic as Kreuger (2015) and Flammer (2021), we
also explore the stock price movements in the intervals [-20, -11] and [-10, -6] before the event
window and [11,20] and [21,60] after.

6   Interpretation of Empirical Findings

6.1   Positive Stock Market Reaction to Corporate Green Bond Issuance
    We set out to study whether or not there was an effect on the demand, and therefore investor
behavior and sentiment, for corporations’ stocks after the announcement of green bond issuance.
Our results yielded mixed findings, but for Sweden, which, in our view, is the most developed
market in the sustainable finance field, we found a positive reaction when accounting for the first
issuance.  This reaction is consistent with the findings of previous research, such as Flammer
(2021), Tang & Zhang (2020), and Pedersen & Thun (2019). This suggests that investors in the
Swedish market perceive the issuance of green bonds as more than just a financial transaction;
rather, following the argument made in “Corporate green bonds” by Flammer (2021), that it is
a meaningful signal of a company’s commitment towards environmentally sustainable business
practices. This interpretation is supported by the stronger market response observed for green
bonds that are certified by independent third parties - such as CBI, which we studied - and
those issued by first-time issuers.
   Our findings underscore the importance of signaling mechanisms in financial markets, par-
ticularly in the context of sustainable finance. By issuing corporate green bonds, companies not

                                         15
only raise capital but at the same time also communicate to investors that they are dedicated
to environmental stewardship, thereby enhancing their reputational capital and investor confi-
      31
dence  . What these findings should entail is that Swedish companies are incentivized to adopt
more sustainable practices and align their long-term strategies with environmental objectives,
contributing to the broader transition towards a green economy.
    Focusing on Sweden specifically offers valuable insights into the dynamics of sustainable
finance in the region. Given the region’s strong commitment to environmental sustainability and
its leading role in promoting green initiatives, it is reasonable to expect that companies issuing
green bonds in these countries would experience similar positive market responses.   Further
analyzing the nuances of investor behavior and market reactions in this specific context would
provide valuable contributions to the literature, especially considering the region’s progressive
stance towards these issues.
    For China, our findings are the opposite. Our analysis resulted in a negative market response
after the announcement that the firm would issue green bonds.  Thus, the signaling rationale
for our Chinese observations can not be concluded as with the Swedish firms.  However, one
argument that is consistent with Tang & Zhang’s findings (2020), where they found no significant

6.2   Implications for Sustainable Finance and Corporate Behavior
    Our result, which shows the positive stock market reaction to the issuance of corporate green
bonds, suggests that investors value environmental sustainability as a key determinant of firm
value. This aligns with the growing recognition of environmental, social, and governance (ESG)
factors in investment decision-making. Thus underscoring the growing importance and demand
for integrating ESG considerations into corporate strategies. From a corporate perspective, such
findings highlight the strategic importance of demonstrating commitment toward these contem-
porary issues through actions such as issuing corporate green bonds. Companies that prioritize
sustainability not only attract capital from environmentally conscious investors but also enhance
their long-term competitiveness and resilience (Flammer, 2013). Looking at previous research
that observed improvements in environmental performance following green bond issuance fur-
ther reinforces the notion that sustainability initiatives can generate tangible business benefits
beyond regulatory compliance (Flammer, 2021).
   For our research on Swedish corporations, what is of interest is that the implication of
our result gives insight into the potential drivers of sustainable finance adoption and corporate
behavior. By examining how investors and companies in these countries respond to signals and
changes in preferences, our findings shed light on the effectiveness of these new green finance
instruments in promoting environmental objectives and driving corporate sustainable practices.

6.3   Limitations of Our Research and Future Research Directions
    Given our dataset’s relatively small sample size of corporate green bonds, future studies will
benefit from expanding the sample to include a broader range of issuers and industries.  The
sample size is the weakness in our research, and a larger dataset would provide a more compre-
hensive understanding of the market dynamics and enable a more robust statistical analysis to
validate the observed trends that we show. Secondly, future research could explore alternative
empirical settings and governance regimes in the corporate green bond market. Analyzing the
role of regulatory frameworks, institutional arrangements, and market dynamics that are specific
to the markets studied would offer valuable insights into the drivers of sustainable finance adop-
tion and the effectiveness of green finance instruments in achieving environmental objectives.
Lastly, addressing methodological challenges such as endogeneity and sample selection bias is
crucial for advancing the understanding of the green bond markets. A limitation of our research
is that we did not employ rigorous econometric techniques and quasi-experimental designs, and
future studies can enhance the credibility and robustness of these findings, thereby contributing
further to the academic literature on sustainable finance and corporate sustainability.
   These future research directions provide valuable guidance for expanding the scope and
depth of our study. By addressing the methodological challenges and exploring context-specific
factors that influence the markets and observed firms, further studies can generate meaningful
insights and contribute to advancing knowledge in the field of sustainable finance. Acknowledging
the limitation of a small sample size and mitigating potential biases through robust statistical
analysis will also strengthen the validity and reliability of the research findings.

7   Conclusion

   This thesis explores the response of corporate green bond announcements on the stock mar-
ket, focusing on divergent responses observed in Sweden and China. We contrast these regions
to shed light on how differing economic contexts and cultural perceptions shape market dynam-
ics in sustainable finance. As sustainability emerges as a global concern, financing instruments
like green bonds are increasingly utilized to drive towards greener economies. The study begins
by outlining observed trends in corporate green bond issuance, noting a steady rise in total
issuance and their prevalence in sectors where environmental considerations significantly influ-
ence business operations, such as utilities, banking, and construction. Following this overview,
we delve into our analysis by examining the effect of these issuances on respective stock prices.
This approach aims to provide nuanced insights into the strategic implications of green finance,
highlighting the interplay between investor perceptions and the effectiveness of signaling through
green bonds.
    The primary focus of this paper is to delve into the Swedish market, followed by an explo-
ration of the Chinese market segmented across its major stock exchanges.  Our rationale for
this comparative analysis stems from the desire to discern potential differences in how corpora-
tions navigate divergent social norms, preferences, and expectations within these markets. Our
findings reveal contrasting reactions: (i) the Swedish stock market responded positively to the
announcement of the first green bond, while (ii) the Chinese stock market exhibited a negative

                                         17
response to the first corporate green bond announcements.  Initially contradictory, these out-
comes prompt further examination. In Sweden, our results align with the signaling hypothesis,
suggesting that corporations utilize green bonds as authentic signals of their commitment to
environmental causes. However, our analysis of robustness, as indicated in figure 10, highlights
significant disparities in certification rates between Chinese and Swedish green bonds.   This
disparity may underpin the negative response observed in China, indicating a potential lack of
robust signaling in self-labeled bonds.  The disparity may also be explained by what we dis-
cussed in section 6.1, that investors may see the green projects that the Chinese firms pursue as
value-destroying and not value-adding. Conversely, in Sweden, our findings resonate with prior
research indicating a positive correlation between environmentally friendly actions and stock
market performance (Flammer, 2021; Klasen & McLaughlin, 1996; Krüger, 2015; Pedersen &
Thun, 2019), reinforcing the favorable market response observed.  While our analysis did not
explicitly address concerns regarding green bonds being used to mask greenwashing practices
or analyzing the individual projects, the negative market response observed in China, coupled
with the predominance of non-CBI-aligned and self-labeled bonds in our sample, particularly
within the banking sector, suggests that this concern could be a factor influencing the observed
outcomes (Tang & Zhang, 2020).   This speculation gains traction considering the increasing
                                                              35
investor prioritization of environmentally friendly practices   .  Thus, it presents an intriguing
avenue for future investigation. However, it is important to note that this aspect falls beyond
the scope of this study, and definitive conclusions cannot be drawn based solely on our findings,
highlighting the necessity for further research and inquiry.
   Furthermore, the governance framework surrounding the green bond market presents a com-
plex and vital area of inquiry.  The prevailing system leans towards private governance, often
facilitated through third-party certification providers. However, the efficacy of this model com-
pared to alternatives, such as those proposed by Flammer (2021), remains uncertain. Neverthe-
less, exploring these questions promises to be an engaging pursuit for future research endeavors,
given the novelty of corporate green bonds as a financial instrument and the limitations inher-
ent in our relatively limited dataset. As more data accumulate, subsequent research will offer
broader-scale evidence and a deeper understanding of the lasting impacts of corporate green

A.7   In-Depth Literature Review
A more detailed literature review of Flammer (2021) and Tang & Zhang (2020).

Flammer. Corporate green bonds. 2021
   In this section, we delve into the detailed methodology and models employed in the study
conducted by Flammer in her paper titled "Corporate Green Bonds". This study scrutinizes the
empirical characteristics and market reactions associated with the issuance of corporate green
bonds, offering insights into the dynamics of sustainable finance in Sweden and China.
    The study encompasses a dataset comprising 565 green bonds issued by public firms, yielding
225 unique firm-year observations.  To contextualize the characteristics of green bond issuers,
the study meticulously compares them with other public firms engaged in bond issuance. Lever-
aging Bloomberg’s comprehensive fixed income database, this comparison is tailored to extract
informative insights. Key metrics include issuer scale, profitability, firm value, capital structure,
environmental ratings, and ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) ratings.   Notably,
green bond issuers exhibit, on average, larger scale compared to their counterparts, while demon-
strating similar profitability, firm value, and capital structure. Moreover, they tend to possess
higher environmental and ESG ratings, underscoring a clear propensity towards environmentally
beneficial projects.
    To scrutinize the stock market’s response to corporate green bond issuance, the study employs
a robust event study methodology. Focusing on the announcement date rather than the issuance
date allows capturing the information dissemination phase accurately. A comprehensive event
window spanning from five days prior to ten days post-announcement is utilized to encompass
market reactions effectively.   Abnormal returns are computed using the market model, with
meticulous attention to robust estimation techniques that account for country-specific market
returns.   The event study uncovers significant positive cumulative abnormal returns (CARs)
of 0.49% during the event window [5, 10], indicative of a favorable stock market response to
green bond announcements. This positive market reaction is attributed to the signaling effect
of companies’ environmental commitment, a phenomenon well-documented in prior literature.
Investors interpret green bond announcements as signals of corporate sustainability initiatives,
leading to enhanced investor perceptions and favorable market sentiments.
    To ensure the robustness of the findings, the study conducts a battery of rigorous robustness
checks. These checks encompass alternative market models, precision-weighted CAR computa-
tions, and meticulous exclusions based on confounding events or industry-specific nuances. By
subjecting the results to stringent validation procedures, the study enhances the integrity and
reliability of the empirical insights, reinforcing the credibility of the observed market reactions
to corporate green bond issuance.

Tang & Zhang. Do shareholders benefit from green bonds. 2020
   The study utilizes an extensive international green bond dataset, combining data from the
Climate Bond Initiative (CBI) and Bloomberg.   These sources offer comprehensive coverage
and share similar criteria for selecting green bonds, including project eligibility and reporting
standards. The CBI database is preferred due to its broader coverage, encompassing historical
green bond issuances such as the pioneering climate awareness bond by the European Investment
Bank in 2007. However, Bloomberg provides additional bond characteristics and announcement
dates, enhancing the analytical depth. The dataset spans from June 2007 to December 2017 for
CBI and from March 2010 to December 2017 for Bloomberg. Green bonds, as defined by CBI,
fund projects with positive environmental impacts, with proceeds earmarked for green projects
while backed by the issuer’s balance sheet. The dataset includes various types of green bonds,
such as revenue bonds, project bonds, and securitized bonds. Despite limited data fields in the
CBI dataset, it comprises 1181 green bonds totaling USD 248 billion, with an average issuance
amount of USD 211 million. Bloomberg’s dataset supplements CBI’s data, providing additional

                                         29
bond types and characteristics. The merged dataset includes 1510 bonds issued globally during
the 2007–2017 period, with an average issuance amount of USD 285 million and an average
coupon of 3.26%. The study also incorporates stock prices, market indices, and financial data
from DataStream, Worldscope, and WRDS Compustat Global. Institutional ownership data is
sourced from FactSet Ownership, while green bond yield spread data and conventional bond
data are obtained from the SDC Platinum New Issues database.
   The green bond market experienced modest growth in its early years, primarily driven by
development banks and supranational organizations.  However, a significant surge occurred in
2014, attributed to increased participation from commercial banks and corporations. The mar-
ket continued to expand steadily, doubling in size by 2017, with projections indicating further
growth. Geographically, green bond issuers are widely distributed globally, attracting interest
from institutional investors.  The sample comprises various issuer types, including commercial
banks, corporations, development banks, and sovereign entities.   Supranational organizations
such as the IFC, World Bank, and EIB are prominent issuers.  Notably, China, the US, and
European countries lead in green bond issuance.
    The study focuses on public listed corporations, resulting in a final sample of 132 unique
issuers. Summary statistics reveal that green bonds have an average coupon of 3.3%, a maturity
of 7.6 years, and an average issuance amount of USD 286 million. Public issuers’ bonds exhibit
similar characteristics but with slightly larger issuance amounts. The study investigates stock
market reactions to green bond announcements using event study analysis. Event windows range
from 10 days before to 10 days after the announcement, with robustness checks using different
window lengths.  The analysis excludes asset-backed bonds and private placements to ensure
data integrity.
    Results indicate a significant positive stock market reaction to green bond announcements,
suggesting the presence of a "green label" effect.   This effect signifies investor recognition of
firms’ environmental commitments and their positive valuation impact.  Non-parametric tests
confirm the significance of abnormal returns during event windows.  The study also explores
factors influencing stock market reactions, including bond issuer types and industry sectors.
Corporations issuing green bonds for their own projects exhibit a significant positive market
reaction, while financial institutions’ bonds show no such effect.  This distinction underscores
the importance of firms’ direct involvement in green projects for market valuation.   Overall,
the findings suggest that green bond issuance positively influences stock market perceptions,
highlighting the growing importance of environmental considerations in investment decisions.
