                         ABSTRACT

Seasoned equity offerings have been found to affect share prices dissimilarly depending
on institutional differences. Much research has concerned itself with the choice of
disparate flotation methods which seemingly affects firm value, as well as market
reactions to SEO announcements. This event study of rights issues in Sweden during
the period 2007-2012 examines the prospectus release, a formal filing date on which all
issue terms are finalized and made public. By studying this date as opposed to the first
announcement of an SEO, on a market characterized by an incremental release of
information, this paper effectively examines another phenomenon altogether. Investors’
expectations are built up during the weeks leading up to the prospectus and are then
confirmed or corrected, causing a reaction. We find no unilateral movement on
publication, but significant movements occur before and after, upwards and then
downwards, suggesting an overreaction pattern.  We also  find that  firms with fully
guaranteed issues  strongly  outperform  other  firms  on prospectus publication and over
the following four-month-period.

1. INTRODUCTION

Firms who would like to raise additional capital can do so by taking on debt, issuing new
equity or selling hybrid securities. Much research has concerned itself with the choice of
disparate flotation methods as this seemingly affects firm value. Market reactions to the
issuance of new equity in publicly traded companies, known as  seasoned equity offerings
(SEOs), have been particularly pronounced and differ substantially across countries.
Empirical evidence from the US, Canada and Australia has shown negative returns to
SEO announcements, while Norway, Japan, Korea and Finland  experience  a positive
effect.   These   opposing   findings   have   been   attributed   to   numerous   theoretical
frameworks and much of the variation is thought to stem from institutional differences.
The Swedish SEO process is characterized by preliminary  releases  of issue terms leading
up to the publication of an  issue  prospectus  –  a mandatory marketing document
containing all the final terms of an SEO. This thesis will investigate market reactions
surrounding the publication of prospectuses by means of an event study on Swedish
data during the period 2007-2012. To our knowledge no study has been done on this
particular date, as previous research has focused on the first announcement  of an offer.
This date is made more interesting by the distinct price  movements  that have been
observed surrounding it. In addition to the overall reaction the effect of  guarantees  is
studied,  as this service  is thought  to mitigate the negative effects related to SEOs.

Since unsuccessful offerings can be very costly, many firms choose to enter into
guarantee  agreements  whereby  individuals  or  firms  commit  to  purchase  certain
percentages of the issue. By disclosing their private valuation of the company and
substantiating it by taking on risk, guarantors provide investors with a ‘second opinion’
which is thought to be a positive signal. Guarantees can be of two types, normally the
term refers to an  underwriter  agreeing to cover unsubscribed stock should any remain at
the end of the SEO subscription period,  for which  a fee is  paid  to compensate for the
risk. As a complement to underwriting,  major shareholders in Sweden often promise to
subscribe to their pro  rata share or more of the issue without compensation, henceforth
referred to as  shareholder commitments.

To test the impact of guarantees and study the overall market reaction to prospectuses,
data on 90 rights issues in Sweden over the period 2007-2012  is used  and abnormal

                                    3
returns are estimated using a market model approach. These  are averaged over firms
and aggregated over several days to create  cumulative abnormal returns  (CARs)  which  are
used  to  test  the  null  hypothesis  of  no  unilateral  market  response  during  three
consecutive event windows: (1) the two-day prospectus publication, (2) a five-day period
before, and (3) a five-day period after the prospectus. To test for the effect of
guarantees the data  set is split into two subsamples consisting of 52 and 38 observations
respectively: the  former  including all fully guaranteed issues (either through underwriting
or shareholder commitments, or a combination thereof) and the  latter  containing  all
remaining offerings. For completeness, a supplementary regression analysis is provided
that attempts to explain cross-firm variation.

We find average cumulative abnormal returns for the five days preceding and following
the prospectus date, significant at the  1% level; first an increase of 2.9% and then a
decrease of  -7.3%. The two-day prospectus publication window sees an average CAR of
0.6%, which is not statistically significant. For our subsample of fully guaranteed issues,
the average prospectus window CAR is 1.8% and statistically significant at the 5% level.
Comparing  the  means,  fully  guaranteed  issues  experience  higher  returns  on  the
prospectus publication by between 2.3% to 3.3% with 99% confidence.  Furthermore,
we observe fully guaranteed  offerings in our sample to strongly outperform the
remainder of issues over a 140-day period, generating a gap of more than 20% in
indexed returns.

The remainder of this thesis is structured  as  follows: In  Section  2, the institutional
setting in Sweden is  described in greater detail.  Section  3 summarizes previous literature,
separated into  theory and empirical research.  Section  4 describes the time series and
cross-sectional data  set  while  Section  5 presents the methodology and potential sources
of bias. All results are listed in  Section  6 and are discussed in  Section  7. Conclusions
from these results are  then  presented in  Section  8 at the end of  the  thesis.

2. INSTITUTIONAL SETTING IN SWEDEN

2.1 SEASONED EQUITY OFFERINGS
The Swedish market is characterized by a strong  prevalence of  rights issues  as  opposed  to

                                1
other forms of public SEOs. In a rights issue,  warrants  (also known as  subscription rights)
are  distributed  to current shareholders pro rata to their ownership. Depending on
offering terms,  the  subscription rights allow their  holder to purchase a given number of
newly issued shares. The rights themselves are publicly traded and may be sold to other
investors in order to compensate shareholders who don’t  wish to  subscribe to the issue
for  the  dilution  incurred.  Swedish corporate law  dictates  (Aktiebolagslagen  1975:1385)  that
a  rights issue must be formally admitted at the Annual General Meeting (AGM) or at an
Extraordinary General  Meeting (EGM). However, the  AGM/EGM  may  give  a  mandate
to  the Board of  Directors (BoD) to initiate rights issues at its discretion, as long as this
does not interfere with the company's Articles of Association.

Prospectuses are required to be formally  admitted  by the Swedish Financial Supervisory
Authority (SFSA, in Swedish  Finansinspektionen) before  publication. Anecdotal evidence
shows that prospectuses are rarely complete surprises as companies often choose to
publish preliminary offering terms such as discount, issue size and  certain  guarantee
details  prior to the prospectus  release  date.  There may also be an announcement
following an AGM/EGM decision to execute a rights issue.  In any  event, the amount of
preliminary   information   released   differs   significantly   on   a   case   by   case   basis.
Determining on what  date  different pieces of information  are  incorporated into share
price is therefore difficult and  likely to lead to problems in comparability across
observations  and studies.

The  main  event window  surrounding which we have studied abnormal returns, is  a
clearly defined date  on which the  prospectus  (including  all  final  issue  terms)  is
published. In this sense, the irrevocability of the prospectus may confirm investor
uncertainty and  constitute new information  even if all terms of the prospectus have
been disclosed beforehand.  Informational value in prospectuses may also stem from
‘soft’ information; firm prospects, risk factors, and market conditions are all mandatory

2.2 SUBSCRIPTION GUARANTEES
The service of underwriting has been  suggested  to mitigate the negative effects related
to SEOs.  The prevailing method for underwriting in the United States entails issuing
equity and selling it to a group of underwriters (usually the same entities that provide
financial advice for the issuance process) who then attempt to  sell  the shares to the
public.  The underwriters  themselves are then at risk of ending up with any remaining
stock, a practice dubbed  firm commitment underwriting. In Europe the far less expensive
method of  rights issues  is almost exclusively employed. This is coupled with  standby
underwriting,  whereby  management  along  with  the  financial  advisor  labor  to  find
investors  willing to guarantee to cover certain portions of the offering should they
remain unsubscribed.  As with the US method, investment banks often underwrite parts
of the rights issues for which they have provided financial advisory.  As a complement to
underwriting,  major  shareholders  in  Sweden  often  provide  a  similar  service  by
promising  to  subscribe  to  their  pro  rata  share  or  more  of  the  issue  without
compensation.  By demonstrating their continued confidence in and commitment to the
firm,   shareholder   commitment   provides   a   similar   signaling   effect   to   that   of
underwriting.

3. PREVIOUS LITERATURE

This  section  is divided into three parts: first, we present theoretical explanations for
stock market reactions to the announcement of  SEOs; second,  we  explain underwriting
theories;  and  third, we list a summary of empirical research for international markets
and comment on the implications of institutional differences.

3.1 THEORY ON SEO ANNOUNCEMENT RETURNS
The purpose of this study is  not to test specific announcement theories, but to make
empirical observations  on  Swedish market reaction to the prospectus.  Numerous and
diverse  theories have addressed  SEO announcements  and  while the  predictions they
make for  announcement day returns  vary and even oppose each other, the  overall
impression  is that SEOs are  negative  signals.  It should be noted that theory in large
focuses  on  the  indirect  effect  of  offerings  rather  than  the  intrinsic  value  of  the
investments which will be undertaken with the  issue proceeds. Characteristics of the
individual  investment opportunity  constitute the primary impact on firm value, and  is
likely to account for  most  of the variation in announcement returns, why theories are
only able to explain secondary  (and  lesser)  effects.  As we study prospectus dates and not
announcement dates,  the following theories are not entirely applicable to our  research
setting  since  preliminary pre-event information  releases  habitually  occur, but remain the
closest  substitutes  available.

3.1.1 CAPITAL STRUCTURE THEORY
According to Modigliani & Miller (1958), the value of a firm  in imperfect markets  may
be increased by a larger portion of debt in relation to equity, as tax shields arise from
debt financing. For  a  rights issue the opposite  effect would then follow,  as newly issued
equity  lowers the overall  gearing of the company. Anticipating this, investors will adjust
their valuation and  share  price  should drop  accordingly. However, for highly indebted
companies who are in a situation of financial distress, the debt burden may  be lowering
firm value  (Stiglitz, 1969). Therefore, the announcement of an SEO  could instead
increase firm value as financial distress and business erosion costs are  anticipated to
diminish  with more equity in the firm.  A graphical representation  of this  is shown in

Furthermore, Masulis (1983) argues that well-informed managers who possess valuable
insider  information  regarding future cash flows are  at all times  able to choose  the
optimal gearing  for the firm  by  estimating  positive and negative effects from additional
debt  or  equity. Under this line of reasoning, the announcement of an SEO indicates that
there has been a downward adjustment in cash flow expectations, which should also put
negative price pressure on the stock.

Despite being well motivated, variations of the capital structure theory do not seem to
hold  in practice. Eckbo & Masulis (1992) find no significant evidence that an effect
from gearing takes place in a cross-sectional regression study on 1,139 SEOs  in the  U.S.
during the period  1963-1988.

The price pressure theory  (illustrated in Graph 2), labeled  by Scholes (1972),  addresses
supply and demand effects of an equity issue. The  theory  posits  that  if  characteristics
and risk factors vary enough between securities to make each asset  unique,  cross-

negative returns on announcement.  Should  prices instead solely be determined by risk

3.1.3 ASYMMETRIC INFORMATION THEORY
Opportunism

Equity issues are troubled by an asymmetric information problem, as new investors are
wary of potential opportunism by insiders. Myers & Majluf (1984) create a theoretical
framework based on the assumption that management possesses information which
investors do not and show how such a relationship may lead to firms passing up
positive  net  present  value  projects.  By  issuing  equity  at  stock  price  levels  that
management  can  estimate  to  be  overvalued,  a  wealth  transfer  occurs  from  new
shareholders   to   current   shareholders   (which   often   include   senior   management).
Investors who take this into account will lower their firm valuation and this  theory
therefore predicts negative returns on announcement of an SEO. The asymmetric
information problem may lead to a market failure as shown by Akerlof (1970) results,
but  entering into an underwriting agreement  may help alleviate  this problem.

Agency problems

Another  problematic scenario  arises  in  firms where  management does not hold  shares
and  wish to issue equity at overvalued prices only  to  bring capital inside the firm; capital
which  may  ultimately end up on their pay checks,  paying  no heed  to value creation  for
shareholders. These so-called agency problems were described by Jensen & Meckling

                                    9
(1976)  who claim that  rational investors  will  be cautious of SEOs and lower their firm
valuation  accordingly.

Pecking order principle

The pecking order principle states that firms should use a “pecking order” when
assigning funds to new projects (Myers  1984).  Primarily, firms  should employ  internally
generated capital  and  secondly,  they  can  raise  external  capital, turning  to the safest
security first;  bank debt, then corporate bonds and then possibly hybrid securities such
as convertible bonds.  Equity is  only  issued  as  a  last resort.  If firms act in line with  this
principle,  little focus  is devoted to  maintaining  a  target debt-to-equity mix,  as  otherwise
implied  by capital structure theory.  Since asymmetric information problems  can  in part
explain firm reluctance to issue equity,  implementing the pecking order principle could
be rational.  It  is  still debated whether  the principle  is used  in practice (Frank & Goyal,
2003).

3.2 THEORY ON UNDERWRITING

3.2.1 SHAREHOLDER TAKE-UP
Bøhren  et al.  (1997)  suggest  a  framework  to explain  value implications of  underwriting
decisions,  the  shareholder take-up model,  where  total  flotation  costs  of a rights issue  are
dependent upon the expected subscription of current shareholders. They argue that low
quality firms with low  expected shareholder take-up will not attempt an uninsured offer,
while firms of high quality may. Although  guarantees are  costly, firms with low  expected
take-up will  still  be better off  by  employing  underwriting  because  this increases the
likelihood  for  their issue to succeed at a given offer  price  –  ultimately  lowering  total
flotation costs. This is illustrated in  Graph 3  where  k  denotes shareholder take-up,
uninsured rights are offerings with zero guarantees,  and standby rights are  fully
underwritten offerings.  The  theory predicts an effect from underwriting that depends on
firm quality;  the  announcement day return  can go  either way  depending on  whether
management  makes  the correct decision or not.

3.2.2 CERTIFICATION HYPOTHESIS
Booth & Smith (1986)  relate underwriting to asymmetric information  theory  and
attribute  a positive certification effect to  underwriting by noting the  external  source of
the   guarantee.   After   assuming   costs   to   become   an   informational   insider,   the
underwriting firm puts its reputational capital at stake by agreeing to support an issue. In
the long run, underwriters  would not be able to  repeatedly  put their seal on issues for
which the offer price is not fair with respect to firm quality,  as this would undermine
their reputation and deter future business. The financial commitment in combination
with the reputational risk remedies the asymmetric information problem and  alleviates
investors’  moral hazard concerns.  This hypothesis  suggests that underwriting has a
positive certification effect for the issuing firm’s value and that underwritten  issues will
experience superior returns to uninsured offerings.

Although all of the above flotation methods are not comparable, the survey suggests
that there are variations between countries. Cooney  et al.  (2003) list three potential
reasons for the international differences: (1) institutional differences in the underwriting
process; (2) cross-country differences in the types of firms that issue stock;  and (3)
international differences in general market sentiment. One important factor for  research
on this topic  is the period for which the underwriter is at risk. In the  U.S., where
announcement reaction is negative, the final offer price is usually set less than 24 hours
before the issue is sold to investors  (Eckbo &  Masulis, 1995). In Norway, where law
requires the offer price to be set at least  three  weeks prior to the beginning of the
subscription period, Bøhren  et al.  (1997) have shown a  positive  two-day announcement
CAR  of  0.5%  to be significant  at  the 5% level.

Evidence of  underwriting and guarantees  also differ between studies and countries.
Ginglinger  et al.  (2007) find significant negative announcement returns on the French
market  for  both  standby  underwritten  offerings  and  uninsured  offerings.  Their
regression analysis shows that standby underwriting translates into a 1%  increase in
announcement day returns,  significant  at the 10%  level.  Bøhren  et al.  (1997) study the
Oslo Stock Exchange and find  the opposite effect,  that  fully underwritten offerings

                                    13
perform approximately  1.7  percentage points  worse than uninsured offerings, also
significant  at the  10%  level.

Studies  on  the  Swedish rights issue market include a working paper by Molin (1996), a
master thesis Fritzell & Hansveden (2006) and a published paper by Cronqvist &
Nilsson (2005). All of these studies have examined press releases and news articles in
order to determine  the  first announcement date. As described in  Section  2.1, Swedish
SEO practice  involves  the  gradual release  of issue  information at firms’  discretion. For
the purpose of our study we have chosen the prospectus date  as our  main  event
window,  which is why results from these studies will not be directly comparable. Their
results are as follows: Molin (1996) examines 62 rights issues for the years 1980-1994
and find  no statistically significant  average abnormal returns  on  SEO  announcement.
Fritzell & Hansveden (2006) study 201 rights issues between 1986 and 2005 and find a
negative   and   statistically   significant   average   abnormal   return   of   -2.2%   on
announcement. Cronqvist & Nilsson (2005) study 144 rights issues between 1986-1997
and show significant average abnormal returns  on announcement of 2.0%.

In summary:  in accordance with the varying predictions of theories in  Section  3.1 and
3.2,  empirical  evidence  on average market  reactions to SEO announcements  is mixed
and may  largely  depend on  the  general quality of investment opportunities  during the years of
study and  institutional differences  between markets examined.

4. DATA

4.1 DATA SELECTION
The data set  contains panel data for stocks which are listed on Nasdaq OMX Stockholm
(OMXS) and have filed an SEO prospectus over the time period January 1, 2007
through December 31, 2012. Time-series data has been retrieved from Datastream and
includes closing  prices for each  firm  during a period of at least two years surrounding
the issue prospectus date. Prices adjusted for corporate actions are used for most
purposes, while unadjusted prices are used to calculate historical market capitalization.
In addition to stock prices, time series data on the OMXS All-Share index has been
obtained from Datastream  for  the period June 24, 2005 through March 28, 2013. This
index includes all shares listed on OMXS and is value-weighted according to market
capitalization.

The cross-sectional part of the data  set has been manually collected by the authors of

A substantial amount of the regressions suffer from nonnormal residuals,  which makes

5.3.2 OVERLAPPING EVENT WINDOWS
Although we implicitly assume that the separate rights offerings are independent, there
is reason to believe that this assumption is violated  because  the  event windows overlap
each other at times (i.e. several offerings occur clustered).  This  may  lead to  covariance
between the security abnormal returns which  adds a covariance term to variance
estimations.  24  of 90 offerings in the entire sample have prospectus filing dates within
one  day  of  another offering.  Through various tests, we find that clustering  has no
pivotal  impact  on  our inferences  (see Appendix A3).

5.3.3 ILLIQUIDITY
Nonsynchronous trading can introduce a bias into the market model  by affecting
securities’  variances  and covariances and arises whenever prices are recorded at irregular
time intervals.  Scholes & Williams (1977) present empirical evidence that  suggests  betas
to  be  underestimated  as  a  result,  by  10  to  20%;  they  resolve  this  problem  by
constructing a  consistent  non-trading-adjusted beta.  However,  Jain (1986) considers the
influence  of illiquidity  on abnormal returns to be minimal and that adjustments are
unimportant  in general.

                                    27
While all  stocks listed on OMX Stockholm are subject to liquidity requirements, the
Small Cap  Section  is in general characterized by lower liquidity. Stocks in our sample
display different degrees of liquidity, but no adjustments have been made  in this study.
The possible underestimation of market model betas would  cause an overestimation of
abnormal  returns  and would misleadingly  increase  the  significance  level  of  the  results.

7. DISCUSSION

Research objectives

The first of our two research objectives aimed to  map market reactions to prospectuses  and
determine whether they carry informational value when many details have already been
released. Abnormal returns for individual firms do occur, however the event study
results  in  Section  6.1  finds  no  statistically  significant  average  effect.  Instead  an
‘overreaction’ pattern is observed in the surrounding event windows. The second
objective of  guarantee effects  is addressed by splitting the data set into subsamples in
Section 6.2. We are able to point to a positive signaling effect for guarantees and
observe a remarkable difference in share price development between fully guaranteed
and remaining issues.

Institutional differences

The many dissimilar results from around the world support the theory that institutional
differences are a key factor to explaining market reactions to SEOs. Factors unique to
Sweden include; highly discounted issues which entail warrants  with substantial nominal
value; offer prices which are set long before the end of the subscription period, putting
guarantors   at   substantially   higher   risk;   issues   which   are   often   very   large   w.r.t.
outstanding shares; and large blockholders who take active  roles in managing firms. For
the sake of discussion we will speculate how these factors might relate to the results we
see.

Prospectus date

On announcement of the prospectus, we find average abnormal returns of 0.6% which
are not sufficient to statistically establish a positive reaction (Section 6.1). Since key
information has, to varying extents, already been incorporated into share prices, a
limited reaction is not surprising. The prospectus acts as a confirmation of investor
expectations which, in line  with the insignificant results, seem to be neither over or
under optimistic on average, but fairly accurate. Had we found significant results, it
would have entailed that the prospectus itself was ceteris paribus a signal to investors,
which could be attributed to the fact that the execution of the issue is imminent.

                                    38
Any event study must consider the potential for information leakages which makes
determining the date when the new information is incorporated into share price
difficult. By studying the prospectus filing date as opposed to the first announcement of
an impending SEO, which many before us have done, on a market characterized by an
incremental   release   of   information,   this   paper   effectively   examines   another
phenomenon altogether. Investors’ expectations are built up during the weeks leading
up to the prospectus and are then officially confirmed or corrected, causing a reaction.
However, the fact remains that the amount of information released beforehand is highly
discretionary. This means that for  every offering, investors will be reacting to disparate
amounts of new information when the prospectus is released, which may very well be
the reason for the nonunilateral announcement window movements we observe. It may
also explain the fact that regression analysis has proved inconclusive and the results
puzzling. Additionally, the theories presented in Section 3 are for the same reason not
directly applicable to our analysis, but owing to the lack of studies conducted on the
prospectus filing date they  remain the most relevant substitutes. This knowledge void
entails that we cannot say whether the peculiar movements we see are an isolated
observation.

Theory and empirical research

The relationships suggested by our regression analysis do in fact not echo the theoretical
background. A specific intended offensive use for the proceeds (such as an M&A or
new market entrance) does not relate to announcement performance, statistically
speaking, despite that such plans are regarded as positive information to shareholders.
Neither is financial commitment from the lead financial advisor statistically positive for
the announcement returns. A positive effect is expected according to the certification
hypothesis, which states that if the financial advisor puts its reputation at stake, the issue
should  ceteris  paribus  be  received  more  positively.  While  the    effects  of  this
endorsement may be too small to discern, it is more likely that such information in
general reaches the market before the prospectus date and is already incorporated in
share prices. This is in line with the Swedish practice of a gradual information leakage
before the prospectus which finalizes the terms.

A more tailored model explaining the two-day announcement returns, finds that large
offerings and highly discounted offerings are significantly more positively received by

                                    39
the market. The finding is contrary to price pressure theory and there is no clear cut
explanation for the positive sign. In a wider perspective, it might be the case that firms
who undertake large offerings and highly discounted offerings have been in trouble for
some time which has eroded the share price. When these firms finally publish a
prospectus it may be received as a positive sign by the market that the issue is imminent;
as investors may have been expecting a failed issue prior to the publication. In an
attempt to control for firms that are excessively indebted, we have constructed a capital
structure variable that acts as a proxy for debt problems by multiplying market betas
(which proxy for cash flow volatility) with the net debt-to-equity ratio. Our line of
reasoning is that firms with high volatility in cash flows have low debt tolerance and vice
versa. If the cash flow volatility and the indebtedness are both high, we may expect that
the firm has financial distress issues which will be alleviated by an equity increase,
thereby increasing firm value. If on the other hand, the cash flow volatility and the
indebtedness are both low, we may expect that the firm could benefit  from additional
leverage rather than an equity issue, in which case the announcement will negatively
impact firm value. In line with optimal capital structure theory, the positive loading on
the capital structure measure captures the benefits of increasing equity to excessively
indebted firms, while the negative effect is included in the constant of the model. We
strongly believe that research along these lines with more sophisticated measures for
debt tolerance can provide valuable insights to investors and firms alike.

Overreaction

Visual inspection of the indexed abnormal return development presented in Graph 6
suggests an overreaction pattern, for which we propose several explanations. Our event
study (Section 6.1) finds average abnormal returns of positive 2.9% for the five-day-
period  before  prospectus  and  negative  -7.3%  for  the  five-day-period  after.  The
downward movement then overcompensates for the initial increase, but within two
weeks the curve returns to its (what appears to be a) pre-event downward trend.   If it
could be shown that specific positive information reached the market on the two days
preceding announcement (for which daily abnormal returns were significantly different
from zero, see Table 7) a case could be made for such leakage instigating the increase.
Otherwise it would have to be assumed that investors were aware of the release date and
traded up the stock in its anticipation. A possible albeit hard to substantiate explanation
for this could include various short-term trading strategies.

                                    40
The subsequent share price plummet could possibly be a delayed reaction to the
information in the prospectus, correcting the optimistic beliefs. This delay would violate
the assumption of efficient markets, however, prospectuses are large documents  and it

                                                18
may require some time for prices to adjust.  Regardless of what causes the increase, the
reversal in share prices could simply be a response to the increase itself. Regression
results in Table 13 show strong and significant inverse relationships between near term
share price performance and returns on the following days of trading. It seems that
share   prices   are   negatively   autocorrelated,   a   key   implication   of   virtually   every
overreaction theory according to Lo & MacKinlay (1990), entailing that prices are
predictable from past prices alone and violating weak form market efficiency. The
indication is highly interesting and a larger study is warranted to study the effect.

Guarantees

While we found no statistically significant abnormal returns to prospectus filings for the
entire data set, our fully guaranteed subsample showed a significant 1.8% average
increase on prospectus announcement coupled with a negative  -1.0% average return for
the remaining subsample (Table 9). Fully guaranteed issues accordingly experience a 2.8
percentage point higher return on average which would lend support to the certification
hypothesis. However, when making such a claim we must consider the possibility of
reverse causality; the firms who receive full underwriting may very well have different
characteristics than those who do not, which could account for the difference in
performance. The extended view of subsample abnormal returns (Graph 8) in no way
constitutes definite evidence either way, but does show firms with fully  underwritten
issues consistently performing better than those without following the issue. This would
not follow from a one-time price adjustment, instead it points to the ability to use issue
proceeds well and achieve higher returns than they did during  the estimation window  –
the opposite is true for the remaining subsample.

In the extended view it appears that the curves start to diverge around 40 days after the
prospectus, which may or may not coincide with firms getting access to the issue
proceeds.  Prior to this point however the curves develop similarly, an observation that
can also be made in Graph 7. On the filing date the subsamples experience disparate

8. CONCLUSION

The choice between internal financing and raising external capital is  influential for  firm
value,  and  SEO announcements have been shown to elicit different reactions depending
on institutional setting.  The Swedish rights  issue process is characterized by  releases of
preliminary  issue terms, culminating in the filing of a prospectus  that conveys new
information and finalizes issue terms.  Previous studies have investigated abnormal
returns to SEO announcement, why  a knowledge gap exists for the reaction on the
prospectus date.  Furthermore, the use of underwriting has become an increasingly
important tool in ensuring the success of equity offerings and a trend of fully
underwritten offerings is noted internationally.  Our thesis aimed to give insights in both
these aspects of the Swedish SEO process.

We find that there is an overreaction pattern in share prices surrounding the publication
of prospectuses. Firms  on average  trade up during the five-day period immediately
preceding the release and strongly rebound during the five-day period after the release
window.  This reversion of recent performance is vexing and we find no clear cut
rationale   for   why   it   occurs.   No   unilateral   effect   is   attributable   to   prospectus
announcements in  general and we therefore conclude that the prospectus is not a signal
in its own right, but only conveys firm specific information.

We also  observe that fully guaranteed issues significantly outperform those which are
not, both on the prospectus announcement date and during an extended 140-day period
after the offering.  The difference is much larger than expected and the size of the price
gap between fully guaranteed and remaining offers is striking.  As high quality firms may
more easily find guarantees,  reverse causality is likely to account for some of this
difference in performance.  Still, the  stronger performance of fully guaranteed issues has
theoretical support and appears on the date of the prospectus, why we feel confident to
conclude that the guarantee itself is a positive signal to the market.
