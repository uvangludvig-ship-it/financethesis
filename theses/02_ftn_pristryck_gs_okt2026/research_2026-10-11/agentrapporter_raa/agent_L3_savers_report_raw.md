OVERALL TOP-3 (8 weeks, fund-level data)

1. Sialm, Starks & Zhang 2015 JF. Best fit for both tutor and course standard. The replication leg is real and standard: monthly fund flows on lagged performance rank (Sirri-Tufano piecewise), category and month fixed effects, fees and size as controls, on the 2018-2026 PPM panel; Pensionsmyndigheten also publishes monthly fund-trade reports that can cross-check the panel. The FTN extension mirrors their Table VIII exactly: PPM flows split into agency-driven (default moves on known dates) and saver-driven, then Table IX asks whether agency-driven money went to funds that subsequently did better. Add the sex split and the appeal round as a third flow type (information without default). Honest caveat: the agency/voluntary split depends on dating each default move from fund event letters, and the removal-month flow mixes both.

2. Keim & Mitchell 2018 JPEF, with Choukhmane & De Silva 2026 JF for top-journal cover. It is the only published study of a menu cut with default mapping, so it is the natural template for Q10 and Q14 together: opt-out share, fee, risk and allocation changes of mapped money. The 2019 purge is an independent replication sample (313 funds, AP7 or sibling default, 97% defaulted) and FTN is the extension (equivalent-winner default, 94%). Honest caveat: JPEF is not on the list, its tables are individual-level, and with 67 FTN funds the cross-sectional drivers of a 6% opt-out are underpowered; pool with 2019 or the thesis is descriptive.

3. Kronlund, Pool, Sialm & Stefanescu 2021 JFE. The appeal round is the cleanest natural experiment in the whole setting and this is the published template for "information shock, no default": fund-level flows respond to fee and short-term performance after disclosure, less so in large menus. Replication leg without FTN: fee and performance sensitivity of PPM flows and its interaction with category menu size. Extension: flows out of the 31 frozen losers after 24 Feb 2026 versus same-category winners and versus earlier rounds with a default. Honest caveat: no Swedish disclosure shock was verified, so the replication is of the specification rather than the event; 31 treated funds and a window that may close before December.

Runner-up: Pool, Sialm & Stefanescu 2016 JF if the student can pull post-exit NAVs from Morningstar; it gives an asset-pricing flavour (removal predicts alpha?) the tutor will like.
