# Anchor decision for the Fondtorgsnämnden thesis

Prepared 2 October 2026 from three sources: a fidelity audit of all 25 awarded SSE finance theses (how closely each tracked its anchor), a sweep of the 635 non-awarded theses for anything on PPM, fund platforms or fund flows, and a literature search for alternative anchors, including the examiner's and the tutor's own work.

## Verdict

Sialm, Starks and Zhang (2015, JF) is the right measuring instrument and the wrong anchor for the question. The best anchor for the thesis as Klug framed it is **Cookson, Jenkinson, Jones and Martinez (2021, RFS), "Best Buys and Own Brands: Investment Platforms' Recommendations of Mutual Funds"**, with SSZ kept as the second-closest paper and as the source of the PPM versus non-PPM flow split.

Five reasons, each backed below:

1. The winners' pattern is unambiguous: the anchor's question must be the thesis question. SSZ's question (who drives pension flows, sponsors or participants) is not yours. Cookson's (does a gatekeeper's list move money, and does the gatekeeper pick well) is.
2. Cookson's object is literally what FTN produces: a short curated list drawn from a wide universe, with additions and deletions as dated events, flows and subsequent net performance as outcomes, and non-listed funds in the same category as the comparison group.
3. Cookson replicates table by table on 25 years of PPM data, not only on the two post-2024 years. The platform has had entries and exits every month since 2001 and a mass deletion in 2019. SSZ's sponsor table (VIII) cannot be replicated at all in Sweden, because PPM reallocations are mechanical.
4. It is recent (2021) and in RFS, which closes the "recent top-journal paper" question the examiner's slides raise.
5. It delivers two of Klug's listed outcomes (investor behaviour, quality) in one design, and it speaks his language: additions to and deletions from a list, with announcement and effective dates, are index inclusions for funds. His PhD is three essays on index inclusions.

## 1. What the 25 winners actually did with their anchors

The audit classified every winner by fidelity to its anchor. The pattern since the course took its current form is strict.

| Finding | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The anchor's question is the thesis question | 15 of 25 outright, 3 partial, 5 method-only, 2 no anchor. A method-anchor for a different question has not won the Hiller prize since 2021, and never when the design was generic. |
| Table-level replication is now the norm | 9 of 25 are table-level replications, all from 2016 onward; 7 of the 12 winners since 2019. The Hiller prize went to a table-level replication in four of the last five years (2020 LETF, 2022 crypto, 2023 FOMC, 2024 French quota). |
| The anchor's numbers sit next to yours in prose | Nine winners quote the anchor's coefficient and compare. The 2024 quota thesis: Ahern and Dittmar "found that low board gender diversity firms had, on average, a 3.56% worse return"; theirs was 0.83% and insignificant. Two null results won precisely because they were read against the anchor's positive. |
| The extension lives inside the anchor's tables | In every table-level winner the headline extension is a new column, regressor or subsample in the anchor's own specification, with at most one bolted-on table. The theses whose extension was a separate design are the ones that had to disclaim causality. |
| Old anchors are fine if a new shock justifies them | Eight winners used anchors 12 to 38 years old. None defended the age; all pointed to something that happened after the anchor (a new reform, a new crisis, a new institution). |
| Short post-periods are handled by design, never by apology | 2013 FTT thesis: seven months of post-tax data, not even mentioned as a limitation; 2017 unbundling thesis: two fund-years, handled with cross-sectional estimators, bootstrapped p-values and the carry-forward assumption stated outright ("This could be debated"). 2023 FOMC: shortened the cycle window and said so. |
| Deviations from the anchor are listed in one paragraph | All nine replications do this; several turn a deviation into a contribution ("we determine if the results are replicable using daily stock price data"). |

The winning sentence form, in the audit's words, is "we follow X as closely as possible, then break it." The 2023 FOMC thesis: "To guarantee comparability, as well as legitimacy of our contribution, we follow as closely as possible the methodology and data sources of Cieslak et al. (2019)." The 2020 bank thesis: "replicating, with modifications, the study by English et al. (2018) ... and extending it with data from 2012 to 2019."

Apply that test to SSZ. The sentence you would have to write is "we borrow SSZ's flow decomposition to ask a question they did not ask." That is the method-anchor bridge, and the five winners who used one each spent a paragraph arguing why the transplant is valid and where it breaks. It can be done. It is simply the harder road, and the one the recent prizes have not rewarded.

Apply it to Cookson. "We follow Cookson et al. (2021) as closely as the data allow: a listing regression, a flow regression on list status, additions and deletions, and a calendar-time performance comparison of listed against matched non-listed funds. We then break it in two ways: we split flows into platform and non-platform money, and we exploit the 2024 switch from an open list to a procured one." That sentence writes itself.

## 2. What the 635 non-winners tell you

- **Nobody has done this.** "Fondtorgsnämnd" appears in zero of 635 full texts. No thesis treats the 2019 purge, the 2022 agency or any procurement as a treatment. Nothing on recommended lists, best-buy lists or menu curation either. The web search found no working paper or journal article on FTN. The only official outcome evaluation is Pensionsmyndigheten's April 2026 report (first-year returns of the procured European funds 0.3 and 1.5 pp above the previous offering, with the caveat that one year is too short). Riksrevisionen has an audit in progress with unknown timing; it will be a performance audit, not econometrics. Your novelty claim is safe, but check DiVA for spring 2026 bachelor theses before writing it.
- **Eight theses used PPM as object or data source** (2011 to 2023). The fund-level PPM panel was built once (5737, 2023: monthly AUM, fees, ESG from Pensionsmyndigheten, 2018 to 2023, DiD on Covid). Its failures are instructive: treatment was a fixed fund characteristic, so the DiD was a before/after split; survivorship by construction ("we remove all funds that are added or excluded"); no clustering; and they could not reconstruct the roughly 300 funds removed in 2019. Nobody has used PPM net trading per fund as an outcome.
- **Data-access precedents.** Pensionsmyndigheten has given bachelor students a 96,918-person random sample of saver records with daily switches (4092, 2018), the whole platform's fund-level monthly files (5737), and the historical fund archive with daily NAVs from 2000 and monthly fees from 2002 (1463, 2289). Named contacts in those theses: Bengt Norrby (2012, 2018), Yassin Elgadmioui (2012), Dan Frankkila (2023). Known pitfalls: monthly returns in the PA files are integer-rounded (take returns from Morningstar instead); 2000s AUM and fee fields are "unreliable, prone to inconsistent formatting"; the December placement spike (about SEK 35bn in one month) contaminates any aggregate Swedish flow series.
- **The SHoF Morningstar database carries fund-level monthly net flows and AUM for Swedish-domiciled funds** (used by 6591 in 2026), which is your fallback if the share-class subtraction proves noisy.
- **The current house standard** (2025 to 2026 fund theses): one named anchor, Morningstar Direct or LSEG, SHoF for Swedish factors, panel fixed effects with clustered standard errors, horizon plots and placebos. Sirri and Tufano is cited in 10 of 26 fund theses; Del Guercio and Tkac in two, never as the design.

## 4. Why Cookson, and where it is weak

**What maps.** Cookson et al. study three UK platforms (Hargreaves Lansdown type) that recommend about 7% of the funds they carry. Their replicable core is three specifications: (i) a logit of monthly addition to and deletion from the list on fund characteristics (age, size, volatility, fees, Morningstar stars and analyst ratings, 1- and 3-year performance percentiles), clustered by fund; (ii) flows on lagged list status plus addition and deletion dummies over the previous three years, with fund and platform fixed effects; (iii) equal-weighted calendar-time returns of recommended versus non-recommended funds in excess of Morningstar category benchmarks, and a matched comparison within category. Their numbers: a listing brings about 1% of AUM per year in extra net inflow; deletions cause outflows for three years; recommended funds beat non-recommended by 0.94% a year, 0.60% against matched funds, and the outperformance is entirely in non-affiliated recommendations, zero for the platform's own-brand funds.

For FTN: the list is the set of funds on the platform (2001 to 2023) and the set of winners per category (2024 onward); additions and deletions are observed monthly in the Pensionsmyndigheten files; the comparison group is funds sold in Sweden in the same Morningstar category that are not listed, and after 2024 specifically the losing bidders. The affiliation result gives you a clean prediction to test against: FTN is a non-conflicted gatekeeper, so if its process carries the same information as a platform analyst's, its picks should show the non-affiliated outperformance.

**The three regimes are the extension, and they are inside Cookson's design.** PPM has been three different lists: open registration (2001 to 2018, any UCITS fund could join), screened registration (2019 to 2023, after the 29-point tightening removed several hundred funds for size and track-record reasons), and procurement (2024 onward, quality 75%, cost 25%). Cookson's flow regression run with a regime interaction asks: does the listing effect on non-PPM money grow as the list gets more selective? The 2019 purge is a placebo deletion (removed for being small, not for being bad) against the 2024 to 2026 deletions (removed after losing a quality tender). If the market treats those two kinds of removal the same, there is no certification; if it treats them differently, there is.

**Where it is weak, and what to do.**

1. *Pre-2024 listing is registration, not recommendation.* The 2001 to 2018 flow effect measures visibility and distribution, not certification. Say so in the text and treat it as the baseline against which the 2024 effect is read.
2. *Cookson's flow outcome is behavioural; your platform flow is mechanical.* Inside PPM the money moves because the agency moves it. The behavioural outcome has to be non-PPM flow, which is where SSZ's decomposition comes in: non-PPM flow equals Morningstar total flow minus Pensionsmyndigheten net trading. This is the one place the thesis departs from Cookson, and it should be stated as the first deviation.
3. *Non-PPM flow is mostly foreign money for Luxembourg and Irish funds.* Restrict the outcome to share classes registered for sale in Sweden, or to Swedish-domiciled funds, with the all-share-class version as robustness. Confirm in week 1 that SHoF's Morningstar extract carries country of sale. If it does not, the headline flow test loses most of its power and the thesis should lean on performance, fees and supply.
4. *Power after 2024.* About 60 winners, roughly 100 removed funds, 11 events, 6 to 30 months of post-award data. Write the minimum detectable effect before looking at the outcome. Cookson's 1% of AUM per year is the benchmark; if your MDE is above 3% of AUM, say the design cannot see a Cookson-sized effect and let the pre-2024 panel carry the flow result.
5. *Selection is on quality.* Winners may be the 4- and 5-star funds. Lagged Morningstar stars go in every regression, as in Cookson. Balance table of winners against qualified losers before the award; pre-trend plot.

## 5. Why not the others

**SSZ as anchor.** Everything in my earlier reply stands: the measurement maps one to one and the data are better than theirs. But the audit shows what a method-anchor costs, and SSZ's most interesting table (sponsor versus participant) has no Swedish counterpart because the reallocation is mechanical. A thesis anchored on SSZ spends four tables on flow-performance regressions whose connection to FTN is one sentence long. Keep SSZ for what it is good at: Table 2 (PPM flows are inert relative to non-PPM flows, which is why the agency can move SEK 100bn with 85 to 95% of savers accepting the default reallocation) and the flow definitions. Cite Tran and Wang (2023, JFE), which redoes SSZ's decomposition on hand-collected 11-K data to 2016 and ships a public replication package, as the recent paper in that line.

**Jenkinson, Jones and Martinez / Goyal and Wahal.** The selection model (P(win | bid) on past performance, fee, size, stars, incumbency) is Table 3 of your thesis whichever anchor you pick. As the main anchor it fails on the performance leg: weeks to 30 months of post-award returns for 4 to 10 winners per category is description, not inference.

**Price pressure.** This is the framing the tutor and the examiner personally work in (Klug: Russell reconstitutions and demand elasticities; Sabbatucci, Tamoni and Xiao: 401(k) menu shifts repricing stocks by about 10%). The Swedish small-cap procurement (nine funds removed, SEK 34bn moved to ten winners, saver deadline 25 September 2026, transfers in October) is a pre-announced demand shock to illiquid stocks with award and transfer dates playing announcement and effective dates. It is a strong second paper or a single figure if time allows. It is not the thesis: no recent published anchor to replicate, winners and losers in the same category hold overlapping portfolios so net stock-level demand may be near zero outside micro-caps, and managers can pre-trade a known transfer date.

Rules taken from the winners:

- Put Cookson's numbers in the same paragraph as yours: their 1% of AUM per year, their 0.94% and 0.60% performance gaps, their three-year deletion outflow. A null read against those numbers is a finding; a null on its own is not.
- Predict the sign of every table before you see the data. Table 2 should show PPM flows less performance-sensitive than non-PPM flows, the reverse of SSZ's Table III, because pre-2024 PPM had no sponsor. Table 4 should show the listing effect rising across regimes if listing carries information. Table 7 should show that 2019 removals shrank and merged more than 2024 removals, because they were small to begin with.
- Short post-period, by design: cross-sectional variation (winners versus losers within category), the pre-2024 panel for the flow result that needs power, lagged outcomes where possible, and the carry-forward assumption stated in one sentence.
- One paragraph of deviations from Cookson, written early: non-PPM flow instead of platform flow as the behavioural outcome; registration instead of recommendation before 2024; no affiliation or revenue-sharing dimension; monthly data; a public rather than private gatekeeper. Each item is a robustness heading, not a weakness.
- Inference: cluster by fund; wild cluster bootstrap at the category or round level for the post-2024 tests; never iid t-tests on overlapping windows. The non-winner corpus is full of stars that do not match the standard errors.
- Related literature under a page: Cookson et al.; SSZ and Tran and Wang; Jenkinson, Jones and Martinez and Goyal, Wahal and Yavuz on gatekeepers picking managers; Del Guercio and Tkac and Ben-David et al. on labels and flows; Dahlquist, Martinez and Söderlind and Cronqvist, Thaler and Yu on PPM inertia; Berk and Green. Sabbatucci, Tamoni and Xiao as the one sentence on where the money goes next.
- Data from institutions, named in the introduction: Pensionsmyndigheten monthly files, FTN procurement reports, Morningstar via SHoF. Email the named PA contacts in week 1.
